# Social connection: experience, contact and support

## SOC-C1 — Contacts and feeling connected answer different questions

Loneliness concerns distress about wanted connection that is not being experienced. Social isolation concerns relatively sparse relationships, roles and interactions. A network also has functions, including help that feels available, and relationship qualities that can be supportive or strained. Frequent contact can therefore coexist with loneliness or unavailable practical support; a small circle alone does not establish distress. [S112]

If the adult wants to explore this, choose one relevant optional question: what feels missing; which interaction feels connecting; or what limits access to desired support. These are original editorial prompts, not a questionnaire or tested therapy. Allow refusal and preserve the person's preferences, culture and circumstances. Do not prescribe a friend count, infer an absent person's motives or diagnose from contact frequency.
