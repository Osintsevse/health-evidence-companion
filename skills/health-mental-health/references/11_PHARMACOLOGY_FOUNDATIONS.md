# Pharmacology and formulations

2026-10-06. Introductory adult notes from selected open chapters and regulatory materials; not a completed pharmacology course.

## Disciplines

Pharmacology explains a substance's action and movement in the body. Clinical pharmacology relates these to benefit, harm and monitoring. Pharmaceutical technology concerns dosage forms, ingredients and release. A mechanism alone does not establish benefit for a disease; treatment comparisons require clinical outcomes and applicable guidelines.

## Pharmacokinetics: ADME

| Process or parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Absorption/bioavailability | Fraction reaching systemic circulation and speed; formulation and route matter |
| Distribution | Movement into tissues; plasma concentration is not concentration in every organ |
| Metabolism | Transformation into active, inactive or toxic metabolites |
| Excretion | Removal of drug/metabolites; kidney and liver factors affect accumulation |
| Clearance | Equivalent volume of plasma cleared per unit time |
| Half-life | Time for the amount to halve under the applicable kinetics; not universal effect duration |

Dose and concentration differ. Age, genetics, kidney/liver function and dehydration may change exposure. Do not derive an individual's dose from a general formula alone. In first-order kinetics, half-life relates to distribution volume and clearance; that model does not automatically apply to every medicine. [S63, S65]

## Pharmacodynamics

Effect depends on concentration at the target, binding and subsequent tissue response. The same drug level can produce different responses across physiological states and combinations. Milligrams of different ingredients do not indicate comparative effectiveness; increasing dose may change both benefit and harm. [S64]

## Formulations and equivalence

Check INN, brand, strength, concentration, dosage form and route separately. The same ingredient does not guarantee identical release or mg/mL. [S65]

MR/SR/XL/LA and enteric-coated products need separate checks. Crushing may disrupt release. Splitting, dissolving or opening a capsule requires product-specific information; a score line alone does not permit every modification. [S77]

FDA-approved generics meet requirements for ingredient, strength, form, route and bioequivalence; excipients may differ. This describes US approval and does not verify an arbitrary product from another country. Check registration and substitution locally. [S78, S12-S16]

## Interactions

Enzyme inhibition can increase an active drug's concentration but decrease formation of an active metabolite. Codeine activation involves CYP2D6. Induction usually develops and resolves gradually; separating doses by a few hours is not a general solution. [S67]

## General ingredient card

INN -> class/mechanism -> evidence-supported indications -> forms -> ADME -> contraindications -> important adverse effects -> interactions -> monitoring requirements -> current local label/version/check date.

Actual use, dose and tolerability for an individual belong only in a separate authorized private record. Future topics include receptor models, dose-response, therapeutic windows, pharmacogenetics, drug-level monitoring and detailed class cards.
