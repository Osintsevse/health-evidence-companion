# Psychiatry and medication monitoring

Active source routes: S02-S03, S27, S792, S804. General knowledge only; historical NICE sources are bibliographic only.

## Scope

Explain symptoms, diagnostic concepts, medicines, physical causes and monitoring. Psychotherapy notes and psychiatric records may have separate private storage and access. Therapy participation alone does not establish a diagnosis; do not transfer full conversations automatically.

ICD-11 CDDR describes diagnostic requirements; mhGAP provides care guidance. For a disorder, read the relevant section and consider duration, functional impact, substances, physical conditions and alternatives. Screening questionnaires support assessment but do not independently diagnose. [S02-S03]

## Medicine groups

- SSRIs/SNRIs: antidepressants for particular indications; track benefit and tolerability over time.
- Other antidepressants: different mechanisms and restrictions; milligram doses are not interchangeable.
- Benzodiazepines/Z-drugs: special attention to sedation, dependence and withdrawal.
- Antipsychotics/mood stabilizers: distinct indications and monitoring; an SSRI summary does not apply to all groups.

Create detailed ingredient cards from a current local SmPC and condition-specific guideline when needed. This is educational navigation, not medicine selection.

## Monitoring principles

Record the exact product, changes, benefit and unwanted effects for the prescriber. The NHS page describes frequent early review, but a local service and the person's risk determine the actual plan. Seek immediate help if safety is compromised. No fixed appointment interval is prescribed here. [S792]

## Stopping treatment

Withdrawal symptoms can follow antidepressant reduction or stopping and can resemble recurrence. Agree changes with the prescriber; do not stop suddenly or infer a taper from a class name. The NHS describes a plan often lasting weeks or months, with substantial individual variation. No universal taper or tablet-splitting instruction follows. [S792]

## Interactions and driving

Verify serotonergic combinations, bleeding risk, sedation and product-specific warnings. Sertraline labelling warns about NSAIDs and other serotonergic agents. Explain clinical relevance rather than returning a bare alert list. Signs of a severe reaction require urgent assessment. [S27]

AI can explain clinical information and help formulate questions. Initiating, switching or stopping a psychotropic medicine requires clinical assessment; this library provides no universal prescription.
