# Anatomy, physiology and disease-navigation atlas

Public educational navigation; reviewed 2026-10-09. This atlas connects a question to the correct topic and identifies missing context. It is neither a diagnostic engine nor a treatment protocol. Anatomy does not establish clinical validation, and a list of organs is not an instruction to image or screen every organ. Physiology describes interacting processes; a plausible mechanism is insufficient evidence that a treatment works. Investigations and treatment choices require the relevant disease-specific guidance.

## Start with location, time and function

Use anatomical position and the person's own left and right. Anterior means toward the front, posterior toward the back, medial toward the midline, lateral away from it, proximal nearer a limb's origin and distal farther away. Superficial and deep describe relative depth. Sagittal sections divide left/right, coronal sections front/back and transverse sections upper/lower. Surface location does not identify the responsible organ. The chest contains lungs, heart, esophagus, major vessels, nerves, ribs and muscles; abdominal and pelvic structures overlap in three dimensions. Congenital variation, transplant or operation may change the usual map. S341

Collect onset, duration, progression, precise location, distribution, triggers, relieving factors, associated symptoms, impairment and relevant exposures. Ask what was actually measured, with units, date and method. Distinguish an observation from a clinician's diagnosis and from an explanatory hypothesis. A normal isolated result does not rule out every condition in a system. A positive screening test usually changes the next investigation rather than settling diagnosis. Sudden onset, severe functional loss or rapid deterioration changes urgency before anatomical explanation.

Pain can originate in the symptomatic tissue, be referred from another structure, follow a nerve distribution, or arise from a systemic process. Chest symptoms can involve cardiac, pulmonary, gastrointestinal or musculoskeletal pathways; dizziness can involve circulation, vestibular function, medicines, anemia or metabolism. Systemic illness may present locally, and local disease can have systemic consequences. Never use a tidy map to dismiss a mismatched symptom. New severe chest symptoms, breathing difficulty, stroke-like deficits, collapse, uncontrolled bleeding or rapidly progressive illness trigger emergency assessment through the appropriate triage module; explanation must not delay help.

## Organ atlas: location, relations and connections

This is a typical adult map, not an individual organ inventory. Named vessels are major pathways, not exhaustive surgical anatomy. Branches and positions vary. Neuroanatomy and endometriosis receive deeper coverage in separate modules.

| Structure/network | Typical location and relations | Major blood/fluid pathways | Functional connections |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brain and spinal cord | Brain within skull; cord within vertebral canal; peripheral nerves reach limbs and organs | Internal carotid/vertebral arterial systems supply brain; venous outflow reaches neck veins; cerebrospinal fluid is a distinct compartment | Sensory input, integration and motor output link behavior, movement and visceral regulation. Peripheral symptoms may arise centrally or locally; localization needs examination. |
| Autonomic/enteric networks | Central pathways, ganglia, peripheral nerves and gut-wall circuits | Circulation of host tissues | Sympathetic and parasympathetic signals regulate heart rate, vascular responses, glands and gut function. Autonomic symptoms do not independently diagnose an autonomic disorder. |
| Heart | Mediastinum between lungs, behind sternum, above diaphragm; pericardial enclosure | Coronary arteries feed myocardium; cardiac veins largely enter coronary sinus/right atrium | Right pump feeds lungs; left pump feeds systemic circulation. Rhythm, valves, myocardium and coronary supply are different targets. |
| Lungs/airways | Paired thoracic organs around mediastinum; pleura borders chest wall; diaphragm below | Pulmonary arterial blood reaches gas-exchange capillaries and returns through pulmonary veins | Ventilation, diffusion, perfusion and hemoglobin transport must work together. Breathlessness is not synonymous with airway narrowing. |
| Liver/gallbladder | Liver mainly beneath right diaphragm with central extension; gallbladder underneath | Hepatic arterial plus portal venous inflow; hepatic venous drainage; bile uses ducts | Portal inflow links intestine to hepatic processing. Liver produces bile/plasma proteins and manages metabolism; gallbladder stores bile. Obstruction differs from hepatocyte injury. |
| GI tract | Mouth/pharynx/esophagus lead to stomach, small bowel, colon/rectum; mesenteries support much bowel | Celiac/mesenteric arterial territories; much GI venous blood enters portal circulation; absorbed fats also use lymph | Motility, digestion, absorption, barrier function and elimination are distinct. Bleeding, malabsorption and obstruction need different routes. |
| Pancreas | Upper abdomen near stomach/duodenum | Upper abdominal arterial branches; portal-related venous drainage; exocrine ducts | Exocrine enzymes aid digestion; endocrine islets release insulin/glucagon into blood. Diabetes and exocrine disease overlap but are not synonymous. |
| Kidneys/urinary tract | Retroperitoneal kidneys against posterior abdominal wall; ureters lead to pelvic bladder/urethra | Renal arteries enter and veins leave hilum; filtration/tubular processing produces urine | Perfusion, filtration, reabsorption and outflow are distinct. Urine appearance or volume cannot independently quantify filtration. |
| Endocrine glands | Pituitary near brain base; thyroid anterior neck; parathyroids nearby; adrenals above kidneys; gonads in reproductive anatomy | Hormones enter blood and act where receptors/downstream machinery permit | Feedback connects hypothalamus/pituitary with peripheral glands. Hormone results need timing, binding, medicines, life stage and feedback context. |
| Blood/marrow/lymph/spleen | Marrow within bones; channels/nodes throughout body; spleen upper left abdomen | Blood vessels circulate blood; lymph returns interstitial fluid to veins and transports absorbed fats | Oxygen transport, immune defense and clotting use different components. Nodes filter lymph; spleen participates in immune/blood-cell functions. |
| Skin/connective tissue | Epidermal barrier over dermis/deeper tissue; sensory endings and matrix interact | Deeper vessels support tissue/temperature regulation; lymph drains interstitial fluid | Barrier, immunity and sensation connect skin to infection, allergy, neuropathy and systemic disease. Color change may be harder to recognize in some skin tones. |
| Bone/joint/muscle | Axial skeleton protects central organs; appendicular skeleton supports limbs; tendons link muscle/bone | Living bone and muscle require arterial supply/venous drainage | Remodeling, calcium storage and marrow connect endocrine/blood systems; contraction connects nerves, energy and mechanical movement. |
| Reproductive/breast structures | Uterus/ovaries/tubes when present in pelvis; testes usually scrotal; prostate near bladder outlet when present; breast tissue anterior chest | Gonadal/pelvic branches; venous/lymph drainage varies; ducts carry gametes/milk | Gamete production, hormones, pregnancy and lactation are separable. Identity does not specify structures present or processes possible. |

## Follow physiology across systems

Systemic veins → right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary circulation → left atrium → left ventricle → systemic arteries → tissue capillaries → veins. Coronary supply serves heart muscle itself. Artery/vein describe direction relative to the heart, not a universal oxygen-content rule. A portal connection routes nutrient-rich GI blood through liver before systemic venous return. Lymph returns interstitial fluid to circulation. These maps explain connections; they do not establish why one person's edema or pain occurred. S344 S345 S349

Air movement → gas exchange → blood transport → tissue use is another chain. Failure at any point can produce distress. Saturation differs from oxygen delivery, which also depends on hemoglobin and circulation. Carbon dioxide removal contributes to acid-base regulation. Ventilation, perfusion and subjective breathlessness require separate consideration. A reassuring consumer reading must not override serious symptoms. S346 S359

Kidneys connect fluid balance, electrolytes, acid-base regulation and metabolic waste elimination. Both fluid excess and depletion can harm; correct action depends on cause and cardiac, renal and endocrine context. Creatinine-based eGFR estimates filtration, not every kidney function. Acute changes, muscle mass and equation affect interpretation. Chronic classification needs persistence or other evidence plus clinical context, not one unexplained result. Do not recommend fluid restriction, potassium supplementation, diuretic changes or renal medicine doses from this atlas. S348 S354

Gut-liver-pancreas connections link nutrients, bile, enzymes and glucose regulation. Digestive complaints may concern mucosa, motility, obstruction, absorption or systemic metabolism. Liver injury can alter protein production and drug handling; pancreatic disease may affect digestion, endocrine function or both. A liver panel includes markers with different meanings: raised enzymes are not identical to impaired synthetic function. An imaging finding is not automatically the cause of pain. Retrieve the disease module for a specific pattern and investigation question. S347

Nervous and endocrine systems regulate homeostasis through overlapping signaling timescales. Receptor distribution, feedback and sensitivity matter alongside signal concentration. Stress-related autonomic responses are real, but cannot explain all unexplained symptoms by default. A thyroid/reproductive hormone hypothesis should not become an indiscriminate hormone panel. Identify the clinical question, physiological state and interpretive consequence before discussing testing. S342 S343

Blood and immune functions connect distant organs. Reduced oxygen-carrying capacity may impair exercise without a lung lesion; inflammation may alter appetite/energy; a clot may form in one site and obstruct another. Skin is an immune barrier; musculoskeletal tissue is metabolically active. “My organ scan was normal” and “I still have symptoms” are compatible. Further assessment should address the unresolved question through history/examination rather than collecting scans of every system. S349 S351 S353

## Disease mechanism vocabulary

Mechanisms are useful search tags and often coexist. They cannot be selected diagnostically from one symptom.

| Mechanism | Meaning | Overlap/navigation caution |
|---|---|---|
| Vascular | Reduced supply, bleeding, abnormal pressure, thrombosis/embolism | Ischemia may cause secondary inflammation; urgency depends on organ/presentation. |
| Infectious | Pathogen injury and host response | Distinguish colonization, infection and aftermath; antibiotics are not a universal fever response. |
| Inflammatory | Tissue response involving immune signaling | Infection, injury, autoimmunity and other causes overlap; a marker does not identify cause. |
| Autoimmune | Immune activity against self-associated targets | Organ-specific/systemic forms; symptoms alone do not justify antibody panels or immunosuppression. |
| Metabolic/endocrine | Altered production, processing, storage/regulation | Nutrients, feedback, organ function and medicines interact. |
| Genetic/congenital | Variants or developmental differences | Classification, penetrance and phenotype matter; risk allele is not diagnosis; congenital is not necessarily inherited. |
| Neoplastic | Abnormal proliferation, benign or malignant potential | Mass characterization is needed; size alone does not establish type/stage. |
| Degenerative | Progressive structural/functional loss | Age, exposure and biology interact; common images may not explain symptoms. |
| Toxic/iatrogenic | Exposure, medicine or procedure injury | Exact substance/route/timing matters; do not stop essential treatment from suspicion alone. |
| Traumatic/mechanical | Physical injury, compression, obstruction/altered mechanics | Pain and tissue damage may differ; mechanism/examination directs evaluation. |
| Functional | Altered function in a recognized clinical syndrome | Real disorders requiring appropriate positive clinical assessment; normal tests alone are insufficient. |

This is an editorial framework. Specific disease claims need specific sources and criteria. Genetic susceptibility, environmental exposure and inflammation can coexist; do not rank mechanisms as probabilities from a generic table.

## Systems, disease families, targets and vulnerable organs

Treatment entries explain categories commonly discussed in care. They are search destinations, not recommendations, approvals or comparative efficacy claims. Availability, indication and safety depend on exact product, route, market, age and condition. Tests are examples a clinician might select for a defined question, not a universal work-up. Retrieve the current local label and disease guideline before discussing a medicine. Do not generate an individual prescription, titration, pediatric or renal dose from chat.

| System | Representative disease families | Medicine targets and vulnerable organs | Tests/context that distinguish questions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Central/peripheral nervous | Stroke; epilepsy; migraine; neuropathy; degeneration; CNS infection | Excitability, neurotransmission, vascular/immune pathways; sedation, cognition, falls and renal/hepatic elimination may matter | Examination, onset, imaging/EEG when indicated; normal scan is not universal neurologic exclusion. |
| Heart/vessels | Coronary disease; heart failure; arrhythmia; valve disease; hypertension; venous thrombosis | Thrombosis, rhythm, vascular tone/fluid pathways; bleeding, kidney function, electrolytes and conduction can limit choices | Symptoms, reliable BP technique, ECG, selected biomarkers/echo/vascular imaging; bare BP is not a treatment plan. |
| Respiratory | Asthma; COPD; pneumonia; interstitial disease; embolism; sleep-related breathing disorders | Airway muscle, inflammation/pathogens; cardiovascular effects, immune suppression and inhaler delivery may matter | Exposures, examination, appropriate spirometry, targeted imaging/sleep testing; oxygen reading alone does not identify cause. |
| GI/bowel | Reflux/ulcer; infection; inflammatory bowel disease; celiac; motility/functional disorders; obstruction; neoplasia | Acid, immune signaling, pathogens/motility; kidney effects, bleeding and infection risks vary | Alarm features, stool pattern/weight trajectory, selected blood/stool tests, endoscopy/imaging; timing/previous therapy affect interpretation. |
| Liver/biliary/pancreas | Viral/metabolic/toxic liver disease; cholestasis; gallstones; pancreatitis; exocrine insufficiency | Viral, immune/metabolic processes, enzyme replacement; hepatic injury/interactions/renal function matter | Liver-test pattern/synthesis, imaging/lipase/specific tests with timing; incidental fatty liver does not explain all pain. |
| Renal/urinary | Acute/chronic kidney disease; glomerular disease; stones; infection; obstruction; bladder disorders | Hemodynamic, immune, microbial/outflow pathways; potassium, volume and accumulation matter | eGFR trend, urine albumin/sediment, indicated culture, electrolytes/targeted imaging; filtration differs from obstruction. |
| Endocrine/metabolic | Diabetes; thyroid; adrenal/pituitary; calcium/bone metabolism | Replacement/suppression, receptor/metabolic targets; hypoglycemia, electrolytes, cardiac/skeletal effects vary | Appropriately timed paired hormone tests, glucose context/assay intervals; no insulin/steroid initiation from bare number. |
| Blood/immune | Anemia; hemoglobinopathies; bleeding/clotting; immune deficiency; autoimmunity; malignancy | Cell production, clotting, immune activation/malignant cells; marrow suppression, bleeding/infection matter | CBC pattern, targeted iron/coagulation/hemoglobin/specialist tests; fatigue does not specify deficiency. |
| Skin | Dermatitis; psoriasis; infection; urticaria; blistering; tumors | Barrier repair, inflammation, immunity/pathogens; absorption/local effects vary by site/formulation | Distribution/timing/exposure, exam, indicated culture/microscopy/biopsy; photograph cannot clear malignancy. |
| Musculoskeletal | Osteoarthritis; inflammatory arthritis; osteoporosis; tendon/muscle disease; trauma | Pain, inflammatory pathways/bone turnover; GI bleeding, renal effects, infection/metabolic risks vary | Function, joint exam, mechanism, selected markers/imaging; degenerative images may be incidental. |
| Reproductive/pelvic/breast | Endometriosis; abnormal uterine bleeding; ovarian/testicular/prostate disease; infertility; breast disease; infection | Hormone, inflammation, pathogen/neoplastic targets; clotting, bone, fertility, pregnancy/lactation matter | Actual organs, cycle/life stage, pregnancy potential, targeted exam/imaging/labs/pathology; identity is not organ inventory. |
| Multisystem | Sepsis; systemic autoimmunity; genetic syndromes; drug toxicity; cancer complications | Immune, vascular, marrow/metabolic targets; several organs may become vulnerable | Trajectory, vital signs, function, reconciliation/coordinated assessment; do not force single-organ attribution. |

A medicine's intended target, clearance route and adverse effects are different questions. An anti-inflammatory agent may act on immune pathways while changing infection susceptibility; an antithrombotic targets clotting while introducing bleeding risk. This atlas does not establish the frequency or magnitude of those effects for a particular product. Ask what benefit is expected for the actual condition, what monitoring is supported, and what patient factors alter the balance. Retrieve authoritative current evidence rather than converting a mechanism into a universal treatment recommendation.

The test question should be equally explicit. An ECG records electrical activity, while an echocardiogram assesses selected structural and functional features; they are not interchangeable checks that everything cardiac is normal. A urine culture addresses a microbial question and an albumin measurement a different renal question. A biopsy can characterize tissue but cannot represent every organ simultaneously. Normal ranges, reference intervals, analytical error and clinical decision thresholds are different concepts. Results should remain linked to collection conditions and to the reason the test was ordered.

## Sex-related anatomy, hormones and life stages

Ask respectfully about anatomy/physiology only when relevant. Information may include organs present, prior removal/reconstruction, gonadal function, hormone use, menstrual pattern, pregnancy possibility and lactation. Gender identity, legal marker, appearance or a male/female label cannot substitute for this inventory. Transgender and intersex people have diverse anatomy/endocrine histories; infer neither standard anatomy nor a particular variation. Use the person's language and explain why the question matters. Screening follows organs and risk under current guidance, not this atlas. S350

Puberty, cycling, pregnancy, postpartum change, lactation and menopause alter physiology and sometimes test interpretation and medicine suitability. Pregnancy adds placental/fetal compartments; postpartum symptoms need their own urgency assessment. Lactation adds exposure considerations for an infant. Menopause changes ovarian hormonal patterns but does not establish that every new symptom is menopausal. Gonad removal or hormone treatment may matter regardless of identity. Ask about pregnancy potential before relevant imaging/medicine discussions; do not make that possibility contingent on a social category alone.

Sex-related biology includes more than reproduction. Hormone exposure, body composition, immune signaling and life stage can influence disease or treatment response; gendered behavior, access, occupation and discrimination also shape health. Separate these factors rather than attributing them to one undifferentiated variable. A result in one study population may not generalize equally to everyone. NIH's sex-as-biological-variable framework concerns study design, analysis and reporting, not proof that a therapy has been validated in every subgroup. S355

Review enrollment, sex-disaggregated outcomes when available, age/life stage, pregnancy/lactation exclusions, hormone exposure and whether subgroup analyses were adequately powered. Historically underexamined female health needs must remain visible: pelvic pain/endometriosis, abnormal bleeding, pregnancy/postpartum complications and differences beyond reproduction deserve condition-specific investigation. Do not universalize a male-dominated study, invent female-specific benefit from a small subgroup, or treat missing subgroup findings as no difference. Separate biological hypotheses, demonstrated clinical effects and evidence gaps. S355

The individual inventory is a relevance tool, not a demand for exhaustive disclosure. For a respiratory question, reproductive history may be unnecessary unless pregnancy or another factor changes the assessment. For an organ-specific screening question, a relevant retained organ and prior procedure may be essential. Explain the connection and permit uncertainty. If records are incomplete, say which missing information affects interpretation rather than filling gaps from identity. Avoid presenting variations of sex development as a single uniform condition; endocrine and surgical histories can differ substantially.

## Ancestry, race, ethnicity and individual evidence

Race is a social category, ethnicity cultural/historical affiliation, and genetic ancestry a statistical description of inherited relationships. None determines genotype. Record an actual variant, validated test or family history separately. Environment, exposure geography, occupation, diet, care access and inequity can matter without being genetic. Consumer ancestry estimates are not clinical diagnoses or substitutes for targeted tests. Avoid biological stereotypes and racial-category drug-response instructions. S356

Use race-free eGFR models in their intended context; identify the laboratory equation. Race-free does not mean error-free. Creatinine/cystatin C estimates have limitations; combined estimates may help near consequential decision thresholds. Do not splice a race coefficient into a result. For trans/intersex people, hormone exposure/body composition may complicate a model's sex variable; seek appropriate clinical interpretation instead of inventing a universally correct coefficient. S354

G6PD deficiency illustrates individual testing. The relevant question may be a planned hemolysis-risk medicine, previous episode or family history, not racial category. CDC calls for quantitative G6PD testing before primaquine/tafenoquine; local assay ranges and pregnancy considerations matter. No regimen/dose is supplied here. Hemoglobinopathies likewise need blood testing and sometimes genetic confirmation; anyone can have a relevant variant. Screening/confirmation follows local newborn or prenatal programs. Never diagnose or exclude sickle cell disease or thalassemia from appearance. S357 S358

Pulse oximetry estimates rather than directly measures arterial oxygenation. FDA identifies limitations involving pigmentation and measurement conditions. Skin tone is not race, and a device limitation is not an assumption about one reading. Consider symptoms, perfusion, movement, temperature, device type and instructions. Severe breathlessness or deterioration warrants assessment despite a reassuring home display. Do not invent a numerical correction for darker skin; validation across tones is a research/regulatory issue. S359

Population differences in a study can motivate investigation without defining an individual's biology. Ask whether the study measured the actual causal variable or used a proxy. A reported disparity may reflect access, exposure, discrimination, measurement error, biology or several interacting causes. Do not silently convert association into inherited causation. Family history may sharpen a clinical question but still needs detail about diagnoses, age of onset and affected relatives. A negative family history does not exclude every inherited disorder; new variants, small families and incomplete information are possible.

## Answer workflow and limits

Determine urgency through the relevant triage source. Describe the selected normal pathway in one or two sentences, identify possible classes of disruption without diagnosing, and state which missing context changes the next step. Retrieve disease guidance before tests, comparative therapy or referral. For medicines, distinguish target from vulnerable organs and verify product/route/market. Harm in another system is a possibility requiring evidence, not an invitation for a generic warning list.

An existing prescribed rescue plan belongs to its dedicated pathway: clarify its documented instructions and support appropriate use rather than improvising anatomy-based treatment. Verified lay first aid is distinct from prescribing. State known observations, hypotheses and how evaluation could resolve uncertainty. Do not propose all-organ screening, whole-body imaging or broad panels because many systems are listed. This foundational explanation/search routing requires testing independently from clinical safety or diagnostic performance.

When sources disagree, first check whether they address the same question, population, setting and date. A patient overview, anatomy textbook and regulator's label have different purposes. An old educational description may be useful for normal structure while being unsuitable for current treatment decisions. Local approval does not establish universal availability, and an investigational mechanism does not become standard care by appearing in a pathway diagram. Referral should follow the person's problem and current evidence, not the number of systems mentioned.
