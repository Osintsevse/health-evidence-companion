## 1. What the database check showed
In the original 65 records, the main professional reference points were WHO, NICE, APA, BACP/BPS, AAMFT, ESSM and the Australian CCI. This is not the basis of predominantly Russian practice. However, the language of publications and the selection of guidelines are noticeably oriented towards the English-speaking professional field. This source origin does not mean that the participants in all studies are English-speaking residents of rich countries.

This is a qualitative audit of the register and not a bibliometric assessment: the proportion of countries among participants in all included reviews has not been calculated. The addition expands the geography but does not completely eliminate language, publication, and research bias. Translating a review into Russian does not turn it into a study of Russian-speaking people.

International practice includes several levels: specialized methods for specific problems; transdiagnostic programs; participation of trained specialists of other profiles under supervision; digital assistance; cultural adaptation and verification of results. WHO has issued separate guidance on introducing psychological interventions into existing services. The existence of guidance does not equal the effectiveness of any implementation. [S66]

## 2. What is tested outside the usual Anglo-American sample
This is a selection of representative primary studies, rather than a ranking of countries or a comprehensive overview of all cultures.

| Context and source | Design and result | Limits on transfer |
|---|---|---|
| India, HAP [S70] | 495 randomized participants with severe depression; behavioral program with consultants plus enhanced usual care is better than enhanced assistance alone after 3 months | The program was tested in primary care, not free conversation and not AI |
| Zimbabwe, Friendship Bench [S71] | 573 participants, 24 clinics; solving problems with trained workers and supervision; improvement in symptoms at 6 months relative to increased usual care | The majority are women; context and performers are specific; training and supervision are part of the intervention |
| Ukraine, CETA [S72] | 302 participants, veterans, displaced people and adult relatives; the standard and short versions are better than a waitlist on several outcomes; standard is generally stronger than short | The conflict-affected sample does not represent all Ukrainians, Russian speakers, or “Slavs”; this is not an AI test |
| Russia, iCBT-I [S73] | 107 Russian-speaking sleep clinic patients; program plus usual care improved insomnia severity after treatment; there was no intergroup difference in this indicator after 3 months | Disorder Specific and Digital Protocol; not proof of the effectiveness of a universal chatbot |
| USA, Chinese-American sample [S69] | 50 patients: culturally adapted CBT produced greater reductions in depressive symptoms than standard CBT; the majority in both groups did not achieve remission | Small research; the difference in treatment discontinuation did not reach statistical significance; additional benefit of adaptation is not guaranteed |
| Colombia, CETA [S78] | Total three-group sample 521; this publication compares CETA with waiting. Improvements varied between the two cities; the overall symptom score did not change significantly in either city | Even the proximity of language and origin does not ensure the same result. Don't attribute differences solely to culture: conditions, performers and participation varied too |

Conclusion of the synthesis: International methods can work in different societies, but each transfer requires testing of the population, task, execution and outcome. Neither “Western therapy is unsuitable for us” nor “the psyche is the same everywhere, the context is unimportant” follows from these data.

The CFI cultural interview in a six-country study with 318 patients and 75 clinicians was found to be acceptable and useful for gathering context. This is a feasibility study, not evidence of improved clinical outcomes. [S68] Below are the project's own conversational guidelines, not a copy of the CFI or its validated short version.

## 3. How to apply it in casual conversation
Do not require completion of a cultural questionnaire. Clarify only what changes the understanding of the situation or the next step; usually one question at a time.

| What's important | What can you clarify? | What not to assume |
|---|---|---|
| Language of experience | What does “resentment”, “longing”, “emptiness”, “I must” mean here? | That a dictionary translation fully conveys experience |
| Family and mutual obligations | What obligations does a person choose himself, and which ones does he consider imposed? | That independence is always better than interdependence or maintaining a family is always more important than leaving |
| Way of expressing feelings | Do direct questions, stories, specific actions, or a gentler pace help? | That a Russian-speaking man necessarily needs toughness or finds it difficult to talk about feelings |
| Trust and help | What was useful in the previous help, and what made you close down? | Distrust of specialists, trauma or shame only at the place of birth |
| Real circumstances | What restrictions are created by money, documents, work, discrimination, distance, caring for loved ones? | That the real danger is “distorted thinking” |
| Moving and Belonging | Are there significant losses, new opportunities, or a break in familiar connections? | That any expat is traumatized or bound to experience an identity crisis |
| Sexuality and roles | What do the parties understand by intimacy, agreement and fair contribution? | Norm of sex frequency, gender responsibilities or the right to pressure |
| Values and meaning | What does a person himself consider a worthy life and an acceptable cost of choice? | Mandatory religious, individualistic or familial model of meaning |

These topics are hypotheses for clarification, and not a description of a “typical Russian,” Serb, Ukrainian or Slav. Language, citizenship, ethnic identity, generation and personal experience are not interchangeable. The historical background helps ask the question, but does not provide the answer for a specific person. Differences within any group should be taken into account as much as between groups.

## 4. Adapt the form while maintaining the meaning
Published work on PM+ in Colombia describes the sequence: local context collection, adaptation hypotheses, consultation, external evaluation. The study is methodological; Due to safety concerns, planned discussions with the target groups themselves did not take place. Therefore, it does not prove either the clinical superiority of adaptation or the sufficiency of expert opinions alone. [S67]

Working version for this project:
1. Name the task and the proposed mechanism of assistance.
2. Check whether the language is clear and whether the task aligns with the person’s values.
3. Change example, wording, or step size; maintain voluntariness and a functioning mechanism.
4. Ask about the effect without treating agreement with the assistant as improvement.
5. If it gets worse or everything gets stuck, reconsider the hypothesis and format, and do not attribute failure to “mentality.”

Examples of your own adaptations of wording:
- **ACT, acceptance:** giving the experience space to choose action; do not approve of injustice and do not commit to tolerating the relationship.
- **Self-compassion:** evaluate a mistake without condemning the entire person; responsibility and correction remain.
- **Boundaries:** define your own decisions and available actions; do not establish rules for managing other people's behavior.
- **CBT, thought testing:** check the basis of a conclusion; do not force you to think positively and do not deny the external problem.
- **Values:** find out personally chosen reference points, including loved ones and mutual obligations; do not make independence a universal goal.

This is not a new culturally validated protocol. Translating a diagnostic scale “on the fly” is also not the same as validating it; For significant decisions, a suitable language version and correct interpretation are required.

## 5. For couples and for AI
In a joint conversation, find out the meanings of everyone: a common language and origin do not guarantee the same expectations. Do not transfer the preferences of one participant to another. Cultural tradition does not abolish consent or justify coercion. Observe 18_JOINT_CONVERSATION_PROTOCOL.md and 20_DATA_BOUNDARIES.md.

Distinguish between three separate transfers: **method → other population; language → another language; human specialist → AI**. The success of the first does not prove the second or third. The transfer of studies of short-term symptom reduction to long-term Russian-language conversations about sexuality, relationships, meaning, and mortality is particularly weak. Lack of confirmation does not prove uselessness, but it does limit promises. Details - 09_AI_EVIDENCE_AND_TOOLKITS.md.

## 6. How to maintain an international base
When replenishing, indicate the country and language of the participants separately from the country of the authors/journal; who was excluded; performer and supervision; comparison; term; adaptation and outcomes. Do not combine disparate versions of the program into the overall effect. Look beyond positive results. Priority is given to quality and applicability, not to the nationality of the author.
