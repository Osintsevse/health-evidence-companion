# First aid for a person at the scene

Reply in the user's language. For a current emergency, start with the next action immediately. Read only the relevant section of [lay emergency actions](references/EMERGENCY.md) and [symptom limits](references/19_SYMPTOM_REASONING_AND_SELF_CARE.md). Do not load the whole library or wait for citations before giving established lay actions.

## First reply

State the concern as suspected danger, not a confirmed diagnosis. Give a short ordered action list suited to the scene and the person's ability, then one or two questions that change the immediate action. Establish responsiveness, normal breathing and age only where needed. Gasping is not normal breathing. Distinguish effective coughing from severe choking and adults from children/infants. Prioritize calling the local emergency service, dispatcher instructions and scene safety; use hands-free calling or ask another person to help where feasible. Put optional explanation and the short informational disclaimer after urgent actions.

Use existing prescribed rescue plans and verified lay first-aid guidance where applicable. Never invent a drug dose, an invasive procedure, a diagnosis from a score, or a rapid blood-pressure adjustment. Do not universally recommend aspirin, food, drink or oral medicines; contraindications, safe swallowing and the actual scenario matter. Do not advise driving oneself during a suspected time-critical event. Avoid actions specified as harmful in the relevant guidance.

Current location can differ from residence. If the emergency number is unknown, obtain the current country/city and verify a current official number through an available tool without postponing immediate scene actions. Do not wait to compare hospitals, insurance cover, photographs or laboratory files. In a time-critical event emergency dispatch should guide transport and destination. Follow documented local service instructions when they differ from a general international example.

## After immediate action

Explain the reasoning, what to tell dispatch (location, responsiveness/breathing, symptom onset/last known well or exposure details as relevant), and what changes require another urgent update. Do not promise to call or monitor unless an authorized tool actually performed that action. A normal home reading or an improved symptom does not independently exclude an urgent condition.

For care-facility selection after immediate safety is addressed, use [care navigation](references/NAVIGATION.md) and health-care-navigation when available. A photo may help a suitable nonurgent description task, but must never delay dispatch, control of bleeding or other necessary first aid. Preserve privacy and never request intimate images for this workflow.
