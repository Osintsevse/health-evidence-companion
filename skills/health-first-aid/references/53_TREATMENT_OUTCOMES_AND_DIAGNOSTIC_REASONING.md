## DX-E1 — Diagnostic probability, pathway evidence and later severity answer different questions

ASH 2018 makes D-dimer exclusion conditional on an eligible clinical-probability pathway and sufficiently sensitive assay; a positive result alone cannot diagnose PE. PERC belongs to clinician-assessed low-probability emergency presentations. The guideline assumed validated probability assessment rather than comparing rules. Its sequential-pathway modeling sometimes used individual-test accuracy because accuracy after a preceding test was unavailable: this indirectness is not prospective validation of the complete sequence. [S749]

The 2026 AHA official summary concerns severity and management after acute PE, not merely the probability that PE exists. Biomarkers, right-ventricular function and clinical status inform its severity framework. A diagnostic probability estimate does not determine disposition, and a summary is not enough for individual discharge or anticoagulant selection. [S750]

## PAIN-E1 — An acceptance benefit does not settle every pain outcome

Kanzler et al. randomized 26 adults with chronic non-cancer pain in one US primary-care clinic to clinician-supported focused ACT or CBT-principle handouts, both with usual care. Acceptance favored ACT at six months; disability improved within its arm, but between-arm disability differences were nonsignificant. That preserves a positive result while leaving comparative functional benefit uncertain. It was not ACT versus a complete CBT course. [S748]

**Interpretation:** ask whether the target is pain intensity, interference, function or quality of life; what comparator/contact was provided; and whether the effect persisted and mattered in daily life. Nonsignificance does not prove equivalence. Qualitative QoL reports are not quantitative benefit estimates.

**Actual reading/limits:** researcher/root selected methods, detailed results, discussion/limitations and rights. Small pilot, retrospective registration, self-report, unequal contact and expert-investigator delivery limit transfer. Missing outcomes were modeled under an assumption, not recovered; no systematic harms analysis was identified. This is not a specifically chronic-primary-pain cohort, comprehensive efficacy synthesis or chat validation. Scales, protocol and source examples excluded. Kanzler and colleagues, BMC Primary Care 23:77 (2022), original factual synthesis under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), source link in S748.
