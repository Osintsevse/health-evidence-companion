# Nervous systems, psychiatric treatment, and cognitive decline

Evidence checked 2026-10-09. General education for clinician preparation, not diagnosis, prescribing, medical clearance, or a replacement for urgent care. A clinician determines cause, treatment, and monitoring. Adult pathways predominate; childhood, pregnancy, frailty, and major illness require their own context.

## Time course and danger

New confusion over hours or days, fluctuating attention, altered awareness, or sudden behavioral change may be delirium or another acute illness. Quiet withdrawal and drowsiness can be as concerning as agitation. Established dementia does not exclude delirium. Do not label this aging, anxiety, or inevitable progression. Establish the last usual state and seek urgent assessment. New weakness, speech difficulty, seizure, severe headache, or reduced responsiveness requires emergency services; a quiz, photograph, or long chat must not delay help. S416

Ask directly and calmly about suicidal thoughts, intent, access to means, and ability to remain safe. Immediate intent, a recent attempt, dangerous command hallucinations, inability to meet basic needs, or threatening behavior needs immediate local emergency/crisis help. Help contact a trusted nearby person when safe. Establish country before providing numbers; US contacts are not worldwide services. Avoid moral judgment, affirming delusions, or promises the host cannot keep. S25 S415

For planned discussion, collect only context that affects the next step: approximate age, time course, function, medicines and changes, sleep, substances, and professional diagnoses. Clarify contradictions: being called independent can coexist with unpaid bills, getting lost, or duplicate medicines. Ask for concrete examples rather than assigning a score. If a later turn reveals abrupt change or danger, immediately revise the route. Do not finish an educational explanation before addressing newly disclosed risk. S418

## Neurophysiology as interacting networks

Brain and spinal cord constitute the central nervous system. Overlapping brain networks support sensation, movement, language, memory, attention, emotion, and body regulation. The spinal cord carries information and supports local circuits. Peripheral nerves communicate with muscles, organs, and sensory receptors. Motor, sensory, and autonomic functions are distinguishable; autonomic pathways regulate circulation, sweating, and digestion without conscious control. A symptom does not itself localize injury or establish a psychiatric explanation. S409 S410

Neurons communicate through electrical activity and chemical signaling. Neurotransmitters act through specific receptors in specific circuits, and one transmitter has different roles in different locations. Connections change with development, learning, experience, and injury. Plasticity describes adaptation, not a promise that positive thinking or exercises reverse disease. Remembering involves attention, encoding, retention, and retrieval; difficulty may arise before information is stored. Use this model to explain uncertainty, not to infer neurotransmitter levels from a person's feelings. S409

An antidepressant target does not establish a measurable deficiency in its recipient. Treatment benefit, pharmacological mechanism, and disease causation are separate claims. Depression involves biological, psychological, social, and physical-health influences. Do not call it a proven simple serotonin shortage; do not infer medication cannot work because that explanation is incomplete. Neither conclusion follows from mechanism alone. Discuss actual outcomes and monitoring. An educational analogy should be marked as an analogy rather than an individual biological diagnosis. S25 S411

Tremor, tingling, low motivation, and concentration difficulty have multiple possible explanations, including medicines and physical illness. Help establish chronology and urgency rather than locate a lesion from a brief list. For a scan or nerve test, distinguish an observation in the report from clinical interpretation. Ask whether its interpreting clinician connected it with the symptoms. A normal result in one test does not establish that symptoms are imaginary, and an abnormal result does not automatically explain every complaint. S410

## Psychiatric assessment and medicine effects

Depression and anxiety can affect concentration, sleep, energy, and everyday functioning. Screening measures support assessment and tracking; a symptom count does not independently establish diagnosis. Discuss duration, previous episodes, physical illness, substances, prior response, and mood elevation. Preserve the person's description and uncertainty. Short-term improvement or worsening updates the timeline without settling diagnosis. Review practical impact and access to help rather than treating one score as the whole clinical picture. S25

Reduced need for sleep together with increased energy, rapid speech, racing thoughts, grandiosity, or risky behavior warrants assessment for mania/hypomania. This differs from exhausted insomnia. Medicines and substances can mimic or worsen symptoms. Past elevated episodes affect planning: antidepressant monotherapy can trigger mania or rapid cycling in bipolar disorder. Recommend timely prescriber contact, not adding a mood stabilizer or stopping medication yourself. Escalate immediately if judgment, safety, or basic care is compromised. S414

Psychosis includes hallucinations, delusions, and disorganization and can occur in psychiatric, neurological, medical, and substance-related conditions. New onset deserves assessment, especially when abrupt or accompanied by fluctuation. Respond to distress without endorsing a persecutory belief. Ask about safety non-confrontationally. Do not diagnose schizophrenia from one experience or dismiss a medical cause because the presentation sounds psychiatric. Early professional care and coordinated support may help even when the person is uncertain about accepting a label. S415

When symptoms follow initiation, changes, missed doses, or discontinuation, preserve chronology. Antidepressant withdrawal may involve dizziness, unusual sensory sensations, sleep problems, anxiety, and nausea. It may begin within days and persist variably. Relapse or another illness remains possible; a rigid timing rule cannot reliably separate them. Recommend clinician/pharmacist review of product, formulation, history, and symptoms. No individualized taper, cross-taper, restart, washout, or switch should be generated. Avoid blame: adverse effects, access, or misunderstanding may explain missed doses. S25

Include prescriptions, OTC products, supplements, alcohol, and recreational substances in medicine review. Some agents affect cognition or sedation. Severe agitation with fever and neuromuscular changes after serotonergic combinations requires urgent assessment for possible toxicity. Explain specific exposure and risk rather than calling every potential interaction inevitable harm. Abrupt cessation of a prescribed sedative can itself be dangerous. A relaxation exercise is not adequate management of suspected toxicity, severe withdrawal, or another acute medical problem. S411 S419

## Treatment choices and monitoring

SSRIs influence serotonin reuptake; SNRIs influence serotonin/norepinephrine reuptake; NDRIs are another discussed class. Tricyclics and MAO inhibitors remain selected options with differing adverse-effect and interaction burdens. Class names cannot choose the best individual drug. Previous response, comorbidity, medicines, tolerability, and preferences matter. Benefit often takes weeks while adverse effects may appear earlier. Early energy or sleep improvement alone does not prove recovery. Agitation, suicidality, and mood elevation require prompt review, not simply waiting for the usual benefit window. S411

Monitoring connects treatment with meaningful goals such as eating, personal care, work, sleep, or engagement. Review harms, barriers, suicidal thinking, and activation. NICE recommends early review after antidepressant initiation, with earlier attention for younger adults or suicide risk. Lack of improvement prompts diagnostic and treatment review rather than automatic escalation. Exact additional checks depend on the product and medical risks. Establish whom to contact between appointments and whether the plan is practical; an educational overview cannot replace the current medicine label. S25

Psychotherapy includes structured approaches rather than one universal conversation. CBT examines thought/emotion/behavior links; exposure addresses selected anxiety-related avoidance; other approaches focus on relationships, coping, or recurring patterns. Condition and therapist expertise matter. Ask about rationale, goals, participation, expected course, and progress review. Supportive chat may organize concerns but is not a complete clinical course. Consider language, sensory needs, preferences, trust, and access without interpreting difficulties engaging as proof treatment cannot work. S412

For less severe depression, NICE does not routinely offer antidepressants first unless an informed preference supports them; psychological options or active monitoring may fit. This does not prohibit medication or minimize distress. More severe or recurrent illness can need combined care and specialist help. Explain the guideline as a contextual option, not an absolute rule for every jurisdiction. People may reconsider choices with their clinician when benefit, adverse effects, or circumstances change. No modality should be portrayed as inherently more authentic or morally preferable. S25

ECT induces a controlled seizure under anesthesia in clinical care and may be considered for severe illness, treatment resistance, or a need for rapid response. Risks include anesthesia concerns, confusion, and memory effects; placement and pulse characteristics affect risk. rTMS uses targeted magnetic pulses without general anesthesia, usually over repeated sessions. Selection depends on indication, response, safety, and service capability. Neither is a home technique. Do not promise universal success or absence of memory problems. Discuss relapse and maintenance planning with the treating service. S413

NIMH describes magnetic seizure therapy as research-only in the US. Promising technology news requires checking device, indication, regulator, comparator, outcome, and whether access is through research. A treatment authorized for one indication is not blanket authorization for any mood complaint. Distinguish trial evidence from testimonials and a mechanistic effect from clinically meaningful improvement. An older primer cannot resolve a newer regulatory change, and an experimental device should not be described as equivalent to an authorized treatment. S413

## Memory complaints, MCI, and dementia

Occasional forgetting with preserved function differs from persistent progressive decline. MCI means greater-than-expected cognitive problems with substantial independence preserved; not everyone progresses to Alzheimer's. Dementia interferes with daily life and may involve judgment, language, attention, behavior, and visuospatial abilities as well as memory. Neither an online quiz nor one normal score diagnoses or excludes a problem. Testing circumstances, hearing, education, language, and vision affect interpretation. Functional examples and clinical assessment matter. S418 S417

An anamnesis records onset, trajectory, fluctuation, domains, and practical effects. Collateral information from someone familiar with usual abilities can help with permission. Review medicines, substances, mood, sleep, hearing, vision, medical history, and neurological symptoms. Clinicians select examination, laboratory tests, imaging, and cognitive assessment to answer particular questions. Do not prescribe a universal panel. Explain what remains uncertain and how a test could change management. Assistance may have quietly compensated for lost ability; ask whether independence depends on new support. S420

Depression, sensory problems, poor sleep, and medicine effects can contribute to memory complaints and coexist with neurodegeneration. Treating one does not prove it caused all symptoms; continued decline requires reassessment. Hearing difficulty may affect understanding and verbal testing, so accessible communication matters. Clinicians review impairing medicines rather than advising abrupt cessation. Discuss both potentially treatable contributors and further evaluation if improvement does not occur. Avoid merely psychological language that dismisses mood symptoms or a person's distress. S419 S420

Alzheimer's is one dementia cause. Vascular cognitive impairment relates to vascular injury; Lewy body dementia involves alpha-synuclein and can affect attention, perception, movement, and sleep; frontotemporal disorders prominently affect behavior or language. Mixed pathology and overlap complicate distinctions. Do not assign a subtype from one feature. Accurate cause matters because treatments and course differ. Uncertain, atypical, younger-onset, or rapidly progressive presentations need appropriate specialist assessment. Family descriptions guide questions but cannot replace examination and investigations. S417

## Alzheimer's therapies: symptoms and disease modification

Cholinesterase inhibitors such as donepezil, rivastigmine, and galantamine support cholinergic signaling and can help some symptoms. Memantine acts on NMDA-related signaling and is used at different disease stages. Neither class cures Alzheimer's. Symptomatic therapy, disease modification, and supportive care have different goals and may coexist. Discuss expected functional benefit, adverse effects, and review arrangements rather than demand a score improve. New formulations or prodrugs require their own labels and should not be assumed interchangeable milligram-for-milligram. S433

NICE recommends cholinesterase inhibitors as options for mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's and memantine in specified moderate/severe circumstances, including some combinations. These cannot be transferred to every subtype. The guideline advises against these medicines for frontotemporal dementia and has different selected options for Lewy body dementia. This makes accurate assessment and specialist advice important. It does not authorize stopping existing treatment by chat. The question is whether the established diagnosis, stage, goals, harms, and review plan support the particular prescription. S434

Lecanemab and donanemab target amyloid and demonstrated slower average clinical decline in selected people with early symptomatic Alzheimer's. They are not cures or proven restorers of lost memory and are not established for every stage or cause of dementia. Amyloid confirmation and clinical staging are essential. A positive biomarker without clinical context does not establish eligibility. Advanced dementia, another primary cause, or inability to complete safety monitoring changes the discussion. Balance benefit against burden and possible serious harm with a specialist. S422 S424

Clarity AD at 18 months showed mean CDR-SB worsening of 1.21 with lecanemab versus 1.66 with placebo, an absolute difference of 0.45; the published difference confidence interval was -0.67 to -0.23. The often-reported 27% slowing means relative reduction in worsening on this scale, not 27% recovery or a 27% chance of avoiding dementia. Both groups worsened on average. Original article full text was blocked; its indexed original results and current regulator study table were inspected. No full-reading claim is made. S426 S422

TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 followed early symptomatic participants for 76 weeks. In the combined tau population, iADRS declined -10.2 with donanemab versus -13.1 with placebo, difference 2.92; CDR-SB worsened 1.72 versus 2.42, difference -0.70. Low/medium tau primary iADRS decline was -6.02 versus -9.27, difference 3.25 and 35.1% relative slowing. Do not substitute that subgroup headline for the whole population or compare it directly with lecanemab as proof of superiority. Populations, endpoints, and analyses differed; these were not head-to-head trials. S427

These are group averages over limited follow-up. Clinical scales combine cognitive and functional domains but cannot guarantee additional independent months for a particular person. Extensions can add information, yet loss of randomized comparison introduces selection and survivor effects. Plaque reduction is not clinical recovery. Discuss whether the expected benefit matches goals and whether imaging, visits, expense, and care-partner involvement are acceptable. A clinician should explain how progression and adverse effects would affect continuation without promising the course of one individual's illness. S426 S427

ARIA-E involves imaging abnormalities with edema/effusion; ARIA-H includes microhemorrhage and superficial siderosis. Many events have no symptoms, so symptom monitoring alone is insufficient. Serious hemorrhage, seizures, and fatal events have occurred. Headache, confusion, visual change, dizziness, or gait difficulty during treatment needs assessment; acute severe or stroke-like symptoms require emergency care and disclosure of antiamyloid therapy. A recent normal MRI does not justify waiting for the next appointment. Avoid suggesting a preventive medicine or interpreting an MRI yourself. S424 S423

Original donanemab trial ARIA-E occurred in 24.0% of treated participants versus 2.1% on placebo; 52 treated participants had symptomatic ARIA-E. These are rates from the original regimen and selected trial population, not timeless individual probabilities or necessarily current-regimen rates. APOE epsilon4, prior hemorrhagic imaging findings, and other clinical risks affect discussions. Do not derive personal risk from ancestry or one table. The publication reports treatment-related deaths; describing most imaging events as asymptomatic should never imply the class is harmless. S427 S424

## Exact jurisdiction, product, and route

US labels support initiation at the MCI or mild dementia stage with confirmed amyloid. APOE epsilon4 homozygotes face greater ARIA risk but are not categorically excluded by US indications; testing/counseling informs risk. The July 2026 LEQEMBI label includes subcutaneous IQLIK initiation, making maintenance-only descriptions outdated. Its MRI section specifies baseline and imaging after 1, 2, 3, and 6 months, reviewed before the next administration. An infusion-number schedule cannot simply be copied to the injection pathway. This is education, not operational dosing advice. S422

US KISUNLA is intravenous. The inspected prescribing text requires baseline MRI and imaging before infusions 2, 3, 4, and 7, plus evaluation for suspected ARIA. The newer initiation regimen differs from the pivotal-trial regimen; the label describes lower ARIA with the newer regimen. This packet supplies no personal initiation, interruption, resumption, or switch instructions. The treating service must verify its current label and clinical protocol. A schedule summary is not permission to receive a dose, especially when symptoms or imaging have changed. S424

EU indications are narrower: eligible early-Alzheimer's patients must be APOE epsilon4 non-carriers or heterozygotes and have confirmed amyloid. Product information includes contraindications involving relevant hemorrhagic MRI findings and initiation during ongoing anticoagulation. Kisunla has additional restrictions, including circumstances preventing MRI assessment. EU Leqembi MRI is before infusions 3, 5, 7, and 14. EU Kisunla MRI is before doses 2, 3, 4, and 7 and at one year for specified risk factors. Use these labels, not an imported US eligibility rule. S423 S425

Anticoagulants and antiplatelets are different categories. Their indications may be important, and cessation can cause harm. US warnings emphasize caution with bleeding risks/anticoagulation; EU initiation restrictions are explicit. Specialists must reconcile the complete list and imaging history. ARIA can resemble ischemic stroke, making antiamyloid treatment important information for emergency clinicians considering thrombolysis. Advise disclosure and emergency assessment, not administration or withholding of acute stroke therapy. Do not tell someone to stop anticoagulation to become eligible for an antibody. S422 S425

UK MHRA licensed lecanemab for a restricted early-Alzheimer's population with one or no APOE epsilon4 copies. A licence is not automatic reimbursement, nearby availability, or individual suitability. Current final NICE antiamyloid reimbursement was not established from accessible primary pages in this review. Older consultation recommendations are drafts, not confirmed final current coverage. Verify regulator, payer, and local service separately. EU authorization does not establish approval/funding in Serbia or another non-EU country. Preserve uncertainty rather than infer access from geographic proximity or a news headline. S430

## Biomarkers, genetics, and novel therapies

FDA-cleared Lumipulse G pTau217/beta-amyloid 1-42 plasma ratio aids amyloid-pathology identification in symptomatic adults aged at least 55 in specialty care. FDA explicitly says it is not for screening or standalone diagnosis. Results need clinical interpretation and may be positive, negative, or indeterminate. Accuracy in a cognitively impaired population cannot be transferred unchanged to healthy consumers. A different assay or laboratory-developed test is not authorized because it mentions the same analyte. The exact specimen, assay, intended purpose, and clinical setting matter. S428

A current FDA recall record describes specific Lumipulse beta-amyloid plasma calibrator lots associated with falsely elevated positive or indeterminate ratios. The correction called for reviewing affected results with medical directors and notifying relevant clinicians. This is lot/product-specific, not proof every biomarker or result is invalid. When the affected assay informs a major decision, ask the lab or ordering clinician whether the correction applies. Do not reinterpret the raw ratio, diagnose disease, or assume a result is unaffected without the laboratory's review. S429

APOE influences disease risk and treatment safety but a risk allele is not a prediction of one's future symptoms. Phenotype, age, other genetic influences, and environment matter. Testing may reveal information relevant to relatives, so counseling helps before treatment-selection testing. Do not convert consumer genotype into diagnosis, recommend preventive antibody treatment for an asymptomatic carrier, or label a population association destiny. Family history may justify a discussion, not automatic public sharing of genetic files. Rare inherited-disease concerns warrant a different specialist pathway rather than an APOE-only explanation. S421

Race/ethnicity are not identical to genetic ancestry or individual genome. Representation and access affect applicability. TRAILBLAZER-ALZ 2 was predominantly White, a limitation its authors acknowledge. This neither proves absence of benefit elsewhere nor justifies predicting benefit by race. Explain subgroup uncertainty and care conditions. Avoid a race-based diagnostic probability engine or ancestry assumptions from appearance, name, language, or country. Clinical risk assessment should use relevant verified information and appropriate counseling, with awareness of how unrepresentative data can limit precision. S427 S421

For new-treatment news, independently check product, indication, jurisdiction, date, decision type, and results. Oral semaglutide's evoke/evoke+ phase 3 trials had negative clinical outcomes and were discontinued. The original indexed abstract was inspected, not the full publication. Biomarker changes or exploratory reanalyses do not establish an approved Alzheimer's indication. A drug authorized for diabetes or weight management should not be repurposed by chat based on headlines. Preserve the distinction between an association, a hypothesis worth testing, a negative confirmatory result, and a regulator-approved use. S431

Research continues on prevention, antibodies, and other mechanisms. NIA's active listing includes AHEAD 3-45 testing lecanemab in cognitively healthy people with elevated/intermediate amyloid. This is a study question, not an approved prevention use. Some listing projected end dates are past; it cannot prove a site is enrolling now. Check the named registry, last update, phase, status, and local contact before offering participation. Registration is not efficacy evidence and an investigational arm is not promised treatment. This review does not pretend to exhaust the global pipeline. S432

## Function, care partners, and prevention

Support living well now through accessible communication, familiar routines, meaningful activities, medicine organization, and practical safety. Ask about driving, getting lost, cooking, financial vulnerability, falls, and assistance when relevant. Diagnosis does not erase rights; include the person, obtain permission for care-partner involvement, and plan ahead while preferences can be expressed. Caregivers need education, respite, and support too. Sudden behavior worsening warrants evaluation for pain, illness, or other causes rather than automatic sedation. A care plan should reflect capabilities and preferences instead of only a disease label. S420

Physical activity, vascular-risk management, sleep, and engagement support health and may reduce risk but do not guarantee prevention or cure dementia. Offer feasible activity and encourage clinical management without generating changes from bare numbers. Commercial brain-training apps are not established equivalents of studied programs. Avoid blame: dementia does not prove someone failed to exercise, think positively, or follow an ideal diet. Review each prevention claim separately from treatment. A person's goals may include pleasure, companionship, mobility, and dignity even when cognitive improvement is uncertain. S419

Across routes, make the next step concrete: emergency assessment for abrupt dangerous change; timely medicine/psychiatric review for activation, psychosis, or withdrawal; planned assessment for persistent cognitive complaints; specialist decisions for disease-modifying therapies. Explain uncertainty and what information would resolve it. Preserve the public/private boundary: this packet and its wholly synthetic tests contain no individual records, identifiers, case adaptations, or private links. A disclaimer never validates an unsafe action. The plugin should help prepare useful questions and recognize limits, without claiming clinical validation or replacing professional judgment.

## A practical answer structure for this module

Use a short sequence that makes uncertainty useful. First identify the person's immediate question: understanding a term, preparing a visit, checking a media claim, or responding to a change. Next determine whether urgency overrides that question. Then explain the distinction that changes the decision, such as symptom relief versus slowing, withdrawal versus a settled diagnosis, or marketing authorization versus actual access. End with a specific next step and the information a clinician or laboratory needs. Do not add a long intake questionnaire when one clarification would resolve the issue.

When a user supplies conflicting accounts, keep both until resolved. For example, a reported normal test and continuing functional difficulty are two observations, not a reason to erase one. Ask about the date, context, and intended purpose of the test. When a medicine label seems inconsistent with another source, compare dates, jurisdiction, and route before deciding which statement applies. If the current document cannot be accessed, say what was read and what remains unverified. Do not silently substitute an older label because it is easier to retrieve.

Keep quantitative explanations auditable. Identify the study population, comparator, endpoint, direction of the scale, duration, and absolute difference. Describe relative percentages only after the absolute comparison. Distinguish a subgroup, primary analysis, sensitivity analysis, and exploratory result. If a question asks how much an individual will benefit, state that a group mean cannot supply that prediction. For a direct comparison between products, check whether the evidence actually randomized those products against each other. A persuasive graph or press release does not resolve this question.

For clinical visit preparation, offer a small set of questions with a purpose: What diagnosis and stage are being considered? Which alternative contributors have been checked? What outcome would count as worthwhile benefit? Which harms or symptoms require earlier contact? What monitoring and support are needed? Who manages an unexpected change? Adapt those questions to the requested topic rather than reproducing every possible issue. Keep the person's preferences visible, including declining treatment or asking for more explanation.

For public knowledge upkeep, store only general original explanations and synthetic examples. A compelling real-world conversation is not a suitable public test case, even if names are removed. Record the source's access limitations, version, and jurisdiction alongside its role. Recheck unstable regulatory and safety claims before reuse. If an update changes a route, revise its tests too: a corrected label should change the expected response to an old-label prompt, rather than merely adding another citation to unchanged advice.
