# Chronic disease and multimorbidity navigation

Public educational module, checked 9 October 2026. This supports understanding, measurement, safety routing and clinician preparation; it does not diagnose, prescribe or establish an individual treatment target. Acute emergencies override routine monitoring. Children, pregnancy, dialysis and transplantation require dedicated pathways. Health-facing responses need a short disclaimer without delaying urgent action.

## Start with the decision

Separate four questions: Is there a new dangerous change? Is a reported diagnosis confirmed? Is established treatment being used as prescribed? What needs the next appointment? The same measurement has different implications in each. Ask what changed from baseline and when. Obtain units, method, symptoms, diagnoses, medicines and plans. Ask country or service when thresholds, approval or referral matter. Never upgrade a number, family history, image or wearable alert into diagnosis.

Separate future-event prevention from symptom relief. Treatment can be valuable without a sensation of improvement. Feeling better does not prove future risk fell. Prognosis depends on cause, severity, trajectory, treatment and circumstances; one biomarker cannot establish life expectancy. Genetic predisposition changes investigation questions, not destiny. Population estimates are not certainty. Primary-prevention calculators may be inappropriate after established cardiovascular disease.

During deterioration, long lifestyle interviews are inappropriate. Stable review should prioritize measurement quality, function and burden. Identify a practical next step, facts that would change it and who follows up. Options a clinician might discuss must not become a personal medication-selection algorithm.

## Blood pressure

Use a validated automatic upper-arm monitor and a cuff whose circumference range fits the arm. Validation should apply to the population, especially pregnancy. Brand recognition is insufficient. Bring the monitor for technique review. Wrist and cuffless smartwatch values are not interchangeable with properly obtained arm pressure. Record systolic and diastolic pressure in mmHg and pulse separately. S388 S389

Avoid smoking, caffeine, alcohol and exercise for 30 minutes before routine measurement. Empty the bladder; rest quietly more than five minutes. Sit with supported back, feet flat, uncrossed legs and bare arm supported at heart level. Do not talk. Take two readings one minute apart and retain both. Clothing, unsupported arm, pain or rushing can affect interpretation. Correct technique when stable; symptomatic emergencies must not wait for perfect measurement. S388

Office pressure is measured in a clinical setting. Home monitoring is a series by the person or helper. Ambulatory monitoring uses a programmed device across waking and sleeping hours. Each has separate averaging rules and thresholds. Higher clinic versus home values and the reverse pattern both deserve professional interpretation. A diary retains date, time, both values, arm, pulse, symptoms, activity and timing relative to medicines. Preserve raw readings; do not remove inconvenient highs or average different settings together.

NICE's adult diagnostic example combines clinic pressure at least 140/90 with daytime ambulatory or home average at least 135/85 mmHg. Its home protocol uses two seated values at least one minute apart, morning and evening for four to seven days; day one is excluded from the diagnostic average. The 2025 US guideline classifies hypertension from 130/80 and gives an overarching treatment goal below 130/80 with exceptions. Diagnosis, medication-start decisions and targets differ. Guideline, age, pregnancy, frailty, standing symptoms, organ disease and tolerability must be specified. S29 S389

For standing dizziness or falls, NICE uses a lying baseline, or seated if lying is inconvenient, then pressure after standing at least one minute. A drop of at least 20 systolic or 10 diastolic mmHg prompts cause and medication review. A negative seated-baseline test despite symptoms should be repeated from lying; persistent symptoms deserve assessment even without that drop. Do not ask someone prone to fainting to stand alone. Record starting posture, interval and symptoms; other protocols add timed readings. S29

### Severe or low pressure

In a nonpregnant adult around or above 180 systolic or 120 diastolic, ask immediately about chest pain, severe breathlessness, new weakness, numbness, speech or visual change and confusion. Possible acute organ-injury symptoms require emergency assessment; do not delay for repeats or attempt rapid home lowering. If stable without symptoms, rest briefly and repeat correctly. Persistent severe pressure needs prompt professional contact and timely assessment; chat cannot exclude silent organ injury. This is not automatically an ambulance indication and is not routine lifestyle-only follow-up. No extra tablets, borrowed drugs or invented rescue treatment. S388 S389

Pregnancy and after-birth care use a separate route. Pressure around 160 systolic or 110 diastolic is severe and warrants urgent maternity assessment, without waiting for nonpregnant thresholds. Severe headache, visual disturbance, pain below the ribs or epigastric pain, vomiting and reduced urine may indicate pre-eclampsia or another serious disorder. Completed delivery or prior normal pregnancy does not make symptoms safe. Contact maternity or emergency services according to severity. Do not create home antihypertensive or magnesium treatment. S390

Below 90/60 is commonly called low pressure, but a familiar asymptomatic baseline differs from sudden symptomatic decline. Recurrent dizziness or fainting requires review of causes, medicines and posture. Collapse, altered awareness, severe bleeding, chest pain, breathlessness or shock symptoms require emergency routing. Vomiting, diarrhea and poor intake can change urgency, particularly with renal or heart disease. No universal caffeine, salt, fluid-loading or pressor plan; fluids and salt may conflict with restrictions. Avoid abrupt standing when safe and seek assessment for recurring symptoms. S391

### Understanding treatment

Common classes include ACE inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers, long-acting calcium-channel blockers and thiazide-type diuretics. Combinations act through complementary mechanisms; single pills can simplify use. Selection depends on kidney function, potassium, pregnancy potential, adverse effects and comorbidity. Beta blockers may have additional heart indications. Ask what each drug is for and which tests follow changes. Do not combine classes casually because each lowers pressure. S389

Prepare questions: Was diagnosis confirmed outside clinic? Is the target home or office? Should standing values guide care? What happens after a missed dose? Who receives the diary? When are electrolytes and renal function checked? Persistent elevation may require technique, access, adherence and interaction review before labeling resistance. Organize questions without choosing the next drug. Lifestyle changes must fit CKD, heart failure and nutrition; potassium supplements and salt substitutes are not universally safe.

## Diabetes

Type 1 generally involves severe insulin deficiency, often autoimmune. Type 2 involves variable insulin resistance and impaired secretion. Gestational, monogenic and secondary forms differ. Age or body size alone cannot classify type. Family or genetic risk warrants screening questions, not diagnosis. Insulin use does not identify type. New thirst, frequent urination or unexplained weight loss needs assessment, especially if rapidly worsening.

ADA nonpregnant adult diagnostic examples: laboratory HbA1c at least 6.5% (48 mmol/mol), fasting plasma glucose at least 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L), or two-hour plasma glucose at least 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) after a 75 g glucose test. Random plasma glucose at least 200 requires classic symptoms or hyperglycemic crisis in that pathway. Without unequivocal hyperglycemia, confirmation is required. Pregnancy and altered red-cell turnover require different considerations. Meter and CGM trends are not interchangeable with laboratory diagnosis. S392

Retain units, fasting status, specimen/device and time. HbA1c percent and mmol/mol are not glucose units. Ask about anemia, transfusion, pregnancy, hemoglobin variants and other reasons HbA1c can misrepresent glycemia before interpreting disagreement. Repeated diagnostic-range results differ from one mildly elevated result. Diagnostic thresholds do not determine correction doses or necessarily equal treatment goals.

Goals depend on hypoglycemia, function, pregnancy, comorbidity and burden. Lower HbA1c is not always better if it produces dangerous lows. CGM review includes time below range and patterns. Recurrent significant hypoglycemia or an event requiring assistance needs urgent plan review. First aid belongs in the emergency pathway: swallowing safety precedes oral carbohydrate, and an unconscious or unsafe-swallow person receives no food or drink. Support the verified rescue plan while obtaining help. S393

### Treatment options and existing insulin

Type 2 medicines increasingly target heart and kidney outcomes as well as glucose. NICE's February 2026 guidance recognizes SGLT2 and GLP-1 cardiorenal benefits. Metformin, SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 medicines, DPP-4 inhibitors, sulfonylureas and insulin differ in mechanisms, hypoglycemia, weight effects, renal considerations, adverse effects, cost and route. Their benefits are not interchangeable. Brand, formulation and indication matter: obesity and diabetes products may share ingredients but differ in labels and eligibility. S305

Ask whether atherosclerotic disease, heart failure or CKD changes priorities, which tests follow changes, what adverse symptoms require contact and whether cost or device difficulties limit use. Mechanism explanations do not authorize selection: SGLT2 inhibitors increase urinary glucose loss; GLP-1 medicines affect glucose-dependent signaling and appetite; insulin replaces or supplements insulin action. Ask which benefit the prescriber intends. Foreign approvals or reimbursement rules do not guarantee local access. Do not calculate individual initiation, escalation or combinations.

For existing insulin establish exact product, concentration, pen or vial, route, written plan and whether the issue is routine or an error. Basal and meal-time products are not interchangeable, nor are U-100 and concentrated formulations. No starting, correction, titration, conversion or renal-adjusted doses from chat. Borrowed pens and uncertain products require pharmacist or team clarification. A possible incorrect product or excess dose needs urgent advice, even with presently normal glucose.

The inspected US Lantus U-100 label describes subcutaneous use, site rotation, checking the label, avoiding abnormal skin and never sharing pens even with a new needle. It is not pump insulin and must not be mixed or diluted. These are product-specific facts. Priming, needle handling, storage and disposal must follow the exact local instructions and educator demonstration. Repeated injection into lumps, followed by moving to unaffected skin, can alter absorption and needs monitoring and clinical supervision. S394

Teach-back can ask the person to identify the product, read concentration, locate their prescribed plan and describe taught device steps. Explain label sections and direct uncertainty to a pharmacist. Never substitute another pen's technique. Vision, dexterity, cognition, language and supplies may need practical support. Packaging photos are optional and must not delay help for an error. Verify unopened and in-use storage separately; one product's room-temperature period is not a universal rule.

### Sick days and crises

Prepare an individualized plan while well: glucose and ketone testing, insulin continuity, pump-failure backup, hydration within restrictions, contacts, and exact actions during illness, fasting or surgery. Insulin-deficient people must not simply stop basal insulin because they are not eating. Changes require an established plan or direct advice. General advice to continue all pills can conflict with specific precautions. No universal hold or continue list. Include restart instructions and who authorizes them. S395

DKA can cause vomiting, abdominal pain, deep breathing, fruity breath, thirst, frequent urination, drowsiness or confusion. Suspected DKA needs urgent emergency assessment even before diabetes is diagnosed. High ketones, or symptoms without testing, cannot prompt invented correction doses. HHS may involve marked hyperglycemia, profound dehydration and altered awareness; absence of fruity breath or high ketones does not exclude dangerous crisis. Acute routing supersedes routine monitoring. S395 S393

SGLT2 ketoacidosis may occur without strikingly elevated glucose. The inspected empagliflozin label requires assessment regardless of glucose and discontinuation when ketoacidosis is suspected. Explain that verified safety instruction paired with immediate medical assessment, not a stop-all-medicines rule. Vomiting and labored breathing on SGLT2 treatment cannot be dismissed because glucose is near usual. Identify medicine and last dose without delaying help for a complete history. S319

## Respiratory disease

Asthma diagnosis needs characteristic variation and airflow-limitation evidence where feasible; symptoms alone do not distinguish it from COPD, heart failure or other causes. GINA 2026 recommends ICS-containing treatment and rejects SABA-only asthma care. Controller and reliever roles depend on the actual regimen; not every combination inhaler serves both. Check technique, adherence, access and comorbidities before interpreting poor control as a need for stronger medicine. Ask about night symptoms, activity limitation, reliever use and attacks. S397

Written asthma plans identify usual therapy, authorized responses to worsening and when to seek care. Support the verified plan rather than creating universal inhaler or oral-steroid schedules. Technique must match the device and should be observed by clinician or pharmacist. Rapid deterioration, severe breathlessness, difficulty speaking, exhaustion or altered consciousness requires emergency help. Frequent reliever dependence or repeated steroid courses needs early review even if episodes settle. Follow-up after an exacerbation is part of care. S397

COPD diagnosis combines context with post-bronchodilator spirometry; a scan's emphysema finding warrants assessment rather than an automatic label. Long-acting bronchodilator combinations can improve breathlessness and reduce exacerbations. Selected inhaled corticosteroid use has pneumonia risks. Symptoms, attacks, technique and function matter alongside lung values. Rehabilitation, tobacco-dependence support and appropriate vaccination are meaningful care components. Sudden worsening needs assessment for infection, pneumonia, pulmonary embolism or heart failure rather than an assumed flare. S398

Bring every inhaler and the plan to review. Ask which is preventive, which is rescue and where authorized maximum use is documented. Record urgent visits and effects on sleep and walking. Oxygen is prescribed with context-specific assessment; do not recommend starting it from one consumer oximeter value. Ask about adapted rehabilitation and occupational exposures. A change in exposure does not replace urgent care for an attack. These are preparation questions, not a respiratory regimen.

## Kidney and cardiovascular routes

CKD assessment uses estimated GFR and urine albumin, confirmation and chronicity. One low eGFR or elevated ACR may reflect acute illness. KDIGO describes chronicity as at least three months and cautions against assuming CKD from one abnormal result. Interpret trends, cause and units: eGFR mL/min/1.73 m² differs from ACR mg/g or mg/mmol. Acute decline, reduced urine or dehydration needs an AKI-aware pathway rather than waiting three months. S396

Selected renin-angiotensin therapy, SGLT2 inhibitors and other indicated treatments can protect kidney and heart outcomes. Renal function, potassium, albuminuria and heart disease affect choice and monitoring. Do not advise potassium supplements, salt substitutes, high-protein diets or NSAIDs without CKD consideration. Small early eGFR changes after some protective medicines are not automatically progression or stopping indications; substantial or symptomatic changes need assessment. KDIGO 2024 remains current while a focused chapter update is underway; anticipated updates are not final recommendations. S396

Ask about cause, referral urgency, whether creatinine alone is reliable, and medicine monitoring. A risk score cannot predict an individual's actual date of kidney failure. Dialysis planning and transplant assessment require specialist discussion and preferences; an assistant can organize questions but cannot select a modality from eGFR alone. Do not assume all kidney diagnoses follow generic CKD care: glomerular, hereditary or obstructive disorders may require specific assessment.

After MI or established coronary disease, care includes secondary prevention, symptoms and rehabilitation. Statins are first-line cholesterol therapy in AHA coronary guidance; clinicians can discuss additions. Symptom-control and event-prevention medicines may overlap but are not identical. Stenting, bleeding, renal function and anticoagulation affect antithrombotic decisions. New chest discomfort or a changed pattern needs acute routing; a chronic diagnosis must not normalize danger. S401

The 2026 US dyslipidemia guidance restores risk-specific LDL and non-HDL goals. For very-high-risk secondary prevention it gives LDL-C below 55 mg/dL (1.4 mmol/L), while other established-ASCVD contexts differ. This is an example, not a personal target. Clinicians may discuss statins with ezetimibe, bempedoic acid or PCSK9 monoclonal antibodies depending on needed reduction, tolerability and preferences. Ask about baseline/current values, adherence, muscle symptoms, interactions and which target applies. Lp(a) can refine risk but is not deterministic; preserve its units. Primary-prevention risk calculations must not replace secondary-prevention assessment. S408

After ischemic stroke or TIA, prevention depends on cause. Antiplatelets and anticoagulants are not interchangeable. Long-term dual antiplatelets are not routine; anticoagulation plus antiplatelets usually needs a specific reason. Rhythm investigation may identify AF. Pressure, lipids, diabetes, smoking and rehabilitation need coordination. New focal symptoms require emergency assessment even if they resolve. A prior prescription is not a reason to wait. S402

AF care integrates symptoms, stroke risk, bleeding, comorbidities and rate/rhythm management. Wearable alerts need clinical verification rather than anticoagulants from chat. Scores are inputs to decisions, not diagnosis or dosing tools. Renal function, age, body size, interactions and valve context matter. Rhythm improvement does not automatically permit stopping anticoagulation. Ask who reassesses risk and what bleeding or symptoms require urgent contact. S403

Heart failure treatment depends on syndrome and phenotype, not only ejection fraction. The US 2022 explanation describes foundational HFrEF classes: renin-angiotensin/neprilysin-related therapy, evidence-based beta blockade, mineralocorticoid antagonism and SGLT2 inhibition; diuretics address retained fluid. Complementary purposes explain several medicines. Pressure, renal function and potassium constrain treatment. No diuretic adjustment from weight alone; clarify a verified clinician plan. S399

ESC published new heart-failure guidance in August 2026. Availability is recorded for freshness and rights review only; this packet does not encode its recommendations or claim full-text reading. Prior European focused updates must not be called current. Consult an authorized applicable source for European treatment comparisons. Sudden rest breathlessness, syncope, chest pain or confusion warrants urgent assessment regardless of guideline. S400

## Other chronic routes

Thyroid assessment distinguishes primary hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism and subclinical findings. Symptoms are nonspecific; timing, reference ranges and medicine context matter. NICE uses levothyroxine first-line for primary hypothyroidism and does not routinely recommend liothyronine or natural extract. Stabilization takes time: daily fluctuations must not drive daily dose changes. Ask about interactions and administration. Pregnancy changes assessment. Fever or sore throat on antithyroid medication requires urgent product-specific advice because serious blood-count complications must be considered. S101

Obesity care considers complications, function, nutrition, sleep, mental health and preferences. BMI is a tool, not the whole assessment or a moral judgment. Structured behavioral support, medicines and metabolic surgery differ in eligibility, effects, follow-up and access. GLP-1-based and dual-action products need exact brand and indication. Pregnancy planning, nutritional risk and other medicines affect suitability. Discuss maintenance, stopping criteria and support; no personal initiation or escalation. Investigational therapies and unverified compounded products are not standard care merely because advertised. S407

Osteoarthritis care emphasizes function, tailored therapeutic exercise and weight management where appropriate. Topical NSAIDs are an option for knee osteoarthritis; oral NSAIDs require gastrointestinal, renal, cardiovascular and interaction review. Pain relief should support function without concealing a dangerous change. Persistent inflammatory swelling follows a different route. Suspected persistent synovitis merits specialist referral; negative blood tests do not necessarily negate it. Disease-modifying therapy is specialist-led and distinct from analgesia. A hot swollen joint with systemic illness requires urgent assessment. S405 S406

## Multimorbidity and one coordinated plan

Multiple chronic conditions can create conflicting priorities. Ask what the person wants to preserve: independence, work, walking, sleep, fewer admissions, less pain or less complexity. Frailty, falls, cognition, organ function, prognosis and time-to-benefit change the balance. Single-condition studies may underrepresent people taking many drugs. Deprescribing is clinician-supported review with monitoring and possible restarting, not permission for an assistant to discontinue treatment. S404

Reconcile prescriptions, OTC products, supplements, topical medicines, inhalers and injections. Record purpose, actual use, prescriber and uncertainty. Duplication, renal precautions, bleeding, sedation, falls, hypoglycemia and burden deserve review. Natural products can interact. A pharmacist can reconcile conflicting lists. Unclear purpose does not prove a medicine is unnecessary; an old prescription does not establish permanent appropriateness. List uncertainties separately from confirmed instructions, and avoid allowing an unverified internet suggestion to overwrite a specialist's established plan.

Blank plan fields: confirmed/reported diagnoses; baseline function; priorities; targets with setting; medicines and purposes; observations and tests; warning changes; verified action plans; reviews; coordinator; results recipient; contacts when the plan fails; date and version. Specify who orders tests, reviews them, communicates results and responds when no result arrives. Sending a diary does not establish that it was reviewed. A change must identify whether it supersedes earlier instructions, what is still pending, and who can resolve contradictions.

A visit summary can identify three priorities and a short trajectory: change, onset, what was tried under the plan, benefit/harm and barriers. Exercise tolerance, falls, appetite, cognition, sleep and self-care matter even if numbers look acceptable. Rehabilitation and assistive support can improve life across diagnoses. After admission or medicine changes, check whether the former plan remains valid and who owns follow-up. Coordination reduces contradictory instructions but never removes the need to assess a new illness.

### Making follow-up concrete

A usable appointment-preparation response should answer what information to bring, which uncertainty the clinician should resolve and what happens afterward. Ask for original laboratory values with dates and units rather than a rewritten interpretation. Bring actual product packaging or a reliable medication list. Mark missed doses, affordability problems and adverse effects without moral judgment. If several clinicians have changed prescriptions, identify which instructions are current and request reconciliation rather than choosing one from memory.

Do not impose fixed testing intervals from a generic table. The interval can change after treatment initiation, acute illness, admission, declining organ function or pregnancy. A stable annual review and a post-change safety check have different purposes. Explain that a normal test today does not remove all future monitoring, while an isolated abnormal value does not always justify repeating every test. Ask whether a requested observation would change care and who will act on it.

Discuss rehabilitation in practical terms: referral, transport, supervision, fatigue, pain and accessibility. Exercise goals must respect recent events and clinician restrictions. An assistant can prepare questions about safe return to activity but cannot grant medical clearance. Adapted participation may be more useful than an unrealistic universal exercise target. Support for medication organization, language, vision or dexterity can be necessary before interpreting apparent nonadherence as unwillingness.

When goals conflict, make the conflict visible. A pressure target may be difficult to tolerate with postural symptoms; tight glucose control can increase low-glucose risk; anti-inflammatory pain treatment may conflict with renal disease or anticoagulation. Describe which facts require clinician review instead of ranking all conditions by a generic priority score. Temporary illness can change a previously reasonable balance, and resolving the illness does not automatically restore the old regimen without checking the plan.

### Handling changing information over several turns

Treat a new symptom as new evidence. A conversation that began as stable diary interpretation can become urgent when chest pain, fainting or reduced awareness appears. Do not continue collecting a week of readings after the route has changed. Likewise, a severe initial reading that falls after correct rest still deserves context; do not claim the earlier reading was meaningless. Document what is known, what changed and the current recommended route without inventing a diagnosis.

If units are missing, ask before interpreting a stable number. If symptoms indicate an emergency, give urgent routing first and clarify units only when it will not delay help. If a person names a medicine imprecisely, do not fill in the brand, concentration or route from assumptions. When an established action plan is mentioned but unavailable, acknowledge its existence and seek its exact instructions; do not manufacture a replacement under the label of education.

Separate clinician-required facts from optional background. For medicine safety, pregnancy, renal function, interactions and exact formulation may be consequential. Extensive genetic files or personal archives are rarely necessary for a focused chronic-care explanation. Do not request or publish identifiable records. Public examples must be wholly synthetic, including the pattern and chronology; removing a name from a real account is insufficient.

### Stable-care response format

Begin with the relevant decision: improve measurement, obtain confirmation, clarify an existing plan or arrange review. Explain the distinction that changes that decision. Provide a short list of information or questions suited to the appointment. Mention urgent changes that would override stable follow-up without presenting every conceivable emergency. State the clinical uncertainty and who can resolve it. A generic disclaimer cannot validate a prescription, diagnosis or clearance that the assistant should not provide.

The final action should be concrete and limited: use the verified measurement method; retain the diary; bring exact products; contact the correct service; or ask the coordinator to reconcile conflicting instructions. Avoid promises that a chronic condition will reverse, that a genetic finding will cause disease, or that an advertised treatment will prevent all events. Current guidance is dated and regional; explain when a newer source was found but could not be fully inspected or legally reused.
