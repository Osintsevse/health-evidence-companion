# Diagnostic tests, result reports and focused history
Public educational module. Evidence check: 2026-10-09. This supports understanding and clinician preparation; it does not diagnose, clear an emergency, or prescribe from a result.

## Begin with the decision, not a panel
Ask why the test was obtained: investigation of symptoms, screening in an eligible population, monitoring established disease, treatment safety, or confirmation of an earlier finding. A complete blood count and a metabolic panel answer different questions. The existence of a commercially sold package does not make its entire contents appropriate for an asymptomatic person. An imaging abnormality, a positive screening result and a risk estimate also represent different kinds of evidence. Name that distinction explicitly before explaining any abnormality.

Start with current safety. New chest pressure, severe breathlessness, fainting, new one-sided weakness or speech difficulty, major bleeding, confusion, or a rapidly deteriorating condition needs urgent assessment even when yesterday's results were reassuring. Preserve symptom onset and last-known-well time when neurologic symptoms are described. Do not delay action while obtaining a clearer photograph, completing a history form, converting units or calculating a score. A laboratory's critical-result telephone call or critical alert requires immediate contact with the ordering clinician or emergency service following the laboratory's instruction, even if the person feels well. If the designated clinician cannot be reached promptly, use the local urgent pathway.

Then establish whose report, which specimen and which time. A report date is not a collection date; an amended report is not automatically a new sample. Keep preliminary, final and corrected versions distinguishable. Reference intervals are laboratory and population dependent. A high or low flag is a comparison with an interval, not a diagnosis or a measure of urgency. Being inside that interval cannot exclude a disease when the symptoms or test timing are concerning. S28

## Preserve result identity and uncertainty
For every extracted result retain the exact analyte name, numerical or qualitative text, original unit, specimen, collection time, reporting laboratory, method or assay when supplied, reference interval, decision threshold, flag, and report status. Store provenance to the source page or row. Keep a comparator such as less than, greater than or not detected; do not turn a censored result into an exact measurement. Do not silently normalize a comma decimal or repair an implausible number from OCR. Mark unreadable text, ask for the relevant crop or transcription, and withhold calculations that depend on it.

Discovery aliases help find a term but do not prove identity. Hb can mean hemoglobin; HBsAg is a hepatitis B surface antigen and is unrelated to Hb. Urine albumin, serum albumin and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio are different observations. Total and free hormones are separate tests. The optional multilingual glossary is an index for English, Serbian Latin and Russian terms; it must never merge observations or alter the existing result-identity schema. Transliteration can lose distinctions, so confirm the original label and specimen.

Unit conversion must be analyte specific and transparent. A laboratory glucose mass-to-molar conversion uses glucose's molecular mass; creatinine requires a different factor. Hemoglobin g/dL to g/L is a volume conversion. Preserve the original and label the converted copy. Enzyme activity U/L cannot be converted to a mass concentration using a generic factor. U/L and microkat/L are activity units, but an apparent unit conversion does not repair different assay methods. For HbA1c, percent and mmol/mol represent different standardization systems and need the validated relationship, not a simple percent scaling.

D-dimer fibrinogen-equivalent units (FEU) and D-dimer units (DDU) are not interchangeable labels. Check the assay's documentation, unit magnitude and decision threshold rather than applying a universal doubling shortcut. Cardiac troponin I and T, conventional and high-sensitivity assays, and different manufacturers are not interchangeable. Never average their numbers into a trend. Results from different laboratories may be comparable only after verifying analyte, specimen, assay calibration and clinical context; present a method change visibly. S441 S443

## What the test classes can answer
The following is a navigation map rather than a universal checkup order. Ask a clinician which result would change the next decision before suggesting additional testing.

## Marker-family reference table
Units below are common reporting examples, never reference intervals. Serum/plasma means the laboratory's stated accepted specimen, not permission to substitute one for the other. The table explains why context matters; it cannot serve as a treatment algorithm. Standard clinical terminology is summarized originally, with source anchors for the main interpretive principles. For a less familiar method, retrieve its current laboratory interpretive note.

### Blood cells and coagulation
A CBC describes cells and red-cell indices; it cannot establish the cause of anemia or an infection by itself. Interpret multiple linked observations and the person's circumstances. S102

Coagulation tests have indication and drug-specific roles. Bleeding plus anticoagulant use needs direct clinician assessment, not an INR-based chat adjustment. NICE's PE pathway uses clinical assessment before the selected tests; it does not support indiscriminate D-dimer screening. S443

### Chemistry, kidneys and liver
Kidney assessment combines filtration estimates with urinary damage markers; albuminuria can matter despite a preserved eGFR. S437 Modern race-free equations avoid a race coefficient. Record the equation when the laboratory changes its reporting. Creatinine depends on muscle and some drugs; cystatin C has its own nonfiltration influences. A combined estimate may improve precision where clinically indicated. An acute creatinine change does not establish chronic kidney disease; chronicity and repeat/contextual assessment matter. S438

ALT and AST are injury markers. ALP and GGT help characterize a cholestatic pattern; ALP can originate in bone. Bilirubin concerns processing/excretion; albumin and clotting factors contribute to synthetic-function assessment. Low albumin or prolonged INR have nonliver causes, including protein loss, inflammation, vitamin K issues and anticoagulants. An AST/ALT ratio is a clue, not proof of alcohol exposure. Jaundice with confusion or major bleeding needs urgent assessment. S436

For a reported hemolyzed potassium, explain that damaged cells may contribute potassium to the sample. The ordering team may need prompt repeat sampling and ECG/clinical assessment. It is unsafe to declare the value spurious from chat, especially with a critical flag, renal disease or symptoms. UKKA identifies severe hyperkalemia as potentially life-threatening and requiring urgent hospital assessment; its treatment recommendations are for trained clinical settings. Do not issue insulin, binders, bicarbonate, fluid loading or electrolyte replacement instructions. S442

### Metabolic, endocrine, inflammation and infection
An HbA1c reflects glycemia over previous months, not today's acute state. Red-cell lifespan changes, transfusion and some hemoglobin variants can distort the result; discordant glucose and HbA1c need reconciliation rather than averaging. Diagnostic confirmation and pregnancy-specific pathways differ from ordinary monitoring. S99

Iron studies work as a set; identify why deficiency or overload is being considered before explaining a single result. S439 TSH, thyroid hormones, symptoms and medication context belong together. Ask about biotin and other supplements and use the assay laboratory's instructions; do not invent a universal washout interval or stop prescribed therapy. S440 B12 assessment may need additional clinical/laboratory evidence when borderline, and neurologic symptoms should not be dismissed because a CBC is unremarkable. S456

A troponin elevation indicates myocardial injury in the appropriate laboratory context, not automatically a heart attack. Different assays have different upper reference limits and changes over time are method dependent. A single early low result cannot be offered as home clearance for new chest symptoms. Explain that clinicians combine serial sampling, onset time, ECG and other evidence. Preserve ng/L versus ng/mL exactly; a thousand-fold magnitude difference is consequential. S441

Urine testing can examine appearance, chemical substances, cells and microorganisms; these are complementary rather than interchangeable observations. S451 Ask about urinary symptoms, fever, pregnancy, collection and prior antibiotics before discussing culture. A susceptibility category belongs to the specific organism/drug/method; it is not an instruction to start the listed antibiotic.

Stool investigations are selected from history and examination; a person with diarrhea does not universally need a pathogen panel, parasite test, calprotectin and occult blood. Severe symptoms, blood, systemic illness and duration can change assessment. S452 The WHO semen manual standardizes laboratory work; this packet inspected its publication overview, not all procedural chapters, and supplies no numerical fertility cutoff. Ask for the complete report and collection details. S453

Cytology studies cells; histopathology examines tissue and architecture. Preserve adequacy, the final diagnostic wording, grade, margins, receptor/molecular findings and pending addenda. Do not turn "suspicious" into confirmed malignancy or "negative margins" into certainty that no further care is required. Pathology can establish a diagnosis, but treatment and staging require its clinical setting and often other information. S454 Germline and tumor testing answer different questions. A variant of uncertain significance is not a positive actionable pathogenic result; a negative test can be uninformative without knowing family variant and coverage. Arrange qualified interpretation and consent before family implications are discussed. S455

## Imaging, ECG, echocardiography and function tests
Explain a report's findings and impression while preserving uncertainty terms and recommendations. Record body region, modality, protocol, contrast, technical limitation and prior study actually compared. "No acute abnormality" is narrower than "no disease." Incidental findings need the stated follow-up and relevant risk context, not an invented biopsy recommendation. Ask whether the report is preliminary or finalized and whether an addendum exists. An assistant can discuss terminology but cannot provide image-based medical clearance.

For ECG retain recording date, symptoms at recording, rate/rhythm, clinician interpretation and technical quality. A machine interpretation needs qualified review; a normal resting recording cannot exclude intermittent arrhythmia or all acute coronary syndromes. Echocardiography addresses structure and function; retain measurement method, image limitations and interpreting clinician's conclusion. Ejection fraction is not a complete heart-health score. For spirometry, exercise tests and other functional measurements, preserve protocol, effort/quality, predicted reference model, medication conditions and the actual interpretation. Compare equivalent measurements, not unlike machine summaries. These are conservative report-handling rules; this packet does not implement specialist interpretation criteria.

## A clear report that supports action
Use the following structure for a result explanation. Offer a brief version first, with optional detail. Do not bury urgent action inside a long marker table.

1. **Action and urgency:** State any urgent finding and the action/timeframe. Describe symptoms that independently require emergency care. If no urgent evidence is supplied, say what uncertainty remains rather than asserting safety.
2. **Facts and dates:** List the source-confirmed result, original unit, interval/threshold, collection time and status. Distinguish user report from document evidence.
3. **Interpretation:** Explain the likely question addressed, relevant pattern and what cannot be concluded. Use plain language and avoid an unsupported ranked diagnosis.
4. **Context to resolve:** Ask only the few missing questions that could change the next step; label missing answers unknown.
5. **Next step:** Specify who should review, when, and what verification or clinical decision is needed. Do not prescribe a treatment or an arbitrary test bundle.
6. **Follow-up ownership:** Identify the ordering clinician or service and how the person will confirm that the result was reviewed. A booked repeat test does not complete follow-up until someone reviews it.
7. **Evidence and limitations:** Cite the current relevant guidance, region and important method/population limitations. Recheck a changed guideline before using a treatment threshold.

Synthetic example: "The laboratory has called this potassium critical. Contact the ordering team immediately and follow their urgent instruction; if they cannot be reached promptly, seek urgent assessment. The sample is marked hemolyzed, which can affect potassium, but this does not establish that the result is harmless. Keep the reported value, collection time and note available for the team. They decide whether repeat sampling and cardiac assessment are needed." No hydration regimen or supplement restriction is used as a substitute for assessment. S442

A nonurgent report can say: "ALT is above this laboratory's interval on the stated collection date. ALT reflects liver-cell injury rather than measuring all liver functions. The other liver tests and recent medicines, supplements, exercise and symptoms help determine the next investigation. Arrange review with the ordering clinician on their advised timeframe, and confirm who receives any repeat result." Avoid "your liver is failing," "just detox," and "repeat in three months" without context.

## Focused, voluntary anamnesis
History gathering should be adaptive and proportionate. Explain the purpose, ask consent for sensitive sections, and allow skip, unknown and correction. A host interface may provide a form, but ordinary chat must support the same sequence without implying a built-in data store. Do not request an entire private archive when a short symptom chronology and relevant medicines suffice.

Start with the person's concern and desired decision: understand a report, prepare for a visit, or assess whether a symptom needs urgent help. Ask age band and location only when needed for guidance; pregnancy possibility and relevant anatomy only for the question at hand. Establish onset, course, severity, functional effect, associated symptoms, triggers and relieving factors. Then ask targeted safety questions. Do not treat unasked symptoms as absent. For a new acute event, timing outranks routine preventive detail.

Separate diagnoses confirmed by a clinician, suspected diagnoses, the person's interpretation and unresolved questions. Record prior operations, hospitalizations and relevant chronic conditions with approximate dates and uncertainty. Ask current prescription medicines, actual use, last dose when relevant, OTC products, supplements, allergies and the exact reaction. A prescription from two years ago is not evidence of current use; a medication list is not proof of adherence. Do not mark a drug allergy merely because a predictable side effect was reported.

Family history should specify relationship, side of family, condition and age at diagnosis when known. "Grandparent had cancer" is insufficient for a specialized inherited-risk model. Unknown family details stay unknown; adoption or limited contact may explain missing knowledge. Ask lifestyle and exposure questions neutrally: tobacco, alcohol, work, travel, diet, exercise and sexual exposure only as relevant. Include practical context such as sleep, caregiving, access, language, disability and preferences because these affect feasible follow-up. Do not upload histories into public source queries or examples.

End with a concise readback: current concern and timeline, known findings, medication use, relevant history, unknowns, and agreed next action. Invite corrections. If a later turn adds fever, fainting, pregnancy, a critical alert or a new medication, revise the route instead of maintaining the initial reassurance. Saving this history needs owner authorization and the private-record workflow; offering a form alone does not authorize persistence.

## Risk tools: navigation, eligibility and rights
A validated score estimates an outcome in a defined population over a defined timeframe. It is not a diagnosis, a guarantee, or a substitute for urgent assessment. First confirm the exact tool/version and the intended clinical decision. Check country calibration, age range, excluded conditions, missing data handling, and whether the available website is a clinical implementation or a demonstrator. Never fill unknown inputs with "no," invent a probability, or rebuild a licensed calculator in chat.

| Tool | Appropriate navigation | Limits and required context |
|---|---|---|
| Tyrer-Cuzick / IBIS | Breast-cancer risk discussion and specialist preparation | Exact name/version, pedigree, personal/reproductive history, density if available, local implementation |
| PREVENT-ASCVD / older ASCVD PCE tools | US cardiovascular prevention conversation; verify current algorithm | Eligible age, measured BP/lipids, relevant history, exact algorithm and endpoints |
| QRISK3 | UK-context cardiovascular prevention | UK calibration, input completeness and accredited clinical implementation |
| SCORE2 / SCORE2-OP | European cardiovascular prevention | Region, age/tool choice and clinical eligibility |
| PHQ / GAD | Validated symptom screening and follow-up | Original version/language/timeframe; clinical assessment after a positive result |
| FRAX | Ten-year hip/major osteoporotic fracture risk | Country model, clinical factors, appropriate femoral-neck BMD if used |
| Wells / PERC | Clinician-selected suspected-thrombosis pathway | Clinical assessment and eligible population; no home exclusion of PE |
| STOP-Bang | Navigate suspected sleep-apnea assessment | Original validated tool and appropriate follow-up; not a sleep-study diagnosis |

If a user says a name resembling "Tyrer-Cuzick," offer that as a possible match and request confirmation from the report or link. Do not silently replace their term. The official IBIS site identifies version 8 and describes incorporating density; its downloadable evaluator is explicitly offered for research purposes and commercial use requires a license. Gather both maternal and paternal histories, ages at breast/ovarian cancer and uncertain entries. A relative's age at diagnosis is not their current age. Confirm whether the intended clinical implementation covers the person's previous cancer, sex/gender context, genetic findings and population; do not generalize an unaffected-population model to cancer recurrence. Discuss referral if the pedigree is concerning, rather than producing an unverified number. S444

For US primary-prevention lipid decisions, the 2026 multisociety dyslipidemia guideline now recommends PREVENT-ASCVD rather than treating older Pooled Cohort Equations (PCE) as the default. Its official announcement identifies the relevant population as adults aged 30-79 without known ASCVD or subclinical atherosclerosis and LDL-C 70-189 mg/dL. Confirm the guideline pathway, endpoint (ASCVD versus total cardiovascular disease or heart failure), valid age for each displayed time horizon, actual measured inputs and local implementation before discussing a result. Do not infer a PREVENT result from a PCE percentage, mix thresholds between models, or enter fictional/missing values. This packet inspected the official announcement and calculator landing page; the embedded calculator's detailed field validation and individual outputs were not tested. Navigate to the official tool and clinician review rather than recreating its equations. S408 S457

ASCVD, QRISK and SCORE2 differ in derivation, calibration, inputs and endpoint definitions; matching percentages are not interchangeable treatment thresholds. The ACC site supports navigation to the US tool, while the ESC source describes ten-year fatal and nonfatal cardiovascular risk for Europe. Current local guidance and the person's existing disease determine which prevention pathway applies. S446 S447 The QRISK website expressly labels its demonstrator nonclinical and directs professionals to accredited systems. It forbids using the site as a score web service or for software development/testing without permission. This packet neither calls it nor implements its formula. S445

PHQ and GAD scores can help screening and monitoring but cannot alone establish depression or an anxiety disorder. Use an original validated instrument and validated translation, preserve its timeframe, and arrange assessment rather than diagnosing from a total. Assess distress, impairment and safety directly; a screening answer or low score cannot clear suicide risk. The USPSTF recommendation requires systems for diagnosis, effective treatment and follow-up; insufficient population-screening evidence for suicide risk is not advice to ignore an individual warning sign. This packet reproduces no full questionnaire and claims no translation license. S449

FRAX estimates ten-year fracture probabilities; country calibration and clinical risk inputs matter. Femoral-neck BMD is the relevant optional measurement, not an arbitrary spine score substituted into the field. The current FRAXplus site labels post hoc adjustments beta; do not present those adjustments as a universally established clinical standard. A fracture history or suspected fragility fracture may require assessment independent of a numerical result. S448

PERC belongs only within a clinician's already low-suspicion PE assessment. Wells includes clinical judgments and guides a diagnostic pathway rather than confirming disease. New breathlessness, chest pain, hemoptysis or collapse warrants medical assessment; no lay checklist should exclude PE at home. Pregnancy, children and other populations need appropriate pathways rather than assumptions about applicability. S443 STOP-Bang is navigation only here: the official page was unavailable during this check, so item wording, scoring and license were not verified or reproduced. S450

## Follow-through and research boundaries
Once a result is explained, confirm the practical next step: the responsible service, how to contact it, whether urgent instructions were understood, and who will receive pending tests. A deferred decision should have an explicit reason and a route back if symptoms change. Do not let the presence of multiple mildly flagged results distract from one critical or time-sensitive finding.

Distinguish accepted clinical testing from commercial or investigational claims. A research biomarker, exploratory polygenic score or consumer wearable algorithm may be informative without being validated for diagnosis or population screening. Ask about intended use, regulatory status, independent clinical validation, population performance, downstream consequences and current local implementation. A laboratory's accreditation does not establish the clinical utility of every marketed test. The public packet offers no proprietary questionnaire reproduction, embedded calculators, universal panel recommendations or dosing rules. Missing evidence is a reason to limit the claim, not to convert uncertainty into reassurance.
