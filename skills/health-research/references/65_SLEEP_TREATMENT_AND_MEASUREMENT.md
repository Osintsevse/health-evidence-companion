# Treating adult insomnia and sleep apnea, assessing apps and timing exercise

**Last targeted search: 2026-11-25.** This chapter concerns adults. It covers what improves insomnia symptoms, alertness or breathing, and how each outcome was measured. For a practical sleep diary and shift-work planning see [adult sleep and regularity](58_ADULT_SLEEP_AND_REGULARITY.md). For evaluation of possible apnea see [adult sleep assessment](43_EVIDENCE_SLEEP_AGEING_REPRODUCTIVE.md) and [home and laboratory testing pathways](54_MODELS_STRATEGIES_AND_IRON_MARKERS.md).

## When insomnia and obstructive sleep apnea coexist

Difficulty sleeping despite adequate opportunity can coexist with repeated obstruction of the upper airway during sleep. Ask about witnessed pauses or gasping, sleep opportunity and schedule, difficulty falling or staying asleep, and unintended dozing during the day. Prioritize immediate safety if the person is struggling to stay awake while driving. Stop in a safe place, arrange other transport and seek prompt clinical assessment. Evaluation for apnea can proceed while insomnia care is arranged. [S649, S657, S821, S828]

| Treatment question | Outcome to discuss |
| --- | --- |
| What can positive airway pressure (PAP) improve? | In adults with diagnosed obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) and excessive sleepiness, the American Academy of Sleep Medicine strongly recommends PAP over no treatment. Its recommendations for impaired sleep-related quality of life or coexisting hypertension are conditional. Sleepiness, waking function, blood pressure and breathing events are different outcomes. [S894] |
| Which options can the clinician discuss? | In mild to moderate OSA the 2025 VA/DoD guideline suggests either PAP or a custom titratable mandibular advancement device as a first-line option. Severity, other illnesses, dental suitability, goals and preferences affect the choice. With PAP, arrange early follow-up for mask discomfort, leak, use across the sleep period and residual sleepiness. The guideline suggests education and support in person or remotely. [S657, S894] |
| What treats persistent insomnia? | Multicomponent cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is recommended. A trained clinician can address conditioned wakefulness, unhelpful sleep beliefs and time spent awake in bed. Deliberately shortening time in bed can initially increase sleepiness. Seek clinical adaptation for excessive sleepiness, safety-sensitive duties, bipolar disorder or poorly controlled seizures before starting this component. [S649, S657] |
| Will PAP prevent a heart attack or stroke in someone with few symptoms? | In extended follow-up of 191 minimally symptomatic participants at one MOSAIC trial centre, cardiovascular events were similar enough that the trial could not establish benefit or harm. The hazard ratio for CPAP versus standard care was 0.83 (95% CI 0.46–1.51). Follow-up differed between arms and 46% of those assigned CPAP stopped it. This estimate cannot answer the same question for a very sleepy person or decide whether to continue prescribed treatment. [S895] |

### Does treating insomnia help someone use PAP?

Two randomized trials found improvement in insomnia but different results for PAP use. In an Australian study of 145 adults with insomnia and at least moderate OSA, four sessions of CBT-I before starting continuous PAP increased average use by 61 minutes per night (95% CI 9–113) by six months relative to usual care. Daytime impairment showed no detectable between-group difference at six months. In the US MATRICS trial, 121 adults were assigned to CBT-I before PAP, CBT-I alongside PAP, or PAP alone. The combined approaches improved insomnia severity and remission, but neither improved the primary measure of PAP adherence over 90 days. The trial detected no difference between sequential and concurrent treatment. Different study populations, comparators and follow-up periods leave the best sequence uncertain. These trials do not establish a reason to postpone clinically indicated apnea treatment. [S896, S897]

At follow-up, ask which problem persists. Time awake in bed, unintentional naps or near misses, and difficulty wearing the mask call for different responses. For someone using PAP, the care team can compare symptoms with device usage and leak data and troubleshoot the interface or reassess breathing. Persistent insomnia can warrant CBT-I even when breathing is adequately treated. [S657, S894, S897]

## Which digital intervention was tested?

- **Clinician-guided CBT-I in people with both disorders.** A Chinese trial randomized 88 adults to six weekly online clinician-guided CBT-I sessions or sleep-hygiene education. At three-month follow-up, mean insomnia severity fell by 9.5 points and 4.0 points respectively. On laboratory polysomnography immediately after treatment, the apnea–hypopnea index (AHI) fell by 6 events/hour with CBT-I and rose by 2 with education. AHI was a secondary outcome. Sleepiness rose transiently during weeks 2–4. The study did not establish sustained control of breathing, capacity to drive safely, or equivalence to PAP. The October 2026 accepted-manuscript abstract was available. [S898]
- **Fully automated CBT-I in adults screened for significant insomnia symptoms.** In Norway, 1,720 self-referred participants were randomized to a structured program or online patient education. At the follow-up termed two years, the estimated between-group difference in insomnia severity was −1.77 points (95% CI −2.65 to −0.90), favoring the program. Only 587 of 1,720 provided data at that follow-up. The difference had diminished over time and depended on assumptions about missing outcomes. The trial excluded people working nights and those screening at risk of apnea or hypersomnia. It cannot establish effectiveness for those groups. [S899]
- **A different program with an active comparison.** A South Korean trial randomized 52 adults with chronic insomnia to a culturally adapted CBT-I app or a patient-education app for six weeks. Both groups improved and the trial detected no between-group difference in its primary insomnia-severity outcome. Some secondary outcomes favored CBT-I. Only the CBT-I group was followed at three months. The small sample leaves uncertainty about the size of any difference. [S900]

When considering a program, ask for the trial of that program or version, the population and comparator, the components it actually delivers, how a diary informs treatment, who monitors daytime sleepiness, and whom to contact if symptoms worsen. A clinician-guided course, a fully automated structured course and an app that displays a score or plays sounds perform different tasks. Gains in one trial cannot be assigned to another product by name or app-store category. [S657, S898–S900]

### Interpreting a watch alert

In the US, the FDA-cleared Apple Sleep Apnea Notification Feature looks for patterns suggesting moderate to severe apnea in adults at least 18 years old without a prior sleep-apnea diagnosis. An alert is a reason to discuss symptoms and testing. Its absence cannot exclude apnea. Its indicated use does not include diagnosis, treatment or monitoring a person already receiving therapy. This clearance applies to the specified feature and intended use. Other device features and countries require their own verification. Recurrent gasping, witnessed pauses or significant daytime sleepiness merit evaluation regardless of the nightly score. [S657, S901, S902]

## Can someone exercise in the evening?

**The exercise, the person's usual sleep time and their next scheduled wake time matter.** In a 2025 observational study of 14,689 physically active wearable users, later, more strenuous exercise was associated with later sleep onset and shorter sleep. When exercise ended two hours before habitual sleep onset, the model estimated 22 minutes less sleep after maximal versus light strain. Training and bedtime were self-selected. Work or social schedules, caffeine and the wearable algorithm could influence the association. The data do not establish a universal hour after which exercise should stop. [S903]

In a New Zealand randomized crossover study, 28 adults contributed data after an evening of prolonged sitting and after an evening with brief bodyweight activity breaks. The first night after the activity breaks, estimated total sleep time was 27.7 minutes longer (95% CI 2.3–52.4). Participants went to bed at a similar time and woke later. Sleep efficiency showed no detectable improvement, and the difference in sleep measures did not persist across 48 hours. The sleep endpoints were secondary. The study did not compare hard late training with rest or impose an early alarm. [S904]

One 12-week trial in Lahore randomized 150 sedentary adults aged 40–60 with cardiovascular risk factors and a clear morning or evening preference. Exercise scheduled to match that preference improved self-reported sleep quality more than exercise at the opposite time. Sleep quality was a secondary outcome, participants knew when they exercised and there were exclusions after randomization. The study excluded people with intermediate chronotypes, irregular schedules, night work and diagnosed insomnia or OSA. It cannot supply an exercise timetable for these groups. [S905]

A Dutch crossover trial reported in October 2026 directly compared morning and evening coached activity, each for 14 days, in adults aged 60–80 with insomnia symptoms. Among 31 participants with sleep data, the estimated evening-minus-morning difference in insomnia severity was 0.55 points (95% CI −0.80 to 1.91). The study detected no difference in subjective sleep quality. A shift in measured sleep timing can occur without an improvement in insomnia. This short trial does not determine how months of training or exercise immediately before sleep would affect symptoms. [S906]

For someone on a stable schedule who repeatedly lies awake after hard late training, keep the opportunity for sleep and try moving the hardest session earlier or using the late slot for lighter activity. Compare similar workdays in a sleep diary, including wake time and how alert the person felt the next day. An evening walk or brief light activity that fits well need not be removed. For rotating shifts, describe timing relative to the next planned main sleep and the fixed demands of the next shift. The studies above did not establish an optimal cutoff for rotating schedules. [S821, S822, S903–S906]

## Scope of the evidence search

On 2026-11-25 the targeted web search used, among others, the following queries. Primary reports were checked against publisher records, PubMed or Europe PMC when available. Official guidance and device labeling were located separately through VA/DoD, AASM and FDA.

- `2026 co-morbid insomnia obstructive sleep apnea CBT-I positive airway pressure randomized trial adherence`
- `2026 digital cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia randomized trial adults follow-up patient education`
- `2026 evening exercise timing sleep onset randomized trial adults morning afternoon evening chronotype shift workers sleep exercise strain`

Studies were selected to compare different populations, routes of delivery and outcomes. This targeted update does not estimate a pooled treatment effect. Direct evidence remains limited for the best treatment sequence in people who are dangerously sleepy, long-term breathing and safety outcomes after digital CBT-I in COMISA, and exercise timing during rotating night work. Trial-level reading status and rights notes are in the source register. Independent clinical review has not been documented.
