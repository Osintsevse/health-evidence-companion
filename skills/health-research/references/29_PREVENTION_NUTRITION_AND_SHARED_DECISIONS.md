# Prevention, nutrition and shared decisions

Scope: adult general prevention and evidence literacy. Checked 2026-10-08. This is a targeted source review, not a complete prevention guideline or a new systematic review. Sources have different reading depths. Guidance is informational and does not provide a diagnosis, prescription or medical clearance.

## Build a prevention plan from eligibility

Distinguish screening in an asymptomatic person from investigating symptoms, monitoring established disease and surveillance after an abnormal finding. A normal screening result does not resolve new symptoms. Before constructing a checklist, establish age, relevant organs and previous operations, pregnancy potential when relevant, personal and family risks, smoking exposure, immunocompromise, previous screening findings, vaccination documentation and country of care. Ask only for information needed for the decision; do not infer anatomy from gender identity.

For each proposed service, record the jurisdiction, target population, guideline date, strength of recommendation, starting and stopping criteria, interval, test options, benefits, harms and follow-up requirements. Record public or private insurance eligibility separately. A service can be covered without being useful for every person, or clinically appropriate without being covered. The USPSTF A/B index is an entry point for US guidance, not a Serbian or Russian calendar. An A/B-only list omits preference-sensitive, discouraged and uncertain interventions. [S186]

### Four bounded US examples

These examples show how eligibility changes a recommendation. They are not a complete panel and must not be silently transferred to another health system.

| Service and version | Eligible population and interval | Important boundary or harm |
|---|---|---|
| Colorectal cancer, USPSTF 2021 | Asymptomatic average-risk adults: screen ages 45–75; selectively offer at 76–85 after considering health, prior screening and preferences. Options include annual FIT or colonoscopy every 10 years. | Prior cancer, adenomatous polyps, inflammatory bowel disease or a hereditary syndrome needs a different pathway. An abnormal stool test requires colonoscopy; bleeding and perforation risks accompany colonoscopy and increase with age. [S202] |
| Breast cancer, USPSTF 2024 | Biennial mammography ages 40–74 for the guideline population. | High-risk genetic syndromes or previous breast cancer need separate guidance. Evidence is insufficient for routine screening at 75+ and supplemental ultrasound/MRI after a negative mammogram, including dense breasts. Discuss false positives, biopsy, overdiagnosis and radiation. [S203] |
| Lung cancer, USPSTF 2021 | Annual low-dose CT ages 50–80 with at least 20 pack-years, currently smoking or having quit within 15 years. | Stop after 15 years without smoking or when health substantially limits life expectancy or ability/willingness for curative surgery. False positives, invasive procedures, incidental findings, overdiagnosis and radiation are possible. Screening does not replace cessation support. [S204] |
| Hypertension, USPSTF 2021 | Screen adults 18+ without known hypertension; suggested annual screening at 40+ or increased risk, and every 3–5 years at 18–39 with prior normal pressure and no increased risk. | Confirm outside the clinic before routine treatment decisions, using validated measurement. A single reading can misclassify white-coat or masked hypertension. This routine pathway is not guidance for acute symptoms or extreme readings. [S205] |

“Insufficient evidence” is not a recommendation against every use; it means the assessed benefit–harm balance is unresolved. Likewise, a screening interval is not a waiting period for investigating symptoms. Shared decisions need access to follow-up, not merely the initial test.

### Vaccination and country versions

For vaccination, retrieve the current national schedule, catch-up rules, previous dose evidence, contraindications and risk-specific recommendations. Travel, occupational exposure, pregnancy and immunocompromise can change the appropriate pathway. Distinguish routinely recommended, risk-based, mandatory and publicly funded vaccination; these categories are not interchangeable.

Batut published Serbia's mandatory and recommended immunization instruction for 2026, with a landing-page update dated 9 February 2026. The document was opened, but its complete vaccine tables were not appraised in this targeted review. It is a verified current entry point, not a validated schedule reproduced here. Russian 2026 amendments were not verified; do not label a remembered or older calendar current. Check the exact current order and local implementation before giving dose or interval advice. [S107]

## Broad check-ups and low-value care

More abnormalities detected does not establish better health outcomes. Before a test, ask: What decision will change? What happens after positive, negative or uncertain results? Can follow-up improve outcomes? What burdens and harms might follow?

The 2019 Cochrane review of multi-disease health checks in unselected adults included 17 trials, 15 reporting outcome data (251,891 participants). Eleven trials assessing total mortality found RR 1.00 (95% CI 0.97–1.03), with high-certainty evidence of little or no effect. Its search ended 31 January 2018. This result concerns broad invitations to health checks; it does not invalidate symptom assessment, management of known risks or evidence-supported individual screening. Geriatric trials were excluded. [S187]

Assess treatment claims by ingredient/formulation, indication, comparator, meaningful outcome, follow-up, uncertainty and harm. Keep “not adequately studied,” “uncertain benefit,” “little/no benefit demonstrated” and “recommended against” separate. Publication count, mechanism, marketing language or regulatory presence alone cannot establish benefit. Manufacturer funding is a reason to inspect bias and conflicts, not an automatic reason to discard a study.

Homeopathy is not a substitute for vaccination or established treatment. Products labelled homeopathic can contain active substances or alcohol and cause adverse effects or interactions. [S114] Update evidence beyond the 2015 Australian report: the Natural Therapies Review 2024, published in 2025, reports little/no benefit for most priority outcomes with moderate-to-very-low certainty overall. It preserves low-certainty positive signals against placebo for allergic-rhinitis medication use and atopic-dermatitis severity. Outcomes versus no intervention must not be conflated with effects beyond placebo. This targeted reading covered the report summary, not every trial or appendix. These signals do not justify a general endorsement or substitution for effective care. [S193]

## Healthy ageing and longevity evidence

“Longevity” claims need a defined endpoint. Better strength, symptoms, cardiovascular outcomes or function may matter without proving longer survival. A changed biomarker, biological-age score, animal lifespan or plausible mechanism does not establish longer human life. Evaluate the intervention, dose, population, comparator, follow-up, harms and applicability before claiming benefit. No anti-ageing drug or supplement regimen is prescribed here.

### Food patterns and nutrition

WHO guidance supports dietary variety, vegetables, fruit, pulses and whole grains, while limiting excess salt, free sugars and unhealthy fats. Adapt choices to affordability, culture, allergies, nutritional adequacy and relevant disease. A population food target is not a treatment for malnutrition, an eating disorder or a condition requiring specialized nutrition. Avoid attributing a whole dietary pattern's effects to a single food or supplement. [S188]

A 2023 randomized-trial network meta-analysis compared structured dietary programmes in people at increased cardiovascular risk. Mediterranean and low-fat programmes showed moderate-certainty reductions in all-cause mortality relative to minimal intervention; Mediterranean programmes also had evidence of reduced stroke and non-fatal myocardial infarction. For mortality, reported odds ratios were 0.72 (95% CI 0.56–0.92) and 0.84 (0.74–0.95), respectively. Follow-up varied from 0.75 to 17 years. These are odds ratios, not percentages of life added. The review did not find convincing evidence that Mediterranean programmes were superior to low-fat programmes for mortality or non-fatal infarction. [S189]

This is evidence about programmes in a higher-risk population, potentially including additional support, rather than isolated nutrients or a guaranteed effect in healthy young adults. Baseline risk changes absolute benefit. Full-text access failed during this check; abstract/results/discussion excerpts were read, so a complete risk-of-bias and supplement appraisal remains pending. Do not turn this limited reading into a personalized diet prescription.

### Activity, strength and sedentary time

WHO's adult population goals are 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity or 75–150 minutes vigorous activity weekly, plus muscle strengthening on at least two days. Older-adult guidance also addresses balance and functional activity. These goals require adaptation for ability, disability, pregnancy and clinical limitations; they are not sports or medical clearance. Some activity can be valuable before a person reaches a population target. [S190]

A 2019 harmonized review associated greater accelerometer-measured activity, at any intensity, and less sedentary time with lower all-cause mortality in middle-aged and older adults. The dose-response was nonlinear. Observational data can retain confounding and reverse causation, and a device-measured association is not a randomized test of a training prescription. Do not promise a particular lifespan increase or infer that unlimited training is better. Only the abstract/conclusion was accessible during this check. [S191]

### Sleep

Sleep-duration reviews associate both short and long reported sleep with mortality and cardiovascular outcomes. A 2017 dose-response meta-analysis of prospective cohorts searched through December 2016; its abstract was inspected. Observational association cannot determine whether changing sleep duration itself changes mortality. Illness can affect sleep, and self-reported duration introduces measurement error. Do not tell a long sleeper to restrict sleep because a risk curve is U-shaped, or use a wearable sleep score as a diagnosis. Persistent difficulty, daytime impairment or suspected breathing-related sleep problems needs appropriate assessment rather than score optimization. [S192]

### Tobacco and alcohol

Smoking cessation is a central prevention topic. USPSTF recommends asking adults about tobacco use and offering effective behavioral support; for nonpregnant adults who use tobacco, it also recommends approved cessation pharmacotherapy. Pregnancy has a separate evidence and treatment pathway. Its evidence synthesis found effective counseling approaches across individual, group, telephone and other formats, but most evidence concerned cigarette cessation. Explain options and connect the person with appropriate clinical or cessation support without inventing a drug regimen. [S206]

WHO states that alcohol consumption is not risk-free: risk varies with amount, pattern, health and context. Do not recommend starting alcohol for cardiovascular protection or treat an observational association among moderate drinkers as proof of net benefit. Discuss reducing exposure and avoiding intoxication-related harms. A person with possible dependence or prior withdrawal needs clinical support for changes rather than generic abrupt-stop instructions. The WHO fact sheet is public-health guidance, not a complete individualized withdrawal assessment or a comparison of every national limit. [S207]

## Prepare a visit and communicate risk

A private visit agenda can contain the main concern, relevant chronology, impact on function, medicines/supplements actually used, allergies or adverse reactions, previous results and up to three decisions the person wants help making. Distinguish the person's account from a confirmed diagnosis. Bring relevant originals or accurate summaries, not an indiscriminate list of every possible disease.

Useful questions are: What are the leading explanations? What would this test change? What are the alternatives, including observation where appropriate? Which benefits and harms matter for someone like me? What result requires follow-up, who arranges it and when should I ask again? Finish with the agreed next step and symptoms or changes that warrant earlier contact.

NICE advises absolute risks, natural frequencies, consistent denominators and explicit time periods. Show both benefit and harm, and explain uncertainty. A relative effect without a baseline can sound much larger or smaller than its practical importance. [S194]

Entirely synthetic arithmetic example: reducing an event from 4 in 100 to 3 in 100 over five years is an absolute reduction of one percentage point and a relative reduction of 25%. The corresponding NNT is 100 over five years only if those comparable risks validly represent the effect and apply to the population. Do not invent baseline risk, treat an odds ratio as a risk ratio, or convert a hazard ratio directly into an NNT. State when no useful absolute estimate is available.

Decision aids and model outputs do not choose for the person. Elicit what they value, check comprehension in their own words and record unresolved questions. Different preferences can support different choices when benefits and burdens are close.

## Honest insurance and authorization support

Organize accurate information; do not guarantee coverage. Establish the insurer, public/private scheme, country, provider network, proposed service, referral/preauthorization rules, cost sharing, exclusions and appeal route from the current policy or official guidance.

A request may summarize the clinician-documented indication, proposed service and supporting records, and ask what additional documentation or covered alternatives exist. Never invent symptoms, severity, diagnoses, failed therapies, dates or clinician endorsement. Codes must reflect documentation rather than whichever code is more likely to be paid. Keep a denial's actual reason separate from a hypothesis about why it occurred.

RFZO distinguishes care during temporary foreign stays from planned referral abroad. Its published travel guidance concerns emergency care under specified conditions and prior documentation; it is not a promise of unrestricted international coverage. Read the applicable agreement and current procedure before advising a traveler. [S195, S196]

Moscow's OMS fund describes escalation from a medical organization to the insurer or insurance representative and then the territorial fund. This source is Moscow-specific and cannot establish rules for all Russian regions, voluntary private insurance or overseas treatment. [S197] For international private insurance, examine the actual plan: preauthorization, a guarantee of payment and final reimbursement are different statuses. Do not delay urgent care while optimizing paperwork. Sharing policy identifiers or health records requires owner authorization and a suitable private recipient.

## Reading limits and maintenance

The source ledger distinguishes fully accessible official sections from search-rendered excerpts and access failures. “Checked” records a date, not proof that every cited trial was read. This module does not supply a universal annual laboratory panel, a fully audited national vaccine calendar, individualized nutritional treatment or an insurance entitlement determination.

Recheck consequential recommendations when age, risk, country, pregnancy, clinical status or guideline version changes. Public contributions contain only general knowledge and blank templates. Personal prevention schedules, visit notes, policies and results belong in a separately authorized private archive.

## Serbian organized-screening example

Batut's published cervical screening page describes an organized programme for ages 25-64, with Pap testing in two consecutive years and then every three years after two normal results. This is a published programme description, not confirmation that every local clinic currently uses the same pathway or that an individual is eligible/funded. The page gives no clear revision date; verify current local implementation, previous abnormalities, relevant anatomy and risk before applying an interval. New bleeding or other symptoms require diagnostic assessment independently of screening. [S213]
