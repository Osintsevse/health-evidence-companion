# Longevity, prevention and radical life extension

Evidence cut-off: 2026-10-18. General adult educational guidance; not an individual prescription, prognostic calculator, formal clinical guideline or clinical validation. This packet is source-checked in bounded sections and awaits substantive maintainer/clinical review. Read actual source scopes in the ledger; a checked date is not a full-text claim.

## Start with the outcome

Ask whether the person means fewer premature deaths, more years with preserved function, a longer maximum lifespan, a better biomarker, or a future technology. These are different endpoints. No drug or supplement in the reviewed human geroscience packet establishes radical lifespan extension in healthy humans. [medical:S778, medical:S779, medical:S677] This bounded finding is not a proof that future interventions cannot work.

Read [prevention](longevity/10_PREVENTION_EVIDENCE.md), [human geroscience](longevity/20_GEROSCIENCE_EVIDENCE.md) or [radical extension](longevity/30_RADICAL_EXTENSION_EVIDENCE.md) as relevant. Numerical records are in [statistics](longevity/statistics.json); reading/rights/review limits are in the [evidence ledger](longevity/evidence-ledger.json). ## Practical prevention discussion

Prioritize actual risks and the person's goals. Establish age, country, tobacco exposure, relevant conditions and medicines only when they change the recommendation; do not collect a full history merely to discuss research. Do not infer health status from the desire for longevity.

- For tobacco use, offer a concrete cessation conversation: readiness, triggers, prior attempts, behavioral support, locally appropriate evidence-based treatment options and follow-up. A lapse calls for reassessment rather than a moral verdict. Pregnancy and adolescents need separate guidance. [medical:S768]
- Discuss regular aerobic activity, strength and reduced sedentary time with ability/clinical adaptation. Preserving strength and function matters even without proof of a particular number of additional years. [medical:S769, medical:S770]
- Use adequate, varied food patterns and address obesity or malnutrition when present. Do not prescribe severe calorie/protein restriction to a healthy person from animal lifespan experiments. [medical:S188, medical:S784]
- Identify and treat hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes and other established risks using applicable guidance and shared decisions. Medication benefit depends on indication and baseline risk, not an anti-ageing label. [medical:S771, medical:S773]
- Support sufficient sleep opportunity and assess persistent insomnia, breathing symptoms or daytime impairment. Observational sleep-duration curves do not prescribe sleep restriction. [medical:S776]
- Avoid recommending alcohol for longevity. Vaccination and cancer screening follow age/risk, relevant anatomy, previous findings and current local guidance; do not export US thresholds as a global schedule. [medical:S207]; see module 29 for local prevention routing.

These actions are supported to different degrees for specific outcomes; the prevention cards state which evidence is observational, randomized or guidance. Do not sum their relative effects or promised life-years. A highly active nonsmoker and a sedentary smoker do not have the same absolute opportunity for benefit.

## Experimental intervention discussion

Explain mechanism, human primary endpoint, population, follow-up, adverse effects and remaining uncertainty before any recommendation. An approved treatment for diabetes, cancer or transplantation does not acquire a healthy-person longevity indication from that approval. Insufficient evidence for off-label longevity use does not justify stopping a medicine prescribed for an established disease.

Do not recommend a self-directed rapamycin, metformin, senolytic, hormone or supplement regimen solely to extend life from these findings. A dose in a trial is an exposure description, not a suggested regimen. For a desired experimental treatment, prepare clinician questions about regulated research, eligibility, meaningful outcomes, monitoring, opportunity cost and alternatives.

Partial cell reprogramming has progressed into early human research. Sponsor-only, uncontrolled short-term results must retain their date and scope. Registry listing, IND clearance, tolerability, a functional signal, an epigenetic-clock change and longer survival are separate claims. A three-person report cannot establish long-term safety or general efficacy. [medical:S786]

## Showing statistics honestly

Every number needs its outcome, comparator, population and time horizon. Keep HR, RR, odds ratio, absolute percentage-point difference, raw event count and model projection distinct. Preserve confidence intervals and adjustment model. Do not turn a hazard ratio into a personal probability or NNT without applicable absolute risks and a compatible horizon.

For cohort associations, consider confounding, reverse causation, measurement error and healthy-user effects. For trials, inspect primary versus exploratory outcomes, analysis population, missingness, crossover, multiplicity and duration. Failure to reject a null is not proof of no possible effect. Funding/disclosures prompt scrutiny rather than automatic rejection.

Use within-study comparisons. Do not rank a smoking cohort, a blood-pressure trial and a supplement biomarker study on one implied common mortality scale. Do not multiply intervention effects to manufacture a personal lifespan estimate. The statistics dataset preserves these boundaries and is not an individual risk calculator.

## Surviving until a future breakthrough

Separate three questions: survival to a specified date; a useful technology existing by then; and the person being eligible and able to access it. Life tables can describe conditional survival under stated population mortality assumptions. They do not estimate the probability or effectiveness of an undiscovered therapy.

Use age-conditional survival rather than life expectancy at birth for an adult. Period tables assume a specified mortality schedule; future cohort experience and individual outcomes can differ. Country, sex, age, risk factors, access to care and future changes matter. Do not fabricate a percentage, use a centenarian population estimate as a personal forecast or promise a date for longevity escape velocity.

Longevity escape velocity is a scenario in which improvements add remaining expected life faster than time passes. The reviewed clinical evidence does not demonstrate it in people. Demographic extrapolation constrains what follows from observed mortality; it cannot prove the impossibility of a new biological intervention. See the radical-extension cards for source-specific limits.
