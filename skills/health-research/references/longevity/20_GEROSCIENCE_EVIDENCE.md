# Experimental geroscience: selected human evidence

Evidence checked through 2026-10-18. These are original, concise evidence summaries, not source-text reproductions. Protocol exposures identify experiments and are not prescribing advice. This selected bibliography is not a systematic review; its studies do not estimate an individual's probability of surviving until future rejuvenation treatments.

Human functional outcomes, disease outcomes, biomarkers and mortality are different endpoints. Animal lifespan findings do not establish human efficacy. Failure to reject a null hypothesis does not prove equivalence. Small, short trials cannot exclude uncommon or delayed harms. The studies below provide no basis for converting a biomarker improvement into years of life gained or recommending self-treatment for longevity.

## medical:S777 — PEARL: rapamycin

**Design/population:** Double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial; adults aged 50–85 with good health or stable chronic disease; 48 weeks. The publication analyzed 114 completers, excluding 11 discontinuers. Compounded weekly rapamycin had two nominal exposure groups; this formulation's measured exposure was substantially below the commercial comparator.

**Endpoint/result:** Primary DXA visceral adiposity was null (partial eta-squared 0.001; p=.942). A secondary female subgroup showed a between-group difference of +6.194 percentage points in lean-tissue-mass change from baseline (95% CI 0.877–11.511; p=.018) for the higher nominal exposure versus placebo. This is not a kilogram increase or proof of longer life.

**Harms/limitations:** Reported adverse events were 117/116/122 and serious events 1/2/3 across higher/lower/placebo groups. Completer analysis, small subgroups, multiple outcomes and formulation uncertainty limit inference; similar counts do not establish long-term safety.

**Funding/disposition:** Lifespan.io crowdfunding and AgelessRx support; all authors were AgelessRx employees/shareholders. Research evidence only; no routine longevity indication established here.

**Source/read scope:** Moel et al., 2025-04-04; methods, results/tables and disclosures read; supplements not fully audited. [Primary article, CC BY 4.0](https://www.aging-us.com/article/206235/text).

## medical:S778 — RAPA-EX-01: rapamycin with exercise

**Design/population:** Double-blind randomized trial; 40 sedentary adults aged 65–85, 20 per arm; 13 weeks. Weekly sirolimus versus placebo, both with the same home exercise program.

**Endpoint/result:** Primary intention-to-treat change in 30-second chair stands: adjusted sirolimus-minus-placebo difference −2.13 repetitions (95% CI −4.61 to 0.34; p=.089). Prespecified complete-case and per-protocol analyses favored placebo, but do not replace the nonsignificant primary analysis.

**Harms:** At least one adverse event occurred in 17/20 participants in each arm. Event totals were 99 versus 63; incidence-rate ratio 1.57 (95% CI 0.86–2.87; p=.14), a numerical difference with substantial uncertainty. One pneumonia serious event was possibly treatment-related.

**Bias/funding/disposition:** Small exploratory trial with short follow-up and multiple secondary analyses; no mortality endpoint. Lifespan.io/VitaDAO crowdfunding through Dr Brad Stanfield Ltd; authors reported no conflicts regarding the studied intervention. Findings do not support adding this regimen to exercise for healthy aging; they cannot settle all rapamycin schedules.

**Source/read scope:** Stanfield et al., 2026-04-15; main methods, results and disclosures read; no participant-data reanalysis. [Primary article, explicit CC BY 4.0](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13082878/).

## medical:S779 — DPP/DPPOS: metformin and mortality

**Design/population:** Long-term follow-up of 3,234 adults at high diabetes risk randomized to metformin, lifestyle intervention or placebo. Median follow-up 21 years; 453 deaths. After 2001, metformin was unmasked, the original placebo/lifestyle interventions stopped, and modified lifestyle support was offered to everyone.

**Endpoint/result:** All-cause mortality, metformin versus original placebo: HR 0.99 (95% CI 0.79–1.25); 152 versus 143 deaths, rates 7.13 versus 6.59 per 1,000 person-years. Cancer mortality HR 1.04 (0.72–1.52); cardiovascular mortality HR 1.08 (0.70–1.66). Rates are not cumulative risks or an NNT.

**Limits/harms:** Mortality was secondary; crossover, treatment changes and incomplete exposure separation limit interpretation. The confidence interval permits moderate benefit or harm. This mortality paper is not a complete medicine-safety assessment; participants were not an unselected healthy population.

**Funding/disposition:** NIH-led support with industry donations, including Merck medication supply. No longevity benefit demonstrated; appropriate diabetes treatment is a separate indication.

**Source/read scope:** Lee et al., 2021-10-25; main methods, results/tables and disclosures read. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8669534/). ADA educational/nonprofit reuse conditions; original factual synthesis only, no source-text redistribution.

## medical:S780 — TAME: proposal and status, not efficacy evidence

**Design/status:** AFAR's official project page describes planned metformin trials at 14 institutions, involving over 3,000 adults aged 65–79 and six years of trials once launched. The proposed question is whether development or progression of age-related conditions, including cardiovascular disease, cancer and dementia, can be delayed.

**Result/harms:** The accessed page asks for funds to launch and supplies no participant flow, comparative effect, confidence interval, adverse-event results or completed efficacy analysis. A planned trial is not evidence that treatment works. This undated promotional/status page cannot independently establish live recruitment or regulatory approval.

**Funding/disposition:** AFAR is fundraising and coordinating project communication, so it is authoritative about its proposal but not independent efficacy evidence. Describe the study as planned according to the accessed page; recheck a dated registry/update before any statement that it has launched. Do not use TAME to recommend metformin for healthy-person longevity.

**Source/read scope:** [Official AFAR project page](https://www.afar.org/tame-trial), undated; indexed page contents checked on 2026-10-18, including fundraising/status sections. Ordinary website copyright; concise factual status summary only. No trial results read because this page provides none.

## medical:S781 — Dasatinib plus quercetin: bone-metabolism trial

**Design/population:** Open-label phase 2 randomized trial; 60 postmenopausal women, 30 per arm; intermittent dasatinib plus quercetin versus untreated control over 20 weeks. Enrollment selected elevated T-cell p16, a proposed senescence marker.

**Endpoint/result:** Primary CTx bone-resorption change: median −4.1% (IQR −13.2 to 2.6) versus −7.7% (IQR −20.1 to 14.3), p=.611. These intervals describe dispersion, not treatment-effect confidence intervals. Transient secondary bone-formation signals and an exploratory high-p16 subgroup cannot override the negative primary result. Fracture prevention and lifespan were not established.

**Harms/limitations:** Headache affected 53% in the treatment arm; gastrointestinal symptoms occurred. One participant stopped for QTc prolongation. Small size, lack of blinding and subgroup multiplicity constrain both efficacy and safety inference; results do not establish effects of quercetin or fisetin alone.

**Funding/disposition:** NIH funding; several investigators disclosed Mayo Clinic senolytic patents/financial interests. Investigational, not routine longevity self-treatment.

**Source/read scope:** Farr et al., 2024-07-02; main methods/results and disclosures read; supplements not fully audited. [Primary accepted manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11705617/). Publisher exclusive rights; factual synthesis only, no manuscript redistribution or inferred open licence.

## medical:S782 — NMN: sustained NAD augmentation

**Design/population:** Double-blind placebo-controlled randomized trial; 62 healthy Japanese adults aged at least 65; 24 weeks. Analysis included 60 after exclusions; 58 completed follow-up. Oral NMN versus matched placebo.

**Endpoint/result:** Primary pure-tone hearing-level change: median +1.25 dB in both groups, p=.95; no treatment-effect CI reported in the main table. Circulating NAD increased approximately 3.6-fold, but no functional/physiological outcome remained significant after false-discovery-rate correction. A biochemical response does not establish rejuvenation or longer survival.

**Harms/limitations:** Adverse events affected 7/31 versus 6/29. One NMN participant discontinued with elevated liver enzymes; causation was uncertain. Small sample, short follow-up, baseline imbalances, postrandomization exclusions and multiple exploratory outcomes limit interpretation.

**Funding/disposition:** MIRAILAB BIOSCIENCE funded the study and supplied product; employee/leadership authors declared financial interests. No evidence here supporting NMN for life extension; does not establish equivalence across other NAD precursors.

**Source/read scope:** Igarashi et al., 2026-09-25; publisher metadata and accepted article's methods, main tables and disclosures checked; supplements not audited. [Publisher DOI, CC BY 4.0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-026-00482-7); full text read through the licensed author-uploaded article-in-press copy. Numerical/label errors outside the selected endpoint were not adopted.

## medical:S783 — SmartAge: spermidine-rich extract

**Design/population:** Double-blind placebo-controlled trial; 100 adults aged 60–90 with subjective cognitive decline; 12 months. Spermidine-rich wheat-germ extract, containing other polyamines, versus placebo; 89 completed follow-up. Intention-to-treat analysis used multiple imputation.

**Endpoint/result:** Primary mnemonic-discrimination change: between-group difference −0.03 (95% CI −0.11 to 0.05; p=.47). Secondary analyses did not establish benefit; exploratory subgroup findings were not robust to multiplicity. This was not a pure-spermidine trial and did not establish dementia prevention or survival benefit.

**Harms/limitations:** Adverse events numbered 58 versus 71, serious events 7 versus 12, judged unrelated to intervention. Small sample, attrition and a single formulation/exposure limit precision and generalization; no long-term safety guarantee follows.

**Funding/disposition:** German public research support; TLL funded extract development. Investigators disclosed company equity/advisory/executive or patent relationships. Does not support recommending the tested supplement for cognitive preservation or longevity; other exposures remain unproven by this study.

**Source/read scope:** Schwarz et al., 2022-05-26; main methods, results and disclosures read; supplements not fully audited. [Primary JAMA Network Open article, CC BY](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2792725).

## medical:S677 — CALERIE: DNA-methylation aging measures

**Design/population:** Post hoc biomarker analysis of a two-year randomized trial: 220 healthy nonobese adults, men aged 21–50 and premenopausal women 21–47; biomarker sample 197. Targeted calorie restriction versus ad-libitum intake; achieved restriction averaged 11.9%.

**Endpoint/result:** At 24 months, DunedinPACE standardized between-group effect d=−0.25 (95% CI −0.41 to −0.09), corresponding to approximately 2–3% slower change in that marker. PhenoAge d=0.05 (−0.11 to 0.20) and GrimAge d=0.05 (−0.07 to 0.17) did not improve.

**Limits/harms:** Biomarker analysis, incomplete adherence and selected volunteers; no demonstrated mortality reduction or validated conversion to years gained. A projected 10–15% mortality reduction is an extrapolation from separate observational associations, not this trial's observed effect. Safety requires its companion analysis, medical:S784.

**Funding/disposition:** NIH support; investigators disclosed commercially licensed DunedinPACE intellectual property. Interesting mechanistic signal; insufficient basis for prescribing calorie restriction to nonobese people for life extension.

**Source/read scope:** Waziry et al., 2023-02-09; main methods/results/disclosures read; correction concerned affiliation, not these estimates. [Primary article, CC BY 4.0](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10148951/).

## medical:S784 — CALERIE: monitored safety

**Design/population:** Two-year randomized trial safety analysis: 218 healthy nonobese adults, 143 calorie-restriction and 75 ad-libitum controls. This is the same CALERIE trial as medical:S677, not independent replication. Achieved restriction averaged 11.9%, with structured monitoring and dietary support.

**Endpoint/result:** No deaths occurred; the study was too small and short to establish mortality benefit. Overall adverse-event incidence did not differ significantly. Bone mineral density decreased more with restriction at several hip/spine sites; no treatment-effect confidence interval is supplied here.

**Harms:** Three restriction participants permanently stopped for safety: two for persistent hematocrit decreases and one for bone loss. Two others temporarily stopped for excessive bone loss. Hematocrit-surveillance triggers affected 35/143 versus 17/75; these are monitoring triggers, not equivalent to diagnosed anemia. Broad adverse-event subgroup tests were unadjusted for multiplicity.

**Funding/disposition:** NIH/NIA/NIDDK support; no conflicts reported. Results concern closely monitored selected adults and do not establish the safety of severe fasting, nutritional inadequacy or restriction in frail people. Maintain bone and nutritional safeguards in clinical discussion.

**Source/read scope:** Romashkan et al., 2016-03-15; methods, safety results/tables and disclosures read. [Primary article, Creative Commons Attribution licence](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4991370/).

## Interpretation and remaining work

These cards support a cautious distinction between an experimental mechanism and a clinically useful longevity intervention. Neither animal life extension nor improved NAD concentrations or an epigenetic score establishes human life extension. DPPOS is the longest mortality follow-up here, but its population and changing treatment exposure prevent treating it as a definitive healthy-person geroprotector trial.

Remaining work includes complete protocol/supplement audits, independent data checking where feasible, later registry/publication updates and separately reviewed medicine labels for clinical safety. NR, other rapalogs, fisetin, acarbose and additional dietary studies require separate cards before making compound-wide or class-wide conclusions. These summaries need substantive maintainer review before being treated as validated clinical knowledge.
