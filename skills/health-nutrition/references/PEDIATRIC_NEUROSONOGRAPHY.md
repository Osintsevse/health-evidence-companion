# Cranial ultrasound and ventricular enlargement in young children

Checked 2026-11-25. This module supports explanation of written postnatal cranial ultrasound reports and preparation for pediatric review from infancy through the preschool years. It addresses ventricular size, suspected hydrocephalus, and possible raised intracranial pressure (ICP). The child's clinician interprets images, determines the cause, and selects tests or treatment. Use [fontanel and head-growth guidance](FONTANELS.md) for closure, skull shape and measurements.

## Respond to current symptoms first

Seek urgent in-person assessment for a persistently tense or bulging fontanel while the child is calm, new persistent downward gaze, or recurrent unexplained vomiting with neurological changes. Difficulty waking, a seizure, abnormal breathing or rapid neurological deterioration calls for immediate local emergency help. Start that guidance before requesting an image, another head measurement or an old report. S565 S622 S939

The signs depend on age and how quickly the condition changes. In infancy, unusual head-growth acceleration, feeding change, persistent irritability or abnormal eye movements may be relevant. In children who can describe symptoms, clinicians also ask about headache, vision and balance. A change in gait, behavior or acquired skills deserves reassessment. Give weight to current symptoms even when an earlier examination or scan was reassuring. S622 S939

### Suspected shunt malfunction despite a report of stable ventricles

A child with a shunt who develops repeated vomiting and unusual sleepiness needs emergency assessment now. Tell the emergency team about the shunt and follow the treating team's emergency plan without delaying assessment. Call local emergency services for difficulty waking, abnormal breathing or rapid deterioration. An earlier report of stable ventricles describes that examination. A shunt can malfunction with little or no change in ventricular size. In children treated by endoscopic third ventriculostomy, new concerning symptoms also warrant urgent contact using the team's emergency plan. S565 S939

## Separate ventricular size, cerebrospinal fluid circulation and pressure

Ventricular enlargement, also called ventriculomegaly, describes the size of fluid-filled spaces. Hydrocephalus describes a disorder of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) dynamics, assessed from the imaging pattern and clinical findings. ICP is a pressure. A cranial ultrasound width is a measurement of anatomy. Its units and findings describe size or shape, and any assessment of pressure requires separate clinical evidence. S939

Other patterns can include enlargement accompanying reduced brain tissue volume (ex vacuo) or enlargement of spaces around the brain. An extra-axial space is outside brain tissue, and the compartment and imaging pattern matter. Treat labels such as enlarged subarachnoid spaces or BESS as specialist interpretations that need supporting findings. Preserve the radiologist's wording and the visible uncertainty. A prior injury, illness or birth event alone cannot establish the cause of a later imaging finding. S939 S940 S942

## Compare repeat measurements using the same definitions

Preserve a row for each reported value or qualitative statement. The family can bring reports and original images for side-by-side review by pediatric radiology. A report summary should keep the following fields when available.

| Field | What to retain |
|---|---|
| Date and context | Scan date, age at each scan, indication, and relevant neonatal history. Record postmenstrual age separately when the neonatal service uses it. |
| Structure and value | Exact report label, compartment or horn, side, value and unit. Keep a qualitative description with its original wording. |
| Technique | Acoustic window, imaging plane, anatomical level and caliper landmarks, if reported. Keep available probe, facility and image-quality details, and note restricted visibility or a small acoustic window. |
| Interpretation | The radiologist's dated impression, what was compared, reported limitations, and whether the original images are available. Keep the clinical timeline separate. |

Compare the same structure and side in a compatible plane at the same anatomical level using compatible measurement definitions and caliper placement. A similar label or the same unit does not establish compatibility. For example, ventricular index and anterior horn width use different landmarks, and thalamo-occipital distance describes a separate posterior measurement. A different facility, probe or operator is a reason to check technique. Even the same facility can use different views. S939 S940 S943

If key details are missing or the measurements differ in definition, present them as separate observations and describe the trend as uncertain. Preserve observations about the same side, view and measurement if those comparisons remain valid. A reported qualifier such as mild or moderate should stay tied to its report and method. An apparent small change may reflect technique or measurement variation. Ask whether the original scans support a change and what finding would alter the management plan. S939 S940 S943

### Keep reference populations separate

Fandak and colleagues measured specific CSF spaces through the anterior fontanel in 359 neurologically healthy term-born children aged 1 to 599 days. The median age was 46 days. The study used specified coronal landmarks and high-resolution linear ultrasound. Two readers assessed the acquired images. This supports attention to technique and age. Separate-scan repeatability and a minimum meaningful change were outside the study, and older ages were sparsely represented. S940

Prenatal atrial-width classification is specific to fetal imaging. Studies of posthemorrhagic ventricular dilatation in preterm neonates use their own structures, postmenstrual-age references and intervention pathways. For a postnatal report in a term infant or toddler, use reference data matched to the child's age and actual technique. Omit pressure conversions from imaging dimensions and defer decisions about further testing or intervention to the clinical team. S939 S943 S944

## Interpret the eye examination in context

Papilledema means optic disc swelling due to raised ICP. Other causes of a swollen-appearing disc require a specialist distinction. The finding can emerge after a delay or be absent despite raised ICP, especially in younger children. Disc appearance and the absence of swelling cannot quantify or exclude raised ICP. Keep the date, examiner's conclusion and whether the original report or only a recollection is available. S939 S941

Transorbital optic nerve sheath measurements and Doppler indices are indirect adjuncts used in some specialist settings. Their interpretation depends on age, technique, setting and reference method. An open fontanel and practical measurement difficulties can affect interpretation in young children. Evidence from critical care or older children cannot supply a home or toddler cutoff. A prospective pediatric intensive-care study after severe acute brain injury found little within-child correlation between optic nerve sheath diameter and invasively measured ICP in that population. S939 S945

## Put follow-up decisions in clinical context

For a well child with a finding on a report, ask who will review the original images and dated head circumference on the same age-appropriate chart. Include developmental trajectory, current symptoms, neurological and eye findings, family head-size history if relevant, and any existing care plan. Check the method and date of an unexpected circumference measurement. A larger family head size can inform the assessment. Its relevance depends on the child's trajectory and examination. A retrospective study of children already referred for imaging found associations between clinical findings, such as developmental delay or a bulging fontanel, and neurosurgical concern. That selected sample cannot predict an individual outcome. S616 S634 S939 S942 S946

Stable dimensions on comparable studies can inform a clinician's assessment together with growth and development. Worsening symptoms or verified acceleration in head growth warrant reassessment even after a previously stable scan. The clinical team can decide if observation with a clear follow-up plan, review by pediatric radiology, or pediatric neurology/neurosurgery assessment would answer the question. Ask which changes mean earlier contact. S939 S942

The modality depends on the question, acoustic window, urgency and local resources. Ultrasound can assess ventricular size through a suitable window. MRI can clarify anatomy when ultrasound is limited or the question requires more detail. A rapid MRI may answer a focused size question, particularly in known hydrocephalus, where available. Its suitability for a first assessment depends on the protocol and clinical question. Emergency teams may use CT when the clinical situation and availability warrant it. S939 S942
