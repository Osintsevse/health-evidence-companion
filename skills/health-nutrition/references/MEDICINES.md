# Medicines, classes and treatment decisions

Educational navigation atlas, checked 2026-10-09. This is an original foundational map, not an exhaustive formulary, complete contraindication list or individual prescription. Differences between ingredients, indications, formulations and markets matter. Existing clinician-prescribed emergency plans have a distinct pathway from independently initiating, stopping, substituting or titrating treatment.

## Identity, action and evidence

Identify INN, every active ingredient, brand, country, strength/concentration, form, release type and route. A brand can denote different combinations across countries. Liquid millilitres are not milligrams; metered and delivered inhaler amounts may differ. An injection, topical product and tablet with the same ingredient can have different approved uses and hazards. Preserve unknown identity instead of guessing from an unclear spoken name.

WHO ATC provides an anatomical, therapeutic and chemical hierarchy. Defined daily doses measure utilization, not a patient's prescribed dose. ATC membership does not establish equivalent potency, interchangeable indications or benefit. Search INN/class; trademarks and every combination ingredient are not necessarily searchable. WHO Essential Medicines Lists prioritize population needs; they are neither every effective medicine nor universal patient plans. Adult and child lists differ. Approval, national reimbursement, registration and actual pharmacy stock are separate facts. S298 S299

Separate mechanism, pharmacokinetics, outcomes, authorization and guideline position. Blocking a receptor explains biological action, not necessarily better survival or function. A biomarker response may be useful but is not automatically evidence of improved patient outcomes. Conditional recommendations may depend on severity, prior failures, biomarkers, contraindications, affordability and preferences. Evidence for one indication cannot establish another.

Use the national regulator first: Serbia ALIMS search and current SmPC/PIL; Russia current GRLS product entry and instructions. These are routes, not assertions that any specific product was retrieved or stocked. Failed retrieval requires disclosure and a pharmacist's current leaflet, not silent substitution of an old PDF. EMA covers centrally evaluated medicines; national registers cover others. Drugs@FDA establishes approval histories and approved labels; DailyMed provides submitted in-use labeling and includes products that are not approved medicines. A listing is not approval proof. S12 S300 S301 S302 S303

Read indication, contraindications, warnings, interactions, adverse effects, administration/formulation instructions, monitoring, missed-dose advice and life-stage/organ-function sections. SmPC sections commonly include 4.1, 4.3-4.8 and 5.2; verify document structure. Check revision date and manufacturer. Read guideline population, exceptions and evidence date. A contraindication, monitoring need and theoretical mechanism are different findings.

## Class atlas and source instructions

This 87-row atlas is a foundational navigation synthesis. It names legitimate contexts without endorsing an individual's treatment. Every row requires the exact national label (L); G means current indication-specific guideline, S means specialist protocol. The final column is a retrieval instruction, not a claim that all class labels or guidelines were reviewed. Dedicated inspected examples are cited. Uncited ingredient-level warnings remain review questions to verify before answering a personal product question.

## Exposure and cumulative risk

Absorption depends on route, food, gut conditions and binding. Distribution varies across tissues and physiology. Metabolism may activate prodrugs or generate inactive/toxic metabolites. Excretion includes renal and biliary pathways. Half-life is not duration of benefit, time to response or interaction recovery. Modified release, enteric coatings, depot injections and patches change exposure. Do not crush, split, dissolve or switch without product-specific verification. A score line is not sufficient evidence. Topical/inhaled medicines can have systemic effects; eye drops and combined local/systemic steroids matter. Infusion compatibility is distinct from whether two oral medicines can be prescribed together.

Review prescribed, as-needed, OTC, herbal, supplement, alcohol, nicotine/smoking and recently stopped exposures. CYP/transporter inhibition or induction can alter concentrations, and induction changes can persist. Prodrug activation can fall under enzyme inhibition. Separating tablets by two hours does not generally resolve metabolic or additive effects. FDA's table is examples, not every mechanism or substance. S69

Scan cumulative sedation/respiratory depression, anticholinergic effects, bleeding, QT effects, hypotension, potassium disturbance, kidney injury, serotonin effects and immunosuppression. Several contributors can become important together. Infection, dehydration, surgery, electrolyte loss or altered clearance changes risk. Intentional combinations may be appropriate with supervision; a flag is not an automatic stopping instruction.

Renal interpretation requires the drug's specified measure, units, date and clinical context; eGFR and creatinine clearance are not universally interchangeable. Acute kidney injury can invalidate assumptions based on a stable prior estimate. Liver function is more than one enzyme result. Frailty, low body mass, falls, cognition and swallowing matter. Pregnancy, breastfeeding, childhood and older age require separate population/product checks. Do not scale adult doses to children or calculate a renal regimen from chat. S97

Herbal products vary by species, extract, strength and contamination. They are not all homeopathy. Natural origin does not prevent interaction. Include mineral supplements, workout products and high-dose vitamins. Verify the indication before presenting supplementation as treatment. No database match, inaccessible label or unknown composition means insufficient information, not clearance. S310

## Structured review, errors and reactions

Reconciliation establishes prescribed versus actual use and discrepancies. Review asks whether each item remains indicated, effective, tolerable, practical and aligned with goals. Ask about benefit, burden and barriers: cost, adverse effects, technique, confusing instructions or beliefs. Use a table with exact product, indication, intended instructions, actual reported use, benefit/burden, unanswered question and responsible clinician. Separate prescription facts from recollection and do not infer that an undated item is currently taken. S308

Check identity and relevant exposures, duplication, contraindications, interactions/cumulative risk, monitoring and indication-specific evidence. Classify conclusions as exact-label contraindication; professional review/monitoring needed; permitted under stated conditions; no important interaction found in consulted sources; or insufficient data. The latter two differ. An incomplete list cannot establish a whole regimen's safety.

Missed doses require exact product, prescribed schedule, last confirmed use, delay, next scheduled dose and number missed. Use leaflet or treating-team plan. Do not automatically double, reload or copy another formulation's instructions. Insulin, antiseizure/Parkinson medicines, anticoagulants, transplant therapy and contraception can be time-critical; interruption may need specialist reassessment. SPS separates high-risk exceptions from general occasional-missed-dose advice. S309

Extra or uncertain duplicate doses are potential poisoning questions. Establish ingredient, amount/concentration, timing and relevant life stage/symptoms, while obtaining poison-service or emergency advice promptly. Do not wait for symptoms when harm can be delayed, induce vomiting or calculate reassurance from incomplete history. Collapse, seizure, breathing difficulty, marked confusion or severe bleeding requires emergency response. Packaging and a concise timeline help; images are optional and must not delay care.

Cold/flu products can duplicate paracetamol, antihistamines, decongestants or cough suppressants. Different brands do not establish different ingredients. Two NSAIDs can duplicate toxicity without identical ingredients. Methotrexate errors are particularly important: inflammatory regimens commonly use weekly administration, while oncology schedules differ. Unintended daily administration needs urgent professional/poison advice, not merely changing the next dose. S317

Adverse effect does not automatically mean immune allergy. Describe severity, timing, dose/exposure history, route, indication, formulation and other explanations. Isolated nausea differs from airway swelling, wheeze, collapse or severe blistering/mucosal rash. Keep suspected allergy, intolerance and specialist-confirmed diagnoses distinct. Do not erase a label, diagnose from a photograph or recommend home rechallenge. Severe reactions require help before reconstructing records. S94

An already prescribed emergency plan allows support identifying its device and existing clear instructions, alongside emergency help when needed. Examples include adrenaline, asthma, seizure and adrenal plans. Do not invent rescue prescriptions or convert maintenance therapy to rescue use. Serious toxicity or dangerous error follows urgent professional guidance; scope boundaries must not force continuation of a suspected harmful exposure until a routine visit.

All methods need indication-specific evidence. Rehabilitation may begin alongside surgery/medicine rather than after all other options fail. Professionals match function and context to goals. Psychotherapy does not imply imaginary symptoms. For chronic primary pain NICE includes supervised exercise and consideration of trained-provider ACT/CBT; this does not replace investigation or treatment of secondary pain. Cancer care may combine surgery, radiation, drugs, hormone and supportive care, selected by histology, stage, biomarkers, function, organs and goals. Immunotherapy is not generic immune boosting. Prepare questions: What is the working diagnosis and goal? What happens with observation? Which options fit this population and why this one? What meaningful benefit and timescale should I expect? Who handles missed doses, acute illness, procedures or pregnancy? What home support does recovery need?

## Evidence status and new treatments

Use explicit dispositions: supported for a defined indication; conditional support; recommendation against that use; adequate benefit not established; appraisal pending. State population, comparator and outcome. Absence of evidence differs from convincing evidence of little/no useful benefit. Popularity, foreign origin, registration, mechanism, indexing, a positive abstract or trial registration cannot settle efficacy. Preserve uncertainty and referral needs.

Homeopathy is distinct from herbs and ordinary low-dose pharmacology. FDA states no homeopathic product is FDA-approved and warns that measurable ingredients or manufacturing problems can cause harm. Do not endorse it as effective or replace indicated treatment/vaccination/emergency care. US regulatory status is separate from an indication-specific evidence assessment. Weak-evidence claims need current guideline/review appraisal; avoid permanent brand blacklists or automatic withdrawal of prescribed treatment. S112

For breakthroughs confirm regulator/jurisdiction, product, approved indication/population, date and conditions. Inspect trials, meaningful outcomes, current guidance, national access and safety updates. Conditional/accelerated approval may retain confirmatory obligations. A committee opinion, trial authorization, research recruitment, expanded access and marketed approval are different states. Trials offer research participation under eligibility, consent and oversight, not assured benefit or established treatment. Preserve exact status/date rather than describing pending studies as approved care. S301 S300

## Practical interpretation examples and professional decision facts

For a hypertension appointment, preparation may include validated repeated/home measurements, measurement technique, dizziness on standing, current medicines and OTC decongestants/NSAIDs, kidney/electrolyte results and pregnancy possibility. These facts help distinguish an unconfirmed reading, adherence problems, adverse effects and a genuine need to adjust treatment. A number alone does not choose a class or authorize a rescue dose.

For diabetes, identify type and diagnostic basis, symptoms, HbA1c/glucose context, kidney/heart disease, hypoglycemia history, eating pattern, frailty, pregnancy and ability to use devices. A high result does not authorize starting insulin through chat. Severe illness or symptoms of metabolic emergency need acute assessment. SGLT2-associated ketoacidosis may occur without striking glucose elevation; the exact label and established sick-day/procedure plan matter.

For pain, establish cause/duration, functional goal, inflammatory versus neuropathic context, alarm signs and prior response. NSAID suitability requires kidney, GI, vascular, pregnancy and co-medication assessment. Opioid decisions weigh meaningful function and harms; do not apply a rigid policy to cancer/palliative settings or abruptly discontinue established therapy. CDC's adult outpatient guideline explicitly has scope exclusions and supports individual clinical judgment. S311

For infection, a prescription review asks what infection was diagnosed, whether cultures were taken, site/severity, allergies, local guidance, formulation, duration, renal function and response. It must not select an antibiotic solely from fever, sputum color or an isolated inflammatory marker. Antibiotics do not treat an ordinary viral cold, but a worsening course may require evaluation for another diagnosis rather than dismissal.

For contraception, timing errors are product-specific and can be urgent. Establish formulation, active versus inactive pills, missed timing, vomiting/diarrhea and interacting enzyme inducers only as relevant, then obtain current leaflet/professional advice. Do not infer a universal missed-pill window or create a new hormone regimen.

For a long-term psychiatric medicine, indication, response, side effects, adherence, recent changes, other serotonergic/sedating exposures and withdrawal history shape professional review. New distress does not prove medication failure; abrupt interruption can itself cause symptoms. A collaborative question about burden is appropriate; an individualized taper or switch generated from incomplete context is not.

For rheumatology or oncology, explain why laboratory monitoring, infection screening, vaccination timing and reproductive planning may precede treatment. The relevant team must clarify hold/restart instructions during illness or procedures. A generic immune-suppression warning cannot establish a personal drug interruption, and absence of obvious symptoms cannot rule out treatment toxicity.

Formulation questions can be urgent practical issues: inability to swallow, feeding-tube use, damaged skin under a patch, device malfunction or a product unexpectedly replaced at dispensing. Record exact formulation and ask pharmacist/team about verified alternatives. Do not improvise crushing, dissolving, injection, inhaler substitutions or equivalence based only on milligrams.

Monitoring questions should link a test to a consequence: what baseline is needed, what changes are expected, which symptoms require contact, when results will be reviewed and who responds. A lab result outside range is not automatically treatment toxicity or a reason to stop. Conversely, some serious reactions need action before scheduled monitoring. Explain that professional assessment integrates exposure timing, alternative causes, severity and the drug's label.

At a transition of care, discrepancies deserve explicit resolution: discontinued versus paused products, new versus duplicated items, changed strengths/routes and unclear course boundaries. A discharge list and what the person actually takes may differ. Do not silently merge them into a definitive regimen. Help produce questions and a reconciled list for the responsible professional; private record persistence needs its own authorization.

The atlas is useful when paired with current retrieval, honest access limitations and clear translation into clinician/pharmacist questions. It must not produce personal prescriptions, convert screening values into diagnoses, or declare a partially checked regimen safe.

For insulin questions, product/concentration/device verification is distinct from injection technique. Establish the exact insulin, concentration, pen/cartridge/vial or pump compatibility, and existing written prescription/plan. Do not infer interchangeability, transfer a pen's units into a syringe, improvise concentration conversion or start a regimen. Technique support should follow the verified device instructions and trained diabetes team's existing plan; unclear instructions, device malfunction or a possible wrong product/concentration needs prompt pharmacist/team advice, and suspected severe hypoglycemia or metabolic deterioration follows emergency guidance. No label-specific critical instruction should be guessed from the class atlas. S303 S309 S305
