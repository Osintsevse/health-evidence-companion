## SLEEP-D2 — A supported home pathway is more than a stand-alone test

Hui and colleagues randomized 316 symptomatic adults at one Hong Kong clinic to home or hospital investigation/CPAP pathways; 86 per arm entered the reported post-test outcome cohort and 62/69 completed three months. Both strategies included nurse support; home failure or a concerning negative result could lead to laboratory testing. These results concern the complete supported pathway and selected analyses, not universal interchangeability of individual tests. [S751]

The reported home-minus-hospital sleepiness-change difference was −1.3 points (95% CI −2.8 to 0.3). Lower scores are better; the upper bound is below the prespecified worse margin of 2 points, consistent with the authors’ narrow noninferiority interpretation. A nonsignificant superiority p-value alone would not establish equivalence. A small QoL advantage was reported, without established meaningfulness here. Baseline imbalance, post-test selection and unclear handling of missing clinical outcomes limit interpretation. [S751]

The 2019 correction replaces adherence cells and clarifies the accepted-treatment/completed-follow-up denominator. Among those completers, average use favored home; one combined nights-and-hours endpoint remained nonsignificant. Preserve favorable results and differing endpoints without treating completers as everyone randomized. Shorter waits and cost findings were tied to local 2013–14 capacity. [S751, S752]

## HAEM-D1 — Adjustment needs an appropriate reference and analysis unit

Ko and colleagues analyzed serial ferritin/inflammation measurements in 52 norovirus-exposed adults: 26 infected and 26 age-matched uninfected were selected. This constructed balance is not an infection-risk estimate. Adjusted values were compared with measured baseline/recovery, their average and modeled trajectories. Declining ferritin in uninfected participants, plausibly related to repeated blood draws, complicated the reference; the modeled comparator assumed shared time patterns. None is an independent diagnostic gold standard. [S753]

BRINDA regression correction yielded lower ferritin and more deficiency classification than correction factors in this setting; correction factors left most observations unchanged. That is a bounded descriptive result, not proof of a universally superior method. Classification proportions counted repeated person-time observations, not independent patient diagnoses. A similar aggregate result can coexist with individual variability. Ask what was measured versus modeled, which denominator was used and whether the population/clinical question matches. [S753]
