# Choose the knowledge needed for the question

This is an informational routing map, not a clinical algorithm or a qualification. Load the smallest relevant module and retrieve current applicable sources for consequential decisions. Do not load the entire library for each question. Stable source identifiers lead to the source register and its actual reading limits.

## First decision

Address a stated emergency before archive work, research or a long interview. Otherwise distinguish: explanation of a mechanism; symptom assessment; prevention in someone without symptoms; management questions about an established condition; interpretation of a report; private import; or general evidence contribution. A screening recommendation does not answer a symptomatic diagnostic question.

Establish age/life stage, country and relevant context only when they change the answer. Adult examples do not transfer to infants, children, pregnancy or lactation. A historical childhood record belonging to an adult is an archive task, not a request for present pediatric treatment.

## Primary care and family medicine habits

Start with the person's main concern and effect on daily function. Reconcile source facts, the person's account and hypotheses separately. Retrieve relevant accepted history before asking again about a previously answered uncertainty. Check temporal relationships and actual medicine use; an old order does not establish present exposure.

Explain a short differential only when useful: what fits, what does not, what is unknown, and what clinical examination or targeted test could change the decision. Do not fabricate probabilities or transform an administrative disease code into proof. For chronic or multiple conditions, identify conflicting recommendations and medicine burden instead of treating each guideline independently. Ask about goals, treatment burden and preferences when the choice is sensitive to them. The full WONCA competency framework is indexed in module 27; these are project workflow requirements, not claimed completion of supervised training.

End with the next step and a reassessment trigger. For a visit, prepare a concise agenda, actual medicine list, relevant prior results and questions about benefit/harm, alternatives, follow-up responsibility and expected result timing. Use module 29 for numerical risk communication and coverage questions.

## Honest coverage

Module 28 supplies selected entry points, not comprehensive specialty textbooks. Cardiology, gastroenterology, nephrology, endocrinology, hematology, oncology, rheumatology, neurology, ENT, infectious disease, surgery, geriatrics, occupational health and palliative care still need condition-specific guidance retrieved for the actual question. Do not pretend that adding their names establishes competence. Modules 09, 13 and 27 record the remaining curriculum. Do not invent diagnostic scores, procedures, drug doses or local referral rules to fill a gap.

If the needed source cannot be read, give the supported general explanation, state the unresolved point and route the decision to appropriate clinical assessment. Source counts and passing software tests are not measures of clinical accuracy.
