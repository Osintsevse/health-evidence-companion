# Ageing, function, rehabilitation and massage

Checked 2026-10-09. This public educational module helps people describe functional problems, recognise changes requiring assessment and prepare shared decisions. It does not diagnose frailty, clear a fracture, authorise exercise after surgery or replace a rehabilitation professional. A person's needs and goals determine the discussion; chronological age alone does not determine what treatment they deserve. The selected urology, reproductive, hair and nail topics also apply outside later life.

## Start with the person's life

Ask what has become difficult and what the person wants to regain: preparing a meal, standing from a chair, walking to a shop, hearing a conversation, returning to work, enjoying intimacy or remaining at home. Establish baseline ability, the timing and speed of change, recent illness or injury, current diagnoses, prescribed restrictions, medicines and available support. An abrupt departure from baseline belongs in an acute assessment pathway before a routine ageing discussion. Do not describe new weakness, repeated falls, weight loss or loss of independence as inevitable ageing.

WHO's current ICOPE publication supports person-centred primary and community care. Its focus includes mobility, cognition, vitality, psychological capacity, vision and hearing together with social and care needs. An assessment can identify areas for further evaluation and coordinated support; it is not a chat-based label or a reason to deny rehabilitation. This packet inspected the publication overview but could not retrieve the full handbook. It therefore does not reproduce its screening workflow or claim to administer it. S504

A useful conversation follows baseline, change, impact, cause assessment, goal and follow-up. Ask whether a person can still shop but avoids it because they fear falling, or whether they physically cannot get out of a chair. Ask how difficulty varies through the day, whether another person has observed it and whether an aid or environmental change helps. Respect that the person may prioritise family participation over a laboratory target. Explore basic self-care and instrumental tasks such as managing medicines, transport, shopping and finances in ordinary language, without copying a questionnaire.

Distinguish inability from lack of opportunity, unfamiliarity, pain, language barriers or missing equipment. A caregiver's report can help with the person's agreement but should not erase their account. Allow time and accessible formats for communication difficulties. Record uncertainty when accounts disagree. Ask whether support is available and acceptable, not simply whether family exists. An apparently small limitation can have a large impact when someone lives alone, lacks transport or must climb stairs to reach essential facilities.

## Frailty, multimorbidity and treatment burden

Frailty concerns vulnerability and reduced reserve, not a synonym for being old. Multimorbidity means coexisting long-term problems and can affect adults of any age. NICE's approach emphasises quality of life, individual priorities, treatment burden, coordination and potential harms. A clinician may consider whether a single-condition treatment still offers worthwhile benefit. Discuss expected benefit, alternatives, adverse effects and preferences; a medicine count alone is not permission to stop treatment. S404

Prepare a complete list including occasional medicines, topicals, inhalers, supplements and actual use. Ask whether tiredness, dizziness, constipation, sleep problems or schedule difficulties developed after a change. These are possible review questions, not proven drug effects. Agree who coordinates the plan, how changes are documented, when effects are reviewed and whom to contact after deterioration. Deprescribing is supervised and may need tapering or monitoring; do not recommend stopping all medicines, withholding anticoagulation or independently changing blood pressure treatment.

Consider pain, sleep, mood, loneliness, housing and transport, and whether family support is wanted and sustainable. Choose a few concrete priorities. Numerous appointments can themselves become burdensome, so ask whether services can coordinate visits and information. Family involvement follows consent and capacity considerations, not assumptions that age removes autonomy. Where capacity is uncertain, refer to the appropriate clinical and legal process instead of deciding it from chat. Distinguish a person's chosen goal from an observer's judgement about what they ought to want.

## Falls and fracture prevention

Ask about each fall's circumstances, injury, loss of consciousness, ability to get up and subsequent changes. Recurrent falls, gait or balance problems and injury warrant cause-oriented assessment. NICE describes domains including mobility, cardiovascular factors, cognition, medicines, vision and environment, and advises against substituting a falls prediction tool for assessment. Do not calculate a reassuring score and conclude that evaluation is unnecessary. S505

Prepare when and where falls happened, activities and symptoms beforehand, medicine changes and environmental hazards. Distinguish a trip from unexplained collapse without treating either description as conclusive. Ask about footwear, aids, nighttime toileting and confidence. Tailored strength/balance programmes and home assessment may be appropriate. Fear of falling can restrict activity and deserves support; staying in bed indefinitely can undermine goals. An aid must fit the person's circumstances and skills, rather than be recommended merely because they have crossed an age threshold. S505

Bone health and falls prevention overlap but address different problems. Osteoporosis may require clinical assessment and treatment despite feeling well. NOGG supports nutrition, individually adapted weight-bearing and strengthening activity, and falls assessment in osteoporosis or fragility fracture. Vertebral fracture or substantial fall risk changes exercise planning; forceful repeated spinal bending deserves caution. Better bone density or fewer falls does not guarantee fewer fractures for every programme. Dietary adequacy and targeted supplementation differ from vitamin megadoses. S507

A fracture after modest injury warrants questions about bone-health assessment and secondary prevention. New severe back pain with fragile bones, inability to bear weight after a fall, deteriorating head injury or concerning fainting needs the injury or emergency pathway. A photo, ability to move a limb or temporarily improved pain cannot clear a fracture remotely. Do not propose forceful manipulation to test whether the bone is sound. Ask about established restrictions while arranging appropriate assessment, but do not let collecting a perfect history delay urgent care.

## Sensory, dental, nutrition and cognition

Apparent memory or participation problems may be compounded by poor hearing, missing glasses, unsuitable dentures, pain, sleep disturbance or difficulty understanding. Slow change and sudden confusion need different routes. Confusion developing over hours or days, especially with illness, neurological changes or inability to remain safe, needs acute assessment through NEURO/EMERGENCY. A routine memory discussion describes pattern and functional impact and prepares an appointment; it does not diagnose dementia from a message or score.

ICOPE's sensory, cognitive and vitality domains encourage looking beyond one symptom. Appetite, food access, chewing, swallowing and ability to prepare meals affect care needs. Appropriate assessment may involve audiology, eye care, dentistry, dietetics or cognition services. The inspected overview supports integrated care; detailed diagnostic or nutritional treatment belongs in its specialty module. An isolated supplement suggestion should not replace exploration of why the person cannot eat or obtain suitable food. S504

Rehabilitation teams consider hearing aids, glasses, dentures, communication aids, nutrition and swallowing. The team must establish safe swallowing adaptations; do not improvise exercises or texture prescriptions after stroke. Repeated coughing, choking or a wet voice during eating should be described for assessment; severe choking or breathing difficulty enters emergency guidance. Dental pain and poorly fitting dentures belong in the plan. Addressing communication and sensory barriers can make the person's preferences easier to understand without assuming they lack capacity. ## Rehabilitation after injury and surgery

Rehabilitation improves meaningful function while respecting healing, symptoms and verified restrictions. NICE traumatic-injury guidance describes multidisciplinary assessment and an individual plan encompassing physical, cognitive and psychological needs. The surgical team documents weight-bearing restrictions and communicates them to rehabilitation staff. A tailored activity programme can include conditioning and task practice, but progression and protective equipment depend on the injury and clinical plan. Age is not a reason to withhold appropriate rehabilitation. S179

Before interpreting a plan, ask for the diagnosis, operation date if relevant, written restrictions, current stage, follow-up and symptom changes. Distinguish an expired restriction from one never reviewed. If someone says both that they may bear weight and that discharge instructions prohibit it, do not settle the conflict yourself. Help contact the treating team and phrase the discrepancy clearly. The number of postoperative weeks or a generic online protocol cannot override their instructions. A friend who recovered from a similar operation is not evidence of the same healing status.

Choose observable daily-life goals: transferring with the prescribed aid, preparing a meal, returning to a work task or walking an agreed route. Discuss pain, fatigue, fear, cognitive difficulty, transport and accessible space. Therapists can adapt tasks and aids and assess progression. Chat can record questions but cannot examine stability, healing, neurovascular status or hardware. Return to sport, driving or heavy work requires the relevant clinical and legal assessment. Describe which restriction needs clarification and who can resolve it rather than saying that a disclaimer makes the proposed activity safe.

New unexpected swelling, escalating severe pain, wound drainage with systemic illness, new numbness or weakness or a previously usable limb becoming unusable warrants clinical contact or urgent assessment according to severity. Do not intensify exercise merely because progress is slow. Scar massage may be clinician-directed after healing; an open or infected wound is not a generic massage target. Unexpected swelling can have medical causes including thrombosis. S179

The rehabilitation plan should be understandable and practical. Ask whether instructions specify the aid, permitted activities, prohibited movements and review date. If they only say 'take it easy', help request concrete clarification instead of inventing limits. Consider whether the person can perform the plan safely in their actual home and has help with tasks they cannot yet manage. A written goal and a means of contacting the service make follow-up more useful than an elaborate exercise list with no verified context. Continued symptoms do not automatically mean rehabilitation has failed.

## Stroke and chronic pain

Specialist stroke rehabilitation covers mobility, self-care, communication, cognition, vision, swallowing, nutrition and continence. Agree goals with the person and review them over time. NICE describes needs-based multidisciplinary rehabilitation with adaptation when proposed intensity is not tolerable or wanted. This service guidance is not a home prescription; do not turn programme targets into unsupported exercises, forced transfers or swallowing practice. New stroke-like symptoms require immediate emergency routing even during recovery from an earlier stroke. S506

Ask about the established team and current plan, not just the date of stroke. A person may need an accessible communication format and additional time to express goals. Family can assist according to consent, but should not speak over every attempt by the person. When fatigue or attendance makes participation difficult, prepare those concerns for the rehabilitation team rather than interpreting them as unwillingness. Support information about agreed goals, equipment and follow-up. Never treat new weakness or a sudden speech change as an expected fluctuation without acute assessment.

For chronic pain, clarify duration, diagnosis, functional impact, prior treatment and changes. Chronic primary pain and pain associated with a recognised condition can coexist. NICE's primary-pain guidance includes supervised exercise adapted to abilities, continued activity and consideration of psychological approaches. Do not transfer its recommendations indiscriminately to every secondary pain condition or imply pain is imaginary. A flare with features different from the usual pattern deserves reassessment. S313

Separate participation goals from expecting complete pain removal. Ask what activities matter, what is sustainable and how benefit or deterioration will be recognised. A practical diary may support discussion without dominating the day. Disease-specific rehabilitation, procedures and medicines belong with the clinician. Do not produce an individual analgesic change, taper or fixed exercise progression. Keep incomplete assessment visible when pain follows injury, includes neurological changes or accompanies systemic illness. Present optional psychological support as one way of improving coping and participation, not proof that a physical complaint lacks a cause.

## Urology, continence and reproductive concerns

Leakage, urgency, frequency, nighttime urination, weak stream and incomplete emptying are symptoms, not a single diagnosis. Obtain onset, pattern, pain, blood, fever, surgery, medicines and impact. Ask respectfully about relevant anatomy and treatment history. A prostate may be present in trans women and non-binary people; male identity alone does not establish anatomy. Symptoms may reflect inflammation, benign enlargement or another cause; benign enlargement does not exclude cancer. NIDDK describes assessment and options from observation to medicines or surgery depending on cause and severity. S518

NICE LUTS guidance separates uncomplicated symptoms from retention, recurrent infection, suspected renal impairment or cancer requiring specialist assessment. Discuss purposes and limitations of tracking or selected tests. A questionnaire is not a diagnosis and its rights must be checked before reproduction. Urinary medicines can have adverse effects and interactions, so request review rather than recommending a product or dose. Do not assume every nighttime toilet visit is prostate-related or advise severe fluid restriction. S515

Ask how symptoms affect sleep, confidence, skin care, mobility and sexual activity. A bladder diary can be discussed as a clinician-selected information aid rather than a diagnostic engine. Someone may report 'leakage' when they also struggle to empty their bladder; do not infer the treatment from that word alone. Review medicine timing with a professional instead of instructing the person to move diuretics or omit prescribed doses. Goals can include fewer urgent interruptions, better sleep or more confidence outside the home, while cause assessment remains necessary.

Incontinence deserves care without shame. Conservative options include supervised pelvic floor training for stress/mixed leakage and bladder training for urgency, following assessment of pattern and contraction. Specialists can help with technique, pain or prolapse; indiscriminate squeezing is not a universal programme. Medicines, pessaries and procedures have different indications and tradeoffs. Prior pelvic surgery, persistent pain, voiding difficulty or neurological features change referral planning. S516

Menopause-related genitourinary symptoms include dryness or discomfort with sexual activity. Discuss moisturisers/lubricants and appropriate local vaginal oestrogen with a clinician, considering individual factors including breast cancer history. Local and systemic treatments are not interchangeable decisions. NICE does not support vaginal laser for these symptoms outside a randomised trial. State research-only status honestly and verify the exact product, market and current guidance before medicine-specific explanations. S517

Sexual function, pelvic discomfort, fertility and reproductive concerns matter at every adult age. New erectile problems can relate to other health problems, medicines, psychological or lifestyle factors; do not assume one explanation. Ask what is troublesome and which outcome is wanted, respecting privacy and consent. Primary care or an appropriate urology, gynaecology or sexual-health service can assess persistent concerns. This packet does not prescribe erectile medicines or assume that ageing removes interest in intimacy. S519

An inability to urinate, especially with painful fullness, needs urgent medical help, not massage or supplements. Sudden severe testicular pain needs emergency assessment because torsion is possible; improvement or a photo cannot safely settle it. Visible blood in urine warrants clinical assessment even without pain; heavy bleeding, clot-related retention or systemic illness requires urgent escalation. Infection, acute pelvic pain and haematuria route to relevant specialty/emergency guidance. Collecting details or discussing benign enlargement should not delay care for retention or acute testicular symptoms. S518 S168

## Hair and nail changes

Hair loss can reflect inherited pattern changes, ageing, illness/stress-related shedding, traction, medicines, inflammatory scalp disease, infection or nutritional/endocrine problems. Timing, distribution, scalp symptoms, recent events and medicine changes guide assessment; appearance alone cannot prove a deficiency. New rapid loss, painful/inflamed areas or persistent unexplained change warrants examination. Do not abruptly stop suspected medicines. Toxic excess of some nutrients can itself cause hair loss. S512

A dermatologist selects tests or biopsy when history and examination suggest a cause; everyone does not need the same panel. Treatment depends on diagnosis, and a product helping one cause may not help another. AAD cautions against biotin, iron or zinc without evidence of deficiency. Do not infer deficiency from a photo, prescribe megadoses or promise supplement-driven regrowth. Ask about existing products and ingredients to prepare safer discussion. S513

Nails change through exposure, injury, infection or skin disease. New/changing dark bands, changes reaching surrounding skin, unexplained deformity or inflammation should be examined; not every dark streak is melanoma. Lengthwise ridges can be common, while sudden new patterns need context. Colour/shape associations are nonspecific and cannot diagnose anaemia or thyroid, liver, kidney or lung disease from a photo. Do not reproduce tables mapping appearance to disease; explain why examination may help and preserve uncertainty. S514

A photo can optionally help describe distribution or document change, but lighting, polish and image quality limit interpretation. Ask when the change started, which nails or scalp areas are affected, whether it is progressing and whether symptoms accompany it. Cosmetic distress is a valid concern even when a finding is harmless. Avoid a large undirected testing package merely because several possible causes exist. If symptoms change during the conversation, revise the plan: a newly widening band should not be treated as equivalent to longstanding unchanged ridges.

## Massage: evidence, suitability and boundaries

Massage can be chosen for comfort or as an adjunct to a defined goal. NCCIH reports weak or limited evidence for several pain indications, often short-term benefits and no consistent superiority of one style. Appropriate precautions may allow supportive massage in some people with cancer; evidence and technique need matching. Temporary relaxation or reduced pain is not proof of disc repair, permanent posture correction or disease cure. Do not promise structural repair or say a prepaid course is necessary to prevent deterioration. S508

Ask the intended benefit, proposed body areas and techniques, how benefit will be judged and what alternatives exist. Check credentials against the relevant jurisdiction's regulator when one exists; a certificate, spa title or social-media following is not universally equivalent to regulated healthcare qualification. Experience with the specific condition matters. Ask about benefits, limitations and cost. A good professional accepts uncertainty, recognises problems needing medical assessment and avoids pressure to buy a long course. A claim of being 'medical' or 'deep' does not establish either competence or greater benefit.

Consent stays active throughout the session. Agree touch, clothing, draping and boundaries before treatment, taking particular care with sensitive areas. The person may ask questions, decline an area, remain appropriately clothed, request a support person and stop or modify treatment. Pain need not be endured for efficacy. CMTO's September 2026 Ontario standard illustrates privacy and ongoing comfort expectations; it does not state every country's law. Sexualised conduct or ignoring a request to stop is unacceptable. S509

Distinguish massage from forceful spinal manipulation. Do not advise forced neck twisting or claim an unverified provider can exclude serious pathology by touch. New neurological symptoms, unusual severe pain or symptoms after treatment need reassessment, not another forceful session. Pressure, area and technique need matching professional advice with increased injury risk. Fragile bones, anticoagulation, sensory loss, cancer treatment or recent surgery should be disclosed; they are not evidence that all gentle supportive touch is permanently prohibited. S508

A newly warm, swollen, painful leg may indicate thrombosis and needs urgent assessment; do not massage it while deciding whether it is tension. Chest pain or breathlessness requires emergency help. Symptoms alone cannot confirm or exclude a clot. Acute injury, unexplained swelling, fever with local inflammation or an open wound changes the plan away from casual massage. A previous label such as sciatica does not make a newly swollen calf safe. S520 S522

If boundaries were crossed, support the person's ability to stop the session and choose whom to contact. A regulator or the provider's professional organisation may have a complaints pathway, but identify the actual jurisdiction before naming one. Do not insist that they confront the provider, return for treatment or obtain a second massage to test the claim. In discussing a disputed session, distinguish what the person reports, what is uncertain and what practical support they want. Clinical symptoms after a session need their own assessment independent of a complaint.

## Percussion and rotating devices

A 2023 systematic review of massage guns suggests possible short-term flexibility or range-of-motion changes, with small studies and substantial bias. Athletic recovery findings do not establish lasting disease benefit, postoperative healing or injury prevention. The abstract and indexed excerpts were inspected; the full PMC page was blocked by a browser challenge. Do not turn this into a universal force, frequency, depth or duration regimen. Percussion evidence cannot automatically transfer to rotating rollers, heated devices or other mechanisms. S510

Identify the exact model, mechanism, attachments and current instructions. Manufacturer safety documents describe limits, not independent proof of efficacy or endorsement. Therabody's current guidance distinguishes precautions and situations needing professional advice, including fractures, clot concerns, altered sensation, recent surgery/injury and anticoagulants. Its percussion warnings include direct application to head, neck and throat and pressure over surgical sites or hardware. Models with added heat or other therapies may differ. S511

In general discussion, do not suggest percussion or rotating devices on the cervical/anterior neck, head, major vessel areas or acute injury. Do not recommend pushing deeper, overriding pain, working on unexplained swelling or improvising a protocol from another device's studies. Stop when use worsens pain or causes numbness, dizziness or other new symptoms and seek appropriate assessment. For postoperative pain or a swollen warm limb, assessment and verification of restrictions come before choosing an attachment. Rotating-device efficacy remains outside this packet's reviewed evidence.

## Closing a useful discussion

End with the next action, why it matters and what changes urgency. For nonurgent problems, prepare a short baseline/change summary, actual medicine list, restrictions and a few questions for the relevant professional. For rehabilitation, identify who confirms progression and reviews goals. For massage, establish benefit, consent boundaries and a stopping rule. Dangerous new developments override elective-care discussion. Avoid reassurance based solely on age, a photo, a score, an old diagnosis or temporary improvement.
