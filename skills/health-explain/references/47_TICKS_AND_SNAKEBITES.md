# Studied questions: tick and snake exposures

Selected study2026-10-17. General education with dated jurisdiction boundaries. In a current emergency, act under the emergency skill and local dispatch without delaying to read this packet. This is not a post-exposure prescribing or antivenom protocol.

## T1 — Act promptly and preserve the conflict in official removal advice

An attached tick calls for prompt action to arrange removal. Official instructions differ: Serbia's Batut page (updated 2022-04-11) advises medical contact within 24 hours and against self-removal; ECDC advises immediate removal; CDC's US page (updated 2026-06-09) explicitly says not to wait for a clinician. These cannot be combined into one universal self-removal or clinician-only rule. Obtain current local health-service advice promptly; do not interpret the discrepancy as permission to leave a tick attached indefinitely. [S708; S653; S652]

Do not apply heat, oil or chemicals to provoke detachment. Record likely exposure place/time. Subsequent fever, an expanding rash or other illness warrants clinical assessment. A positive tick test does not prove infection in the person; a negative one can falsely reassure. CDC's testing advice is US-specific, not a replacement for every local pathway. [S652; S708]

## T2 — Geography and pathogen change the prevention question

Tick exposure is not a diagnosis. Clothing, label-appropriate repellents and body checks reduce exposure opportunities. TBE and Lyme disease are different prevention/evaluation questions. ECDC describes TBE vaccination in endemic contexts; CDC's Serbia page qualifies its advice for US travelers by itinerary and tick exposure. It does not establish a Serbian resident schedule. [S653; S709–S711]

If illness follows exposure, tell the clinician where and when it occurred. Do not infer a pathogen, personal risk, vaccine indication or antibiotic need merely from finding a tick. Current vaccine availability and eligibility need a dated jurisdiction-specific check; this packet makes no worldwide Lyme-vaccine absence claim.

## E1 — Suspected venomous snakebite needs emergency help

Move away from danger and call local emergency services. Keep the person as still as practical while help/transport is arranged; loosen constricting items near the bite with minimal movement and monitor responsiveness and breathing. Never approach or capture the snake, or delay care to identify it. Avoid cutting, suction, rubbing, tourniquets and cold compresses; follow dispatch instructions. [S712; S654; S655]

**Reading and limits:** IFRC 2025 selected Snakebites action/caution/education text and WHO selected first-aid text read. Source chronology and application matter. This card does not teach pressure immobilization: species/venom, setting, training and local protocol affect its use. No antivenom, medicine, incision or invasive procedure is supplied. Abnormal breathing needs immediate established resuscitation/dispatcher guidance.

## E2 — Regional differences limit transfer of snakebite guidance

Venom effects and matching antivenoms vary by species and geography. A lay identification guess must not determine treatment or delay urgent care. Avoid disturbing snakes; prepare suitable footwear and the local emergency route where exposure is plausible. IFRC asks educators to adapt learning to local snakes and services. [S655; S712]
