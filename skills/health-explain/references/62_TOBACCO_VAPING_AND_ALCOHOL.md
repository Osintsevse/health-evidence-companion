# Tobacco, vaping and alcohol: support for change

Adult education and care navigation. Evidence and service listings checked 2026-11-25. An adult who smokes, someone who vapes exclusively and someone with alcohol withdrawal need different help. Start by asking which product the person uses, what change they want, and whether they have symptoms now.

## Alcohol withdrawal can need emergency care

After stopping or sharply reducing alcohol, a seizure, hallucinations, new confusion or severe shaking warrants **emergency medical help now**. Use the emergency service in the person's current location. They should not drive themselves. Do not wait for a routine counselling appointment. [medical:S870]

If a person experiences morning tremor, sweating, retching or anxiety after a reduction, or has previously had severe withdrawal, encourage prompt medical advice before a further reduction or abrupt stop. When symptoms are present, help them reach a clinician today. Ask when they last drank and about previous seizures or delirium so they can tell the care team. Quantity alone cannot establish that withdrawal will be safe at home. Medical assessment determines whether supported withdrawal can happen in the community or needs hospital care. Ongoing treatment may include counselling, social support and clinician-selected medicines to prevent relapse. [medical:S870, medical:S878]

## Smoking cessation: a plan the person chooses

Ask about cigarettes and any other nicotine products separately, prior attempts, the first situation in which they usually smoke, and what helped last time. If they want to quit, agree on a date or a small preparation step, choose one response to a familiar craving, identify someone they can contact and arrange follow-up. After a lapse, ask what happened and adjust the next attempt. Behavioural support can be individual, group-based or by telephone. [medical:S768, medical:S862]

For interested adults who smoke, WHO lists nicotine replacement therapy (NRT), varenicline, bupropion and cytisine. Depending on local labels and suitability, nicotine replacement can combine a patch with a short-acting form such as gum or a lozenge. This is an option to discuss with a pharmacist or clinician, especially when withdrawal has disrupted previous attempts. Pregnancy, age, medical history, other medicines and local availability influence the choice. A list of international options is insufficient for prescribing a product or supporting an individual dose. [medical:S768, medical:S861]

## Vaping: what is the intended outcome?

- **Someone who does not smoke or vape.** There is no smoking harm to offset by taking up vaping. Nicotine can create dependence; aerosol exposure carries risks. Children and non-smokers should not start. Pregnancy needs a maternity or cessation clinician's advice. [medical:S863, medical:S864]
- **An adult who smokes and is considering switching.** Complete replacement of combustible cigarettes can reduce exposure to combustion products. Continuing to smoke while vaping retains smoking-related risk. Ask whether and when the last cigarette was smoked, and help make continued cigarette abstinence realistic. The long-term health effects of vaping remain uncertain. [medical:S863, medical:S864]
- **An adult who has switched and wants to stop vaping.** Ask about cravings and situations that might lead back to cigarettes. Agree on a quit plan and follow-up that protects their smoking cessation. Time without cigarettes, time without vaping and time without any nicotine are separate outcomes. [medical:S863, medical:S864, medical:S865]

England's NHS presents nicotine vaping as one option for adults who want to quit smoking. In the United States, CDC discusses possible benefit from complete substitution for nonpregnant adult smokers and states that FDA has approved no e-cigarette as a smoking-cessation medicine. These are different questions about health advice and regulatory status. Check current guidance and authorization in the relevant country. [medical:S863, medical:S864]

A single-centre Italian trial tested **quitting vaping**. The 140 adults used e-cigarettes daily, had tried to stop and wanted to quit. Both groups received counselling. From weeks 4–12, cotinine-verified continuous abstinence occurred in 40% with varenicline and 20% with placebo (odds ratio 2.67, 95% CI 1.25–5.68). Nausea and abnormal dreams were reported more often with varenicline. The trial selected motivated adults, required an initial reduction in vaping, and only 95 completed the week-24 visit. It does not establish a universal treatment choice for vaping, safety in pregnancy or an indication approved in Russia or Serbia. [medical:S865]

## Alcohol quantity: define the unit before comparing

Ask about the actual container volume, alcohol by volume (ABV), number of containers, frequency and heavy drinking occasions. A glass poured at home can hold several reference units. Units are conventions for describing exposure, and several conventions coexist.

| Source or setting | Ethanol in one reference unit | How to use it |
|---|---:|---|
| Russian RUS-AUDIT validation instrument (2021 protocol) | 10 g | Use that definition when interpreting this instrument. [medical:S866] |
| Serbian national programme published in 2017 | 13 g | Historical programme convention. Confirm the unit in the current local instrument. [medical:S867] |
| UK | 8 g (10 mL) | The Chief Medical Officers' low-risk advice uses this unit. [medical:S868] |
| US | About 14 g | The US standard drink describes an amount of ethanol. [medical:S869] |

For example, a 500 mL beer at 5% ABV contains 25 mL of pure alcohol, about 20 g. That is 2.5 UK units or about 1.4 US standard drinks. Strength, volume and units matter even when both people call it “one beer”. These calculations cannot predict blood alcohol concentration or fitness to drive. [medical:S868, medical:S869]

UK guidance advises people who drink regularly to stay at or below 14 UK units per week; those drinking that much should spread it over at least three days. This is about 112 g of ethanol, **not 14 US standard drinks**. Risks can occur at lower levels, so a guideline limit is neither a target nor a guarantee. WHO links alcohol to cancers and other harm, including at low exposure. There is no reason to start drinking for longevity. Pregnancy or a plan for pregnancy calls for avoiding alcohol and getting support promptly if stopping is difficult. [medical:S868, medical:S207, medical:S870]

For a person without withdrawal risk who wants to reduce drinking, pick a change they can observe: a smaller serving, a lower-ABV or alcohol-free alternative, a drink-free occasion, or an activity with someone who supports the change. Record volume and strength if measuring intake. If controlling the amount is difficult, withdrawal occurs, or drinking is affecting daily life, seek medical or addiction-service help. [medical:S870, medical:S877]

## Finding help in Russia and Serbia

Public listings were checked online on 2026-11-25. Opening times and intake requirements can change; no calls or bookings were made.

| Location and need | Contact and purpose |
|---|---|
| Russia, tobacco | The St Petersburg Research Institute of Phthisiopulmonology operates the national cessation advice line. From Russia call **8-800-200-0-200, extension 1**. The specialist service lists Monday–Friday 09:00–21:00 and Saturday 09:00–18:00 Moscow time. The round-the-clock healthy-lifestyle service uses a different extension. [medical:S871] |
| Serbia, tobacco | Batut's **Ostavi pušenje** line lists **080 020 0002**, weekdays 11:00–14:00 local time. Its directory lists cessation counselling in individual cities. Contact the centre for its current appointments. [medical:S872, medical:S873] |
| Moscow, alcohol and addiction concerns | The city health service lists the Moscow Scientific and Practical Centre for Narcology counselling line **+7 495 709 64 04**. Elsewhere in Russia, ask a local clinician or the official regional health authority for the nearest addiction service (a psychiatrist specializing in addiction, or the regional narcology dispensary). The Moscow contact is city-specific. [medical:S874] |
| Belgrade, alcohol problems | The **Specijalna bolnica za bolesti zavisnosti** lists **+381 11 3671 429** and an alcohol assessment and treatment department. Call to ask about the first examination, referral and payment for the person's circumstances. [medical:S875, medical:S876] |

For a routine enquiry, specify the goal: “I want help stopping smoking”, “I need help to stop vaping without returning to cigarettes”, or “I have had symptoms when cutting down on alcohol and need advice on safe withdrawal”. A phone line for routine counselling cannot treat current severe withdrawal. [medical:S870, medical:S871, medical:S872]
