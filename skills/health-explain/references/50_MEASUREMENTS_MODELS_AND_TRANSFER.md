## PULM-F2 — Oxygenation, carbon dioxide and acid-base results differ

PaO2 concerns the pressure of dissolved arterial oxygen. PaCO2 is the partial pressure of arterial carbon dioxide; pH describes acidity, while bicarbonate is a concentration relevant to acid-base balance and carbon-dioxide carriage. These are related quantities with different meanings. An oxygenation result does not answer the carbon-dioxide or acid-base questions. Source: MedlinePlus, National Library of Medicine. [S725]

SpO2 denotes an indirect pulse-oximeter estimate; SaO2 denotes arterial oxygen saturation in the inspected FDA terminology. A saturation result alone is not a total oxygen-delivery measurement. The quantity of available red-cell oxygen carriers also matters; anemia and an oxygenation percentage answer different questions. [S730; S726; S359]

## ENDO-F1 — Pancreatic signals, effector responses and glycemic measurements differ

Pancreatic alpha cells respond to low glucose with glucagon secretion; beta cells respond to high glucose with insulin secretion. Glucagon can signal the liver to release glucose into blood. Insulin-responsive muscle, fat and liver participate in the system; reduced responsiveness is different from absence of a hormone signal. A single glucose result does not identify which regulatory step explains it. [S728; S729; S727]

A glucose sample describes glycemia at sampling. HbA1c concerns glucose attached to hemoglobin over a longer period, roughly three months, with greater influence from the most recent month. Red-cell lifespan, recent transfusion/blood loss and method-specific hemoglobin interference can alter its interpretation. Discordant measurements require reconciliation of time, biology and assay context; do not average a percentage and a concentration or assign one correction to every condition. [S99]

## PHARM-F1 — Clearance and half-life belong to an explicit model

For constant parameters, one well-mixed compartment, rapid bolus input, instantaneous distribution and first-order elimination with no further input:

- C(t) = C0 × exp(-k × t), with k in reciprocal time.
- CL = k × Vd, with clearance in volume/time.
- t_half = ln(2)/k = ln(2) × Vd/CL.

Clearance is not an amount removed per time. Within this model, multiplying it by the matching concentration gives an amount/time. Constant fractional loss differs from constant absolute loss; a concentration-dependent process is not necessarily first-order proportionality. [S159]

**Original invented arithmetic:** assume apparent Vd = 80 L and CL = 20 L/hour. Then k = 0.25/hour and the model half-life is about 2.77 hours. Doubling Vd at unchanged CL doubles that half-life. These parameters identify no real medicine, person, effect duration, dosing interval or safe dose.

Plasma decline can include redistribution. The lecture's oral-model claim that late decline identifies elimination is conditional: the slower nonzero exponential term can dominate. Do not apply the one-compartment relation to every observed slope or adopt an unqualified route-independent dose/AUC rule. [S159]

## PSY-D1 — Preserve a transfer result at the level actually measured

Hardy et al. (2015) compared online cognitive training with crosswords in adult existing users. The untrained-test composite favored training at ten weeks (reported adjusted d = 0.255, 95% CI 0.198–0.312). This is a measured positive comparison, not demonstrated durable everyday independence or dementia prevention. [psychology:S108]

The article reports 9,919 randomized, 5,045 with post-tests and 4,715 in the primary analysis after excluding 330 contaminated controls. These denominators differ. Supplemental observed-data checks preserved the positive signal but did not recover missing post-tests. Self-report differs from objectively measured function. Commercial interests warrant scrutiny, not automatic dismissal. Late public registration alone does not prove outcome switching; the attached protocol does not authenticate its chronology by itself. [psychology:S108]

**Application:** distinguish the trained activity, an untrained assessment, a valued daily activity and persistence over time. Training amount was observed rather than randomly assigned; a dose-response association does not isolate a causal effect of extra training. Similar dropout proportions do not establish absence of missing-outcome bias. No universal product recommendation or ineffectiveness conclusion follows.
