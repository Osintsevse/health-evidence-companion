# Hearing, vision and cognitive ageing

Adults with hearing or vision loss may seek better communication, safer mobility, greater independence or a lower risk of dementia. Ask which outcome matters. A trial can support one of these goals while leaving others unresolved. This targeted update was checked 2026-11-25.

## Hearing aids and cognitive decline: interpreting ACHIEVE

ACHIEVE randomly assigned 977 US adults aged 70–84 with untreated hearing loss and without substantial cognitive impairment to hearing aids with audiological counselling or to health education. The primary outcome was the three-year change in a composite cognitive score, measured in standard deviations (SD). In the whole randomized group the adjusted between-group difference was **0.002 SD (95% CI −0.077 to 0.081; p=0.96)**. The trial did not establish an average cognitive benefit on that outcome. [medical:S907, medical:S908]

| Prespecified recruitment group | Three-year cognitive change, hearing care vs education | Adjusted difference in change (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| Participants already enrolled in the Atherosclerosis Risk in Communities study (ARIC), n=238 | −0.211 vs −0.402 SD | +0.191 SD (0.022 to 0.360) |
| Newly recruited community participants, n=739 | −0.213 vs −0.151 SD | −0.061 SD (−0.151 to 0.028) |

The formal test for a difference in treatment effects between recruitment groups gave **interaction p=0.010**. The often-quoted **48%** expresses the relative difference in the magnitude of mean cognitive-score decline within ARIC (approximately 0.191/0.402). ARIC participants were older on average, had more risk factors and declined faster under health education. Recruitment group cannot identify which characteristic caused the difference or serve as a validated rule for choosing an individual patient's treatment. In particular, 48% does not quantify cases of dementia prevented. [medical:S907, medical:S908]

A separate ACHIEVE outcome counted a first adjudicated diagnosis of dementia **or** mild cognitive impairment **or** a qualifying decline on a screening measure. There were 45 events in the hearing group and 49 in the education group, HR 0.90 (95% CI 0.61–1.33). Neither these composite event counts nor the primary cognitive-score comparison yield a risk reduction for dementia alone. [medical:S907]

WHO's July 2026 guidance permits offering hearing aids to adults with hearing loss as one part of reducing the risk of cognitive decline or dementia. The recommendation is conditional, with low-certainty evidence. Address the person's hearing loss and communication goals when considering an assessment or fitting. [medical:S247, medical:S909]

## CHOICE: existing mild cognitive impairment is a different population

The Chinese CHOICE trial recruited adults aged at least 60 with mild cognitive impairment (MCI) and moderate-to-severe hearing loss. Its published protocol specified progression to dementia as the primary outcome and described rating-based cognitive assessments. The investigators' university reported 703 participants randomized to hearing intervention or hearing-health education and 610 completing the 24-month visit. It reported dementia-level progression in 3.09% versus 4.74%, without a statistically significant difference. In a separate endpoint, a normal cognitive rating was reported in 15.34% versus 2.36%. The institution did not supply a confidence interval for the primary comparison in its public account. [medical:S910, medical:S911]

A return to the normal category on a rating scale is a change in measured status. It cannot by itself establish reversal of an underlying neurodegenerative disease, long-term independence, or prevention of dementia. Few primary events and incomplete follow-up leave uncertainty. The journal's November correction concerned the number of male participants in the abstract. The protocol and the investigators' own institutional report are the sources used here for design and results. [medical:S910, medical:S911, medical:S919]

## Communication, falls and quality of life

- **Conversations in noise.** A US survey analysis published 2026-11-07 examined 1,724 adults aged at least 45 who used hearing aids and answered the 2024 question about an aided conversation in a noisy room. An estimated 76.5% (survey-weighted, 95% CI 74.1–78.8%) reported some degree of difficulty. This cross-sectional estimate has no unaided baseline or randomized comparator. Ask about the specific setting and whether refitting, communication strategies or environmental changes could help. The survey cannot determine how much benefit the same person gets from amplification. [medical:S917, medical:S909]
- **Falls with hearing intervention.** ACHIEVE also assessed a prespecified exploratory outcome by asking annually about falls during the previous year. Over three years the adjusted estimated mean was 1.45 falls per participant with hearing care and 1.98 with education, a difference of −0.54 (95% CI −0.77 to −0.31). Counts include recurrent falls. This result cannot be presented as 27% fewer people falling, or as a proven reduction in loss of independence. Recall, lack of participant blinding and changing exposure to fall hazards during the pandemic matter. This is another analysis of the same trial. [medical:S912]
- **Established dementia.** SENSE-Cog randomized 252 people with mild-to-moderate dementia and hearing or vision difficulties across five European countries. The intervention included assessment, sensory aids where indicated and home support. Among 251 included in the analysis, the adjusted difference from usual care on the primary quality-of-life score at 36 weeks was 0.18 points (95% CI −2.13 to 2.30). Some participants reported device discomfort or minor sensory symptoms. A benefit on this score was not established. The result leaves room to treat discomfort and target hearing or visual difficulty for the individual. [medical:S913]

## Cataract surgery: functional outcomes and dementia associations

An English trial randomized 306 women older than 70 with cataract to expedited first-eye surgery or the routine waiting list. Visual function improved in the expedited group. The rate of falls over 12 months was lower (rate ratio 0.66, 95% CI 0.45–0.96), yet 49% versus 45% of participants fell at least once. Multiple events in one person explain how the fall rate and the proportion with a first fall can move differently. Timing of surgery in this group does not answer whether operating prevents dementia in the broader population. [medical:S916]

Two studies of cataract surgery and **incident dementia** illustrate the uncertainty from observational comparisons. In a US prospective cohort of 3,038 people aged 65 or older with cataract, surgery was associated with lower dementia incidence over 23,554 person-years (adjusted HR 0.71, 95% CI 0.62–0.83). Visual acuity and cataract severity were unavailable. A Korean insurance cohort of 300,327 people aged at least 45 with cataract found a slightly higher adjusted hazard of recorded dementia after surgery (HR 1.10, 95% CI 1.02–1.19). Its endpoint required diagnostic coding and a dementia-drug prescription. Selection for surgery, prior cognition, clinical severity, access to care and ascertainment complicate both estimates. They cannot establish that cataract surgery prevents or causes dementia. Base a discussion of surgery on visual disability, goals, clinical assessment and procedural benefits and risks. [medical:S914, medical:S915, medical:S916]
