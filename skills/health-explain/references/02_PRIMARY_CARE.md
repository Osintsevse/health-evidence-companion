# Primary care reasoning

Sources: S01, S04-S06. Educational workflow, not an established diagnosis.

## Three tasks

- Triage: how urgently in-person help is needed.
- Diagnostic reasoning: which explanations fit and what would distinguish them.
- Treatment: improve the condition or relieve symptoms with acceptable risk.

A complaint is a person's experience; a sign is a measurement or examination finding; a syndrome combines findings; a diagnosis is a justified clinical conclusion. An ICD code classifies a condition and does not select a medicine. Clinical education also involves examination, practice and supervised feedback. Reading cannot replace these. [S01, S05]

## Relevant context

Clarify the main problem, onset, duration and trajectory; location and quality; aggravating and relieving factors; relevant measurements; accompanying symptoms and warning signs; relevant conditions, allergies, medicines and substances. Contacts, travel, injury, exertion and sleep matter only when relevant. Do not ask the full checklist for every simple question. Ask only what changes the explanation or next step. Do not solicit identifiers or a personal archive through the public plugin.

## Urgency

Severe breathing difficulty, stroke-like signs, substantial pressure-like chest pain, loss of consciousness or rapid deterioration require an emergency route. Swelling of the tongue or throat with breathing difficulty is also urgent. Do not delay help for a lengthy interview. Use verified local emergency instructions; determine the timing of less urgent assessment using the relevant guideline. [S05; verify a condition-specific protocol for a concrete question]

## Evaluate an explanation

Explain possible causes and findings that help a clinician distinguish them. Identify warning signs separately. Ask which facts conflict with a hypothesis and what decision a test would change. Do not assign a definitive diagnosis or invent numerical confidence from an incomplete conversation. [S05-S06]

## Rational treatment information

Identify the problem and treatment goal; compare approaches; check applicability; explain use, restrictions and monitoring. Symptom relief does not prove treatment of the cause. Separate a prescription, actual use and observed effect. WHO's historical prescribing method remains useful as a workflow, but current drugs and doses require current sources. [S04]
