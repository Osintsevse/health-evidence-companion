## HEP-B1 — Production, conjugation and removal explain different bilirubin questions

Red-cell breakdown produces bilirubin. In the liver, bilirubin-UGT, encoded by UGT1A1, transfers glucuronic acid to unconjugated bilirubin. This conjugation increases water solubility, enabling removal; biliary removal is a further stage. Gilbert syndrome illustrates reduced conjugation with unconjugated bilirubin accumulation. That mechanism does not identify the cause of one person's result. Accelerated red-cell breakdown, liver disease and bile-duct obstruction are other broad possibilities in the NLM test explanation. [S760, S761]

Total bilirubin alone does not describe its components. Newcastle's laboratory explains that its direct bilirubin result considered with total helps estimate the unconjugated component; the relative amounts can help clarify causes of an elevated total. This is the purpose of obtaining additional fraction information. It is not a validated diagnosis from a fraction pattern. Preserve the laboratory's analyte names and method: the source's direct/conjugated terminology does not establish analytical equivalence for every assay. [S762]

A useful explanation separates increased production, altered conjugation and impaired removal, then asks which of those questions the reported measurement can address. A normal ALT alone does not establish Gilbert syndrome or settle all liver/blood-cell questions. This is an inference boundary, not a complete isolated-hyperbilirubinemia pathway. No numerical fraction rule, arithmetic conversion, fasting provocation, personal regimen or newborn interpretation follows.

## ADO-P1 — Growth course, hormone signals and skeletal maturation answer different questions

Puberty involves hypothalamic GnRH signaling to pituitary LH and FSH, which signal ovaries/testes to release sex hormones. Later development can reflect constitutional timing, nutrition/illness or disturbed hypothalamic-pituitary/gonadal function. Chronological age and family timing alone do not establish which explanation applies. [S763]

Dated serial heights describe a growth course; a single height describes size at one point. A clinician can use an appropriate growth chart to assess the trajectory and whether growth has slowed. Bone age adds skeletal-maturation information and can contribute to an estimate of remaining growth/adult height. It does not directly measure the circulating hormone signals. The two PES/AAP guides are one committee evidence stream, not independent replications. [S765, S766]

Hormone measurements and selected stimulation tests examine signals or responses within the pathway; history and examination frame their meaning. The NICHD diagnosis page's simple bone-growth wording does not turn an X-ray into a measured growth velocity. Serial measurements, skeletal maturation and hormonal responses supply different observations; combining them still needs clinical interpretation. Do not infer a harmless cause from delayed bone age or a parent's late puberty alone. [S764, S765, S766]
