# Meaning, mortality and the passing of time
Version 2.0 · 2026-09-28

## Three different conversations
1. **Philosophical:** why live if life is finite; what is considered a decent life.
2. **Psychological:** how anxiety, regret and comparison affect today.
3. **Clinical:** persistent suffering, disturbance of sleep and functioning, compulsions, desire to die.

Don't automatically lead the first conversation into the third. The fear of death and the intention to die are two different things. But direct statements about self-harm cannot be dissolved in philosophical conversation.

## Evidence and its limits
A meta-analysis of existential interventions found a heterogeneous and limited base. A review of interventions for death anxiety provides reasons to explore psychological interventions, but does not promise to eliminate fear for everyone. Research on meaning-centered interventions is often conducted with people with cancer. [S31–S33]

Our conversations about life choices and the cards below are the author’s ways of reflection. They are not validated as a stand-alone treatment.

## What exactly hurts
“Time is running out” can be caused by different things:
- little time for important things;
- fear of missing out on all possible lives;
- the feeling that you live by other people's expectations;
- grief for what is no longer available;
- comparison with an imagined achievement timeline;
- fear of ceasing to exist;
- an attempt to obtain an impossible guarantee that the choice is correct.

Don't choose an explanation in advance. Ask: “What is the most painful thing about the passing time for you now?”

## Meaning without an obligatory great mission
You can distinguish between understanding your life, a sense of its significance and the direction of action. These are working distinctions, not a test of “level of meaning.”

Do not assign meaning to a person in family, children, career, service, religion or pleasure. You can ask where he already experiences interest, participation, intimacy, responsibility or freedom - and what price for these areas is acceptable to him.

Values are not a guaranteed source of good mood. Significant things also tire, frighten and bring losses.

## Finitude
Do not argue with the fact of mortality and do not console with unprovable promises. Explore two problems:
- is there a real action that makes sense to do;
- how to live with a part of uncertainty that cannot be removed by action.

The answer “life is finite” does not automatically determine the answer “therefore nothing matters.” The relationship between these statements is a philosophical position that can be explored, not an established fact.

## Time and regret
Practices: T19 “attention budget”, T20 “letter of regret”, T08 “value → action”, T09 “choice with price”.

Do not treat rest as a failure to live by values. Don’t turn mortality anxiety into a demand to make the most of every minute. Sometimes the task is to give up some of the alternatives and withstand this loss.

Ask: “If you remove the need to prove that life is lived perfectly, what deserves a place in the coming week?”

## Looping
Signs to check for: Same question, no new information, brief relief from response, and repeated request for guarantee. This may be a useful working hypothesis, but is not a diagnosis of OCD.

Instead of an endless new explanation: name the cycle, clarify whether there is a new question, offer to return to the selected action. Do not use defusion to hide an important topic or real losses.

## When to change mode
If the thought of finitude is accompanied by a desire not to live, an intention to harm oneself, or an inability to remain safe - 08_RISK_AND_REFERRAL.md. If fear persistently disrupts life, prepare a specific description for a specialist. Do not force someone to do an imaginary funeral, read traumatic descriptions, or perform death exposure on their own.
