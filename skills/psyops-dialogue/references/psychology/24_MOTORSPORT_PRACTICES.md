# Motorsport: preparation, runs and debriefing
Version 1.0 · 2026-10-02 · original adaptations for an adult athlete

Navigation: conditions; disciplines; eight practices M01–M08; recording; revision.

Bases - 23_SPORT_PSYCHOLOGY.md. The cards did not undergo separate testing. Time indicates a convenient format, not a therapeutic dose.

## Define the task
Specify as necessary: discipline/format, experience, available attempts, familiarity with the route and one episode. Don't guess the ambiguous name of the race. One question should change the next step.

Distinguish between conditions and vehicle mechanics, skill deficiency, physical condition, psychological process, and medical task. Concerns about unreliable brakes require a brake inspection. A training/race difference does not in itself prove a psychological cause.

| Format | Accent | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Autoslalom, short sprint | Current scheme, start of attempt, one task, no chase after error | Few attempts; do not assume warm-up laps |
| Circuit racing, time attack | Sectors, consistency, traffic and sustained attention | Compare clean laps under comparable conditions |
| Drift | Judges' criteria, familiar landmarks, qualification/pair attempts | Courage is not a substitute for control; agree on the technique with the trainer |
| Rally | Current pace notes, crew work, recovery | Do not change the command system on the fly |
| Karting, sim racing | Start/pressure rehearsal and feedback | Transfer to a vehicle is not guaranteed; a simulator does not verify car roadworthiness |

This is an organization of problems, not a comparative study of disciplines.

## M01 - One process goal
**When:** all attention is focused on “win”. **Goal:** controlled action. [S82]
1. Separate the desired result from the method of execution.
2. Choose one element you have mastered with an instructor or from your proven practice.
3. Name the observed task and a familiar short clue.
4. After stopping, evaluate the execution and the result separately.
**Limit:** new braking points, speed and trajectory are not assigned by psychological advice. A new skill is first trained.

## M02 - Pre-start routine
**When:** before the start, attention is scattered. **Goal:** Fewer decisions before moving. [S81]
Guideline 30–90 seconds, try it in training:
- Normal readiness and equipment; The organizer’s instructions remain in force.
- One familiar, verified element of starting the run.
- One execution goal and a familiar prompt.
- Allow for excitement, do not endlessly recheck the degree of calm.
Short version when delayed: readiness → task → prompt.
**Limit:** do not interfere with the start commands and do not turn the routine into a “if interrupted, everything is lost” condition. After the movement begins, remove new psychological tasks.

## M03 - Realistic mental rehearsal
**When:** there is proven knowledge of the course/action. **Goal:** rehearse the action while stationary. [S84]
Guideline 2–5 minutes in a safe place:
1. Choose a familiar fragment, use a proven video/diagram.
2. Imagine normal execution at a realistic pace.
3. Add a moderate glitch, such as a delayed start, and a rehearsed response.
4. End with a doable action, not a must-win.
**Limit:** do not complete an unknown trajectory, do not rehearse the accident to get used to it, do not treat traumatic memories. If it gets worse, stop.

## M04 - Familiar external landmark
**When:** in the competition tries to consciously control every movement of the body.
**Goal:** check the economical organization of attention during training. [S85–S86]
While stationary, describe focus; with the instructor, choose an already understandable target for the task/effect of the action; test in familiar conditions; After stopping, evaluate the performance.
**Limit:** do not “look only at the apex” and do not fix your gaze on a point. Maintain visual scanning, peripheral awareness and attention to flags. A beginner may need a technical explanation; external focus is not a universal rule.

## M05 - Return after error
**When:** a miss triggers a rush to make up time. **Goal:** Select a reaction in advance. [S83, S88–S89]
- While stationary, review a typical error; agree on a familiar phrase, such as “next section.”
- During the run, maintain control and attention to the situation. Only a previously mastered hint, if not distracting.
- After stopping, assess the condition, damage and the need to check the car; then look at the reasons.
**Limit:** no breathing rituals, counting, writing or talking with AI on the go. Danger, damage or disorientation requires a safe exit or stop required by the applicable event procedures, not a psychological reset.

## M06 - Pressure rehearsal
**When:** the mastered action changes only in timed or scored attempts. **Goal:** performance under moderate pressure. [S88–S89]
1. Check the comparability of conditions.
2. Choose a simulator or a suitable organized training session in a closed area.
3. Add one factor: scoring attempt, observer or regular start count.
4. Retain the task, load limit and ability to stop.
5. After stopping, evaluate the change in actions.
**Limit:** no public roads, deliberate lack of sleep, increasing speed to provoke fear, monetary fines, humiliation and dangerous proximity to obstacles. The conditions of the track training are determined by the organizer/instructor. Do not introduce a new practice on the day of an important competition.

## M07 - Debrief without self-condemnation
**When:** “everything is terrible” or you want to change ten things. **Goal:** data and one next experiment.
Guideline 3–5 minutes after normal recovery:
- One observation about what went well.
- Main loss: video/time/penalty separate from guess.
- Conditions that could have an impact.
- One working reason and a significant alternative.
- What to retain and what to test next.
**Limit:** do not declare lack of progress as weak will and do not evaluate a person by lap time. Simultaneous changes to the car, task, and practice interfere with causal inference.

## M08 - Recovery and return
**When:** exhaustion, fear after the incident, travel overload.
**Goal:** eliminate obstacles that psychological techniques do not correct. [S91–S92, S95–S96]
1. Distinguish between anxiety and possible symptoms/unpreparedness.
2. If you experience symptoms after impact, contact your medical team; do not seek medical or racing clearance from a chat.
3. After checking the person’s condition and the car, plan to return with the instructor and, if necessary, a clinician.
4. Reduce organizational load: things, schedule, time to sleep.
5. In case of persistent disruption of life, prepare to seek appropriate professional help.
**Limit:** do not suppress defensive fear, do not consider any anxiety as trauma. Do not advise sedatives, alcohol, stimulants instead of rest, or self-exposure to an accident. Do not set the return period and compliance with regulations from the chat.

|---|---|
| Context | Date, format, conditions; minimum identifiers |
| Task | One execution action |
| Condition | Brief self-rating if useful |
| Data | Time/penalty/video, source and restrictions |

Personal Journal is not included in the overall package or skill. Do not automatically save other people's medical data and private team telemetry.

## Revise the plan
If the practice is distracting, increases panic/shame, becomes a ritual, requires new reassurances, does not help in comparable episodes, or real symptoms/technical problems appear - clarify the reason, simplify or stop. One successful attempt does not prove effectiveness.
