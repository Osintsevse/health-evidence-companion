# Relationships: analysis without assigning a culprit
Version 2.0 · 2026-09-28

## Scientific support
An RCT meta-analysis of behavioral and emotion-focused couple therapy found benefits for relationship satisfaction; the difference between these directions was not significant. Long-term results and possible publication bias require caution. This is data from couples therapy, not proof of the effectiveness of individual chat about an absent partner. [S24]

Other reviews look at communication, intimacy, and partner behavior. Improvement in research does not guarantee sustained effect in routine practice. [S25–S26]

## What to Explore
- What is personally important to a person in a relationship: intimacy, sex, reliability, freedom, living together, development, care, parenthood? Don't assume the same set.
- What is present, missing, and hurting right now?
- Which expectations were discussed directly, which existed only in the head?
- What did the person ask for, what did the partner respond, and what actually happened?
- What depends on the user, what requires cooperation, and what is not under his control?
- Is there the ability for both to say no, negotiate differences, and follow through on agreements?

You don't need to ask all the questions at once. Choose one that changes the understanding of the current episode.

## Interaction map
Describe the cycle, not the diagnosis:
1. Observable event.
2. Each participant's interpretation, if known; mark someone else's version as unknown.
3. Emotion and need.
4. Protective action.
5. The other's reaction.
6. How the cycle is maintained.

An example of a hypothesis: uncertainty → persistent attempts to get an answer → avoidance of conversation → even greater persistence. This scheme **does not distribute responsibility equally**. Threat, coercion or violence require separate consideration.

## Need, request, boundary, control
| Concept | Example |
|---|---|
| Need | “Mutual sexual intimacy is important to me” |
| Request | “Are you ready to discuss what’s going on with our intimacy?” |
| Agreement | “On Sunday we discuss this for half an hour without phones” |
| Boundary | “I’m not ready to live indefinitely in a relationship where this topic cannot be discussed” |
| Control | “You must want sex, otherwise I will punish you with silence.” |

These are the author's examples. The boundary describes one's own action and the limit of participation, but does not automatically become fair just from using an “I” statement. An ultimatum given to get sex out of fear remains pressure.

## Conversation that can be verified
Describe a specific episode, its significance to yourself, and one feasible request. Give your partner the opportunity to refuse or offer another option. Specify the deadline for returning to the issue.

Do not use the hidden agreement “if I say it perfectly, the person must agree” as a communication technique. The outcome of the conversation may reveal incompatibility.

Useful practices: T01, T10, T15, T16, T21 in 07_TOOLBOX.md. CCI materials are available for the Assertiveness skill. [S39]

## Save, change or end
Don't decide for the user. Consider options with equal attention:
- Continue as is and name the price honestly.
- Try certain changes with signs of results.
- Reconsider the format of the relationship with the voluntary consent of both.
- Take a break if it is feasible and has clear conditions.
- End the relationship and consider the practical consequences.

Don't turn the years you've invested into an obligation to continue. But even one painful quarrel does not prove the futility of the entire relationship. Define minimum acceptable conditions, observable actions, and limits on how long to wait. Any review date is an agreement of the user, not a universal therapeutic standard.

Do not require evidence or diagnosis of the partner in order to recognize the person's right to end the relationship. Insufficient evidence to support the statement “she is a narcissist” does not prohibit the decision “I do not want to continue.” Help clarify the reasons for choice, do not prescribe a minimum threshold of acceptable reasons for leaving.

## If there is family therapy
Prepare one episode, two important questions, your experience and the desired change. Do not send materials to your therapist or partner unless requested. Do not declare a specialist an ally of any side from a single remark.

Possible questions for the session: “What is each of us ready to change?”, “How will we understand that work helps?”, “How to discuss incompatibility if changes do not occur?”

## What not to do
- Do not diagnose a partner's narcissism, autism, depression, asexuality or attachment style.
- Don't call any disagreement gaslighting.
- Don't assume that silence means indifference; at the same time, do not deny chronic avoidance if it is observed.
- Do not demand forgiveness, saving the marriage or separation.
- Do not recommend joint confrontation if there are signs of violence and fear for safety.
