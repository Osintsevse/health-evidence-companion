# Hair loss: diagnosis, regrowth and treatment safety

A widening part, a handful of shed hairs and a scaly bald patch can have different causes and can coexist. The first decision is whether delaying examination could cost someone hair permanently. Module 61 covers routine dandruff care, traction and biotin. This chapter covers clinical patterns, scalp examination and what treatment trials actually measured. [medical:S513, medical:S849, medical:S850, medical:S920, medical:S922, medical:S924]

## When the scalp needs an examination

| What has changed? | What an examination can clarify |
| --- | --- |
| Shedding from across the scalp, sometimes about three months after illness, major weight loss or childbirth | Telogen effluvium is one possibility. Shedding often settles over three to six months, while fullness may take longer to recover. A continuing trigger or coexisting pattern hair loss can prolong thinning. A clinician can use the history and examination to decide whether iron or thyroid tests would change care. [medical:S920, medical:S513] |
| Gradual thinning through the part or crown, or a receding hairline | Pattern hair loss is possible. Ask about speed, distribution, scalp changes, medicines, styling and recent shedding. A clinician can examine hairs and follicle openings under magnification. Atypical findings may call for a scalp biopsy. [medical:S512, medical:S513, medical:S922, medical:S927] |
| Crown breakage or thinning spreading outwards, sometimes with burning, scale or a smooth shiny area | Seek dermatology assessment promptly. Central centrifugal cicatricial alopecia can progress **without pain**. Scarring destroys follicles. Early treatment aims to preserve those that remain, and minoxidil alone does not control the underlying inflammation. A slowly receding band along the front and sides with eyebrow loss can be another scarring disorder, frontal fibrosing alopecia. [medical:S921, medical:S922, medical:S923] |
| A scaly bald patch with broken hairs, especially in a child | Ask for examination for scalp ringworm. It can resemble dandruff, eczema or psoriasis. A painful, soft swelling with pustules, crusting, fever or tender neck nodes needs urgent assessment. Severe inflammation can leave permanent bald patches. A clinician can take samples and consider starting treatment before culture results. Scalp ringworm usually requires a prescription oral antifungal. Shampoo can help limit transmission. Avoid applying steroid-containing creams to suspected ringworm. [medical:S924, medical:S925] |
| New round or oval patches, sometimes involving beard, brows, lashes or nails | Alopecia areata is an autoimmune possibility. Examination of the scalp, brows and nails helps distinguish other causes. Spontaneous regrowth and recurrence are both possible. Extent, age and the person's goals affect whether observation, camouflage or specialist treatment makes sense. [medical:S926, medical:S927] |

For a visit, bring the time course, changes in medicines or supplements and any earlier photographs that show the same area. Mention scalp pain, burning or scale even when little is visible. If restoring density is unlikely, ask whether treatment could slow further loss or relieve symptoms. [medical:S513, medical:S920, medical:S921, medical:S922]

## Topical minoxidil for pattern hair loss

**Serbia.** ALIMS lists Regaine 50 mg/g cutaneous foam as available without prescription. Its 11 March 2025 renewal lists an expiry of 11 March 2030. The Serbian product document indicates adult men with androgenetic alopecia and an intact scalp. It directs people with sudden, uneven or unexplained loss or an inflamed scalp away from self-treatment. It advises consulting a doctor first for known cardiovascular disease or arrhythmia. Keep the treated area away from an infant's skin. [medical:S928, medical:S929]

The Serbian label reports a 16-week, vehicle-controlled trial. Among 180 men assigned active foam and 172 assigned vehicle, the average increase in mature hairs in a measured patch was **21.0 versus 4.3 per cm²**, a difference of 16.7. Participants also rated the benefit higher with active foam. Individual response varies. Continued treatment is needed to maintain regrowth. Stop and seek medical advice for palpitations, faintness, sudden swelling or persistent scalp irritation. Current chest pain with emergency features follows the chest-pain pathway. [medical:S929, medical:S460]

**United States.** The separately labelled Women's Rogaine Unscented 5% foam is for thinning on the top of the scalp. The label gives a three- to six-month timeframe for visible results and warns about pregnancy or breastfeeding. Match the person, strength, form and country to the actual pack. The Serbian register establishes authorization and dispensing status. Pharmacy stock is a separate question. [medical:S928, medical:S929, medical:S930]

**Comparison matters.** In a 24-week vehicle-controlled trial in women, the Swedish regulator's assessment found an average advantage of about 9 additional hairs per cm² with 5% foam. Women also rated scalp coverage higher. In a separate trial of 322 women comparing foam with 2% minoxidil solution, the adjusted difference was −0.3 hairs per cm² (95% CI −6.0 to 5.4). The regulator reports that the trial missed its formal margin of −5, which reviewers considered too strict. The sponsor's ClinicalTrials.gov result lists a different margin of −6.565. Average counts were close, but the conclusion about noninferiority depends on the criterion used. [medical:S933, medical:S934]

## Tablets, compounded sprays and prescription treatment

**Oral minoxidil.** A 2026 double-blind trial in Pakistan randomized 100 adults with pattern hair loss to oral or topical minoxidil, with matching placebos. At 24 weeks 90 completed follow-up. Photographs showed at least mild improvement in 67% of oral versus 73% of topical completers (p=0.652). Satisfaction did not differ significantly. Unwanted hair growth was reported in 30% versus 9%, and contact dermatitis in 0% versus 11%. The study could not establish equivalence or estimate uncommon serious harms. The US oral tablet label inspected is for severe hypertension refractory to other treatment and warns about pericardial effusion, tamponade and worsening angina. That label does not quantify risk at doses studied for hair loss. A prescriber should assess cardiovascular history and other medicines before considering off-label use. [medical:S931, medical:S932]

**Finasteride.** The FDA warns about systemic absorption, sexual and other adverse-effect reports, and transfer to a pregnant person from US compounded topical preparations. The spontaneous reports cannot estimate frequency. The EMA review covers authorized products in participating EU countries. It confirmed suicidal thoughts as a side effect of finasteride tablets of unknown frequency. Its advice for new mood changes while taking the 1 mg hair-loss tablet is to stop it and contact a doctor promptly. The review did not identify a link between suicidal thoughts and the evaluated skin sprays. Formulation and jurisdiction matter when explaining either finding. [medical:S935, medical:S936]

**Severe alopecia areata.** In the US ritlecitinib trial, participants aged 12 or older started with at least 50% scalp hair loss. After 24 weeks, 23.0% assigned the approved regimen and 1.6% assigned placebo had at most 20% of the scalp still affected (difference 21.4 percentage points, 95% CI 13.4 to 29.5). This threshold describes the scalp at follow-up. It does not measure treatment-free remission, and most treated participants did not reach it at that visit. The US label requires evaluation for infections and blood-count abnormalities and includes boxed warnings, including serious infections and thrombosis. Specialist decisions weigh these risks against the effect of hair loss on the person. [medical:S927, medical:S937]

## Reading a new regrowth claim

A hair count measures a small area. A blinded photograph can assess visible change, a patient rating can capture how noticeable it is, and follow-up after stopping can test durability. An observed change in a hair follicle or the announcement of a trial does not establish any of those outcomes. For example, the November 17, 2026 registry update for the PP405 trial in women listed recruitment and a planned 24-week hair count and participant-rated coverage. It had no posted outcome data at this check. [medical:S929, medical:S932, medical:S934, medical:S938]
