## F3 — A mechanism or biomarker result is not automatically clinical benefit

**Scope:** evaluating the claim and endpoint; not declaring every surrogate invalid.

Separate a proposed explanation of how a treatment works from evidence about what it does. A biological measurement can show a response under the studied conditions without demonstrating better symptoms, function, complications or survival. Keep the measured endpoint and harms visible. [S681]

FDA describes a surrogate as a measure used in place of a direct clinical-benefit outcome. Some surrogate uses are established; others are considered reasonably likely to predict benefit. Its acceptability depends on the disease, population, treatment mechanism and clinical setting, with case-by-case assessment. A marker accepted in one programme cannot simply be carried into another. The FDA table is a regulatory discussion resource, not blanket validation of every biomarker or treatment. [S681]

**Original fictional example:** a trial finds that Treatment Z changes Marker Q. First report that measured result with its comparator, timing and uncertainty. Then ask whether Q has an adequately supported relationship with the outcome claimed in this population and intervention context. An association between Q and disease risk is a different question from whether changing Q with Z improves outcomes. The study also needs relevant benefit/harms evidence before a claim of net benefit.

These limits do not show that Z is ineffective. They identify what remains unproven by this particular result. Conversely, biological plausibility must not fill a missing clinical-outcome comparison. A direct symptom outcome can be meaningful even if mortality is not the aim; match the endpoint to the actual question.

**Reading:** FDA surrogate-resource definition, key considerations and limitations, checked 2026-10-17; individual table rows and underlying trials not appraised or reproduced. The source is dynamic. A named marker or consequential treatment question requires current context-specific evidence.

## F4 — A score, its intended meaning and its measurement properties differ

**Scope:** general measurement reasoning; no questionnaire administration, diagnosis or validation of a named instrument.

A measure is a specified procedure that yields an observation or score. A construct is what the score is intended to represent, such as attention or distress. Task instructions, context and assumptions connect them. Goldstein's stored selected study illustrates inference from task performance; one slow response alone does not identify a unique mental process, motive or disorder. That textbook was not reopened by this packet's methods researcher. [psychology:S103]

Reliability concerns consistency/precision under relevant conditions and the influence of measurement error. FDA advises examining likely variation from occasions, raters or items in the intended context. Test-retest evidence needs a period in which the underlying health state can reasonably remain stable; a real change is not automatically measurement failure. Consistency alone does not establish that a score captures the intended concept. [S682]

Validity concerns the evidence supporting a proposed interpretation and use of scores in a specified context. FDA treats this as a coherent argument for fitness for purpose, rather than a universal badge attached to a measure. The older NIH-hosted PRO chapter separately discusses responsiveness: detecting change over time in the intended construct. Detecting change does not establish that its size matters to patients or that treatment caused it. [S682, S683]

**Original fictional example:** a memory-task score improves after practice. Identify the task, scoring, population and intended inference; ask about error, task familiarity and transfer. Even a reliable change in that task need not demonstrate improved everyday independence. An adult-language validation cannot be assumed to establish an unrelated use without suitable evidence.

**Reading:** FDA final PFDD Guidance3, October2025, II.B–C and IV.B,G–H; NIH-hosted PRO chapter PDFpp20–27, date/authorship unresolved. These are methods sources, not instrument-specific validation. No items/scoring rules are reproduced; NIH hosting does not establish public-domain authorship. Goldstein reading is inherited selected scope, not new textbook reading.

## S3 — Poor sleep has several possible explanations and different tests

**Scope:** adult educational assessment; child, pregnancy and other special settings need their own guidance.

First distinguish inadequate opportunity or setting from difficulty sleeping despite opportunity. Ask about work/care schedules, time in bed, actual sleep, naps, medicines/substances and daytime impact. NHLBI's insomnia overview describes difficulty sleeping despite time and a suitable environment; a diary can support assessment. Short sleep alone does not establish insomnia. [S672]

Loud snoring, witnessed breathing pauses or gasping, with sleepiness or other daytime effects, can prompt assessment for sleep apnea. Symptoms overlap and are not diagnostic. A consistently shifted sleep schedule can also suggest a body-clock/environment mismatch; the cause and type guide evaluation. Do not assume every sleep complaint is solved by hygiene advice, or that all daytime tiredness is apnea. [S673, S674]

VA/DoD2025 recommends objective sleep testing for clinically suspected obstructive sleep apnea. Home sleep apnea testing is an option for appropriately selected patients, not a universal replacement for laboratory polysomnography. Comorbidities and test suitability matter. In a preselected high-risk patient, a non-diagnostic home test does not rule apnea out; the guideline recommends further appropriate testing. This repeat-testing recommendation is strong despite **very low confidence** in the supporting evidence, reflecting the broader benefit/harm judgment. Strength and certainty are different attributes. [S657]

**Original fictional example:** a symptomatic adult has a non-diagnostic home test and a partner reports breathing pauses. Do not call the result an all-clear or prescribe a device from a note. Bring the symptoms, test type/result and relevant conditions back for clinical evaluation. A consumer wearable's output is not established here as equivalent to the assessed tests.

**Reading:** NHLBI selected overview/symptom sections; VA/DoD2025 selected pp30,34,40–41,43–44, including recommendations2–4 and their context; pp34,44 visually checked. Not the full guideline, diagnostic-criteria appendix or instrument validation. No screening items, thresholds or test-selection algorithm copied. Safety-threatening sleepiness takes priority over a routine sleep-plan discussion.

For a selected primary comparison of supported testing/management strategies, read SLEEP-D2 in [models, strategies and iron markers](54_MODELS_STRATEGIES_AND_IRON_MARKERS.md). Its2017 trial/correction appraisal does not replace the current, separately scoped test-selection guidance above.

## A1 — Healthy ageing concerns valued function, capacity and environment

**Scope:** understanding goals and assessment, not labelling a person by age or administering ICOPE in chat.

WHO's healthy-ageing framework centres on what people can be and do in daily life. Function depends on physical/mental capacity, the environment and their interaction. Having a controlled health condition does not by itself prevent healthy ageing, and people of the same chronological age can have very different needs. [S675, S676]

ICOPE's introductory material emphasizes the person's goals, preferences, autonomy and participation, with coordinated health/social care and follow-up close to ordinary life. Supports in the home and community can matter alongside treatment of a health condition. Its handbook is for trained care workers and local adaptation; reading its opening concepts does not amount to administering its assessments or care pathways. [S504]

**Original fictional example:** an older person wants to keep shopping independently. Clarify what changed: walking tolerance, vision, hearing, remembering purchases, transport, confidence or access. Agree the valued activity and what an improvement would look like. A score or diagnosis label alone does not say which support will help. Record baseline, trajectory and everyday impact rather than compare the person with an age stereotype.

Improved capacity on a task, participation in daily life and caregiver burden are related but distinct outcomes. Environmental changes can improve function without proving a change in biological ageing. A new unexplained decline still needs assessment; do not dismiss it as inevitable age or use the framework to select treatment without evaluation.

**Reading:** WHO functional-ability Q&A2020 and Ageing and health2025 selected sections; ICOPE second edition selected glossary/introductory pages, viewer8–10,13–16,21–23 (printedvi–viii,1–4,9–11), plus rights/contents. No instrument, scoring or pathway reproduced. Landing page is dated2025-09-22; the supplied PDF's copyright and suggested citation say2024. Preserve these distinct dates. This is conceptual study, not full-handbook review or clinical qualification.

## A2 — A biological-age change does not directly measure added lifespan

**Scope:** a real research example of endpoint interpretation, not a calorie-restriction prescription or a complete longevity review.

The CALERIE report examined DNA-methylation measures in a post hoc analysis of a two-year randomized trial in healthy adults without obesity. Although220 people were randomized, the molecular analysis used197 with baseline and at least one follow-up sample. The reported intervention affected DunedinPACE but did not show significant changes in the PhenoAge/GrimAge clock estimates. These measures model different aspects of ageing; they are not interchangeable readouts of “years gained.” [S677]

The authors explicitly say follow-up did not extend beyond the intervention in this report, so translation to reduced long-term disease or mortality remained unknown. Correlations from other observational cohorts are not mortality outcomes observed in this randomized comparison. No full supplement, dataset reanalysis or comprehensive later-follow-up search was completed here. The checked author correction concerns an affiliation. [S677, S678]

**Original fictional example:** a commercial report says a clock moved “three years younger.” Ask which clock, whether change exceeds relevant measurement uncertainty, the comparison group, duration and whether clinical outcomes were measured. Do not convert the change into three additional years of life, disease prevention, an individual forecast or a reason to copy a trial's restriction target.

An intervention can have a useful clinical benefit without extending life, while a favourable laboratory change can coexist with harms. Assess direct outcomes and applicability separately; these cards do not endorse an anti-ageing medicine, supplement or restrictive diet. Suitability, nutrition and health conditions need their own evaluation.

**Reading:** Waziry et al., Nature Aging2023, abstract, opening cohort account and selected outcome/limitations paragraphs through the official PMC author manuscript; article-specific CC BY 4.0 notice and May2023 affiliation correction checked. This is selected original-report study, not full risk-of-bias appraisal, current treatment guidance or proof that ageing cannot be modified.

## D1 — Dementia risk guidance has population, outcome and update limits

**Scope:** selected WHO2026 recommendations for adults living without dementia, including normal cognition or mild cognitive impairment.

WHO's second edition retains a strong physical-activity recommendation with moderate-certainty evidence for reducing cognitive-decline risk in adults with normal cognition. For adults with mild cognitive impairment, its corresponding recommendation is conditional with low-certainty evidence. These statements concern **cognitive decline specifically**. Do not turn them into a guarantee that an individual will avoid dementia, an Alzheimer's-specific prevention estimate or a prescribed exercise plan. [S247]

The physical-activity recommendations are marked validated: the update process reaffirmed prior guidance when new evidence was considered unlikely to alter it. That differs from a newly commissioned comprehensive evidence synthesis. The guideline also explains that, where direct dementia data were absent, cognition evidence informed recommendations. This general method statement does not establish that every specific intervention lacks direct dementia evidence. [S247]

**Original fictional example:** someone sees a strong recommendation and asks whether a new2026 trial proved that exercise prevents all dementia. Explain the population, actual endpoint, certainty and validated status separately. A strong recommendation is not a personal probability; cognitive-test performance, dementia incidence, independence and biomarkers need distinct evidence.

The selected study does not provide an individualized risk score, all risk-factor recommendations or the latest primary trial appraisal. Beneficial general health actions can coexist with residual dementia risk. Symptoms or functional change require appropriate assessment rather than reassurance based on a prevention checklist.

**Reading:** WHO, Risk reduction of cognitive decline and dementia: guidelines, second edition2026, ISBN9789240123557; selected viewer12–15 (printedx–xiii),44–49 (printed18–23). Root visually verified viewer13 and15 for update wording and recommendation alignment. Full guideline/trials not read. Concise original factual summary; no recommendation table, tool or algorithm reproduced. The source is CC BY-NC-SA3.0IGO; the repository does not relicense it or imply WHO endorsement.

## D2 — Diagnostic tools and marketed prevention claims answer different questions

**Scope:** a selected Alzheimer's-related diagnostic example and evidence checks, not population screening or treatment eligibility.

The FDA's May2025 clearance concerns a named plasma-ratio assay used to help identify amyloid pathology in symptomatic adults aged55 or older in specialized care. The result must be interpreted with clinical information. FDA explicitly says it is not a screening or standalone diagnostic test. A different assay sharing an analyte name does not inherit that intended use. Accuracy in cognitively impaired participants cannot simply be assumed for healthy consumers. [S428]

There is also a specific calibrator-lot correction/recall record, checked2026-10-17. It concerns potentially falsely elevated ratios and clinical review of affected results. Ask the laboratory/ordering clinician whether an applicable result or lot is affected; do not reinterpret the raw ratio, assume every test is invalid or ignore the correction because an assay was cleared. The record was open/classified at this check. [S429]

An ability to detect a pathological feature is not proof that screening healthy people prevents disease or improves long-term outcomes. The decision needs the person's symptoms/context, intended purpose, downstream actions and potential false-result harms. Likewise, marketed supplements or “cures” require evidence for their exact prevention/treatment claim; FDA's warning about unproven products does not serve as a comparative trial of every ingredient. [S679; see F3]

**Original fictional example:** a healthy person asks for a blood panel and supplement to guarantee Alzheimer's prevention. These sources do not support that promise or indiscriminate testing. Discuss the actual question and relevant risk/clinical context. Do not confuse diagnosis, risk reduction, symptom treatment and disease-modifying treatment; current therapies and eligibility need their own applicable labels/evidence.

**Reading:** FDA clearance population/interpretation/risk paragraphs and product-specific recall record rechecked; general unproven-product warning read. No raw clinical dataset, full device validation or local screening policy appraised. The older generic “no treatment” wording on a separate fraud article is not adopted as current therapeutic guidance.

## EN1 — Endometriosis diagnosis is an inference with limits

**Scope:** general diagnostic understanding and clinician discussion, not an individual pathway or treatment order.

Symptoms, history, examination and imaging can support a working clinical diagnosis; this is a different statement from histologic confirmation. Cyclical or persistent pelvic symptoms, painful sex, bowel/urinary pain or infertility can raise suspicion but are not individually diagnostic. A diary can help describe timing and impact; ESHRE does not establish that diaries shorten diagnostic delay. [S363]

A normal examination does not end the assessment. ESHRE supports imaging in the diagnostic work-up while warning that negative imaging does not exclude disease, particularly superficial peritoneal lesions. Imaging-supported ovarian/deep disease and a scan that misses superficial lesions answer different questions. Negative histology also does not wholly exclude disease. [S363]

ESHRE discusses laparoscopy and imaging alongside empirical treatment as options without evidence of overall superiority of one strategy. Discussion should cover purpose, uncertainty, procedure risks, preferences and clinical circumstances. Improvement with treatment is not a stand-alone confirmatory test or exclusion of other causes. WHO likewise says surgery is not necessarily required before treatment. [S363, S361]

The2022 guideline advises against diagnosis using the assessed tissue/fluid biomarkers. WHO's2025 overview calls newer tests emerging; this is not validation of a named commercial assay or an update of every diagnostic recommendation. WHO's April2026 development call concerns a future guideline, not completed new recommendations. [S363, S361, S680]

**Original fictional example:** persistent cyclical symptoms with a negative ultrasound warrant a context-sensitive clinical discussion; neither “definitely excluded” nor “everyone needs surgery next” follows from this card. State whether the claim is suspicion, imaging support, surgical visualization or histologic confirmation, and what uncertainty remains.

**Reading:** ESHRE2022 selected recommendation and diagnostic passages, particularly pp9,21–22,27–30; WHO2025 diagnosis/treatment and2026 development status. Not full guideline or underlying-trial appraisal. Later diagnostic advances need current usable independent evidence.

## EN2 — Pain, organ function, fertility, recurrence and test utility differ

**Scope:** deciding what a result supports; not selecting surgery, fertility treatment or a diagnostic product.

Endometriosis care has several outcomes: pain, everyday function/quality of life, organ-specific health and fertility. A pain score falling does not establish restored organ function or a higher live-birth chance. Fertility outcomes such as conception, ongoing pregnancy and live birth must be named rather than collapsed into “success.” Persistent pain alone also does not establish a specific lesion finding. [S363]

ESHRE describes surgery as an option for associated pain. Its deep-disease recommendation about possible pain/quality-of-life improvement is weak and calls for expert care and discussion of risks, benefits and longer-term quality of life. It is not a guarantee of complete pain resolution, organ recovery or improved fertility. Recurrence reports can concern symptoms, detected disease or both; their definitions and follow-up must remain visible. [S363]

For a new diagnostic model, separate discrimination/accuracy, independent validation and usefulness in care. A selected serum-miRNA report used repeated internal cross-validation in surgically characterized cohorts and acknowledged lack of independent external validation. It concerns that serum model, not a different salivary assay or population screening. The source's selected methods/limitations were studied in the earlier packet; it was not fully reappraised here. [S644]

**Original fictional example:** an internally tested classifier has a high score and a user asks whether it can replace specialist imaging or improve fertility. Request independent prospective evidence in the intended population, a suitable reference standard, handling of indeterminate results and evidence that using it improves meaningful care outcomes. A positive model benchmark alone cannot answer those questions. These are evidence requirements, not a claim that every new test fails.

**Reading:** selected ESHRE pain/deep-surgery and infertility passages, including pp10,30,53–54,58–59,84,91; not every page in those chapters or cited studies. Serum-model reading remains inherited S644 scope. NEJM-derived saliva metrics are excluded under current source-use policy; their bibliography is a permission pointer, not active evidence or a source to obtain through a mirror.
