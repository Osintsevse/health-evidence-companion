# Medicines: classes, labels and checks

Sources: S10-S16, S21, S25-S27, S37, S41. Educational information, not a personal prescription.

Before describing a product as effective, apply module 18's indication-specific evidence workflow. Registration, an appealing mechanism and a brand's presence/absence on a discovery list are different from demonstrated benefit. Keep unknown or pending appraisal explicit.

## Identify the exact product

Check brand, international nonproprietary name (INN), dosage form, strength/concentration, route and country. Use product-only packaging text or a registration identifier where appropriate; do not solicit an identifiable document. Standard, Forte, syrup, spray and modified-release versions may have different labels. For Serbia, use ALIMS PIL/SmPC; for Russia, GRLS. SmPC provides professional product information; PIL explains use to patients. Registration is not proof of current pharmacy availability. [S12-S16]

## Adult label examples

These illustrate label verification, not a personal regimen. Recheck the current local product before relying on them.

The ALIMS PDFs were examined in the earlier source edition but could not be retrieved again during this review's access audit. Their filenames/age do not establish current registration or label currency. Do not present a stored example as a freshly verified local instruction when current retrieval fails.

- Standard paracetamol 500 mg tablets: the NHS source describes 500-1,000 mg per dose, at least four hours between doses and a maximum of 4 g in 24 hours. This is that source's upper limit, not a target dose. Weight under 50 kg, liver disease and other factors require a separate limit check. Include paracetamol hidden in cold products in the total. [S10]
- Standard ibuprofen 200/400 mg tablets: the NHS source describes 200-400 mg up to three times daily, at least four hours between doses, and an OTC maximum of 1,200 mg/day. Use the lowest effective dose. Food does not eliminate bleeding risk. Do not apply this regimen to modified-release products. [S11]
- Tantum Verde Forte 3 mg/mL: the retrieved Serbian PIL describes two to four sprays per application for adults, two to six times daily; one spray is 0.17 mL. Assessment is advised if there is no improvement after three days. That label excludes under-18s and includes NSAID allergy restrictions and asthma precautions. Verify the current leaflet against the exact product. [S14]

## Explain a prescription or label

Check indication and guideline support; single/daily dose, interval, route, duration and adjustments; all ingredients including OTC/as-needed products, supplements and alcohol; duplicate ingredients/classes, contraindications and interactions; expected benefit, adverse effects and follow-up. For missed doses, use the exact label rather than automatically doubling.

A negative result in one interaction source is not proof of safety. Sertraline with NSAIDs may increase bleeding risk; serotonergic combinations require assessment for serotonin toxicity. These are label examples, not a statement that an actual person takes sertraline. [S27]
