# Treatment evidence, homeopathy and an indication-specific watchlist

General information; reviewed 2026-10-06. This is a prevention workflow for unsupported recommendations, not a permanent brand blacklist. It records what was inspected and what remains unassessed.

## Evidence before a positive recommendation

Identify the ingredient/product, formulation, indication, population, comparator and meaningful outcome. Inspect current applicable guidance and relevant systematic reviews; examine original trials when needed. Keep regulatory registration, an authorized indication, a plausible mechanism and demonstrated clinical benefit separate. Absence from the FDA register, foreign origin or inclusion in a national medicine list alone cannot prove effectiveness or ineffectiveness.

Use explicit dispositions:

| Disposition | Meaning | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Supported for a defined use | Applicable benefit/harm evidence supports that use | Explain scope and limitations; do not generalize to other diseases |
| Recommendation against a defined use | An inspected guideline advises against it in that setting | Report the recommendation's strength and reason |
| Adequate benefit not established | Evidence is insufficient, biased, indirect or inconsistent | Do not positively recommend as effective; explain the gap |
| Uncertain or conditional benefit | A possible benefit remains uncertain or context-dependent | Describe effect/harms/quality; avoid calling it a cure or universally useless |
| Appraisal pending | Exact identity or current evidence body has not been adequately reviewed | State this; retrieve evidence before endorsing it |

These are not automated safety verdicts. For prescribed medicines, explain concerns and a clinician/pharmacist question without automatic stopping or substitution. Funded trials are not automatically invalid; examine their design, missing outcomes, conflicts, replication and population match. PubMed indexing, a trial registration or a positive abstract alone is not an efficacy certificate.

## Homeopathy

Homeopathy uses serial dilution and a distinct treatment theory; it is not synonymous with herbal medicine or all low-dose pharmacology. Labels may use X/D or C/CH potencies, but product identity still needs verification. Some marketed homeopathic products contain clinically relevant ingredients, alcohol or manufacturing contaminants; being highly diluted does not establish product safety. [S112, S114]

The Australian Natural Therapies Review 2024 report, released through the government site in 2025, found little or no benefit versus placebo for most priority outcomes with available evidence in its assessed populations. Certainty ranged from moderate to very low; it was not an exhaustive review of every condition, and safety/cost-effectiveness were not assessed. Do not overstate it as proving every conceivable claim false. Its plain-language summary and selected methods were read, not all 221 pages. [S113]

NCCIH's page still refers to a new Australian review as underway, although the newer report is available. Use the report for its findings, rather than repeat that stale publication-status sentence. FDA says no homeopathic product is FDA-approved; that is US regulatory status, separately from the efficacy assessment. Do not recommend homeopathy as an effective treatment or substitute for indicated care, rescue treatment or vaccination. [S112-S114]

| Kagocel for routine colds | A clinical report in adults with diabetes claims benefit; only its indexed abstract was available here | Appraisal pending; do not generalize a selected population or mechanism to routine colds | S120 |
| Anaferon for routine adult colds | Retrieved ultra-high-dilution trial concerns adjunctive schizophrenia treatment, not a cold efficacy assessment | Appraisal pending; do not treat an unrelated trial as cold evidence or claim there are no studies | S121 |
| Cerebrolysin/Cortexin added to standard acute-ischaemic-stroke care | Cochrane 2023: probably little/no mortality benefit; functional outcomes insufficiently reported; Cerebrolysin may increase non-fatal serious adverse events | Do not promise stroke recovery from these data or claim harmlessness; urgently route suspected stroke to care | S125 |

The four named routine-cold candidates with pending appraisal are not completed findings of ineffectiveness. They receive no positive routine-cold recommendation from this package without an adequate indication-specific review. Old reviews are dated starting points: inspect updates, corrections and new independent evidence for a new consequential question.

## Secondary discovery lists and further candidates

Encyclopedia Pathologica, the NCHPL popular article and Russian Wikipedia can help locate names and references. They are secondary discovery sources, not the authority for a drug verdict. Do not import their prose, publication counts or blanket class labels. The harmlessness claim in one list is unsafe to repeat; lack of demonstrated benefit does not remove adverse effects. The NCHPL rimantadine example conflicts with the inspected current CDC advice. [S122-S124, S128]

Further candidates for indication-specific appraisal include Actovegin, Solcoseryl, vinpocetine/Cavinton, glycine, phenylpiracetam/Fenotropil, meldonium/Mildronate, inosine/Riboxin, essential phospholipids/Essentiale, ademetionine/Heptral, azoximer bromide/Polyoxidonium, Wobenzym and Sinupret. These names are **unassessed leads**, not a completed ineffective/unsafe classification. Verify ingredient, formulation and claimed indication before seeking the current primary evidence; no routine endorsement follows from this queue. [S122-S124]

Two checked counterexamples prevent a blanket ban:

- EMA's 2012 trimetazidine review retained a restricted adjunctive stable-angina indication while removing tinnitus/vertigo/vision uses. Recheck the current local label; this historical review does not establish every marketed benefit. [S126]
- The inspected UK Nootropil 1200 mg SmPC lists adult cortical myoclonus with other therapies. That authorized indication differs from general memory/learning claims. It is not a new efficacy systematic review or a reason to start/stop piracetam independently. [S127]

All four routine-cold candidates above remain pending even when a discovery site calls them ineffective. A failed lookup is an access limit, not proof of absent evidence.

## Do not create new false blacklists

Vaccines, established allergy treatments, indicated antivirals, genuine replacement therapy for a deficiency and other evidence-supported medicines must not be dismissed because another marketed use is unsupported. Do not label every vitamin, herbal product, probiotic, biologic or immunotherapy ineffective as a class. Likewise, a symptom-relief drug need not reduce mortality to have a useful indication: choose outcomes that match the claim.

For a new product, use a structured card: exact identity -> claimed use -> current recommendation -> benefit/harm and certainty -> applicability/conflicts -> disposition -> source/version/section/check date -> remaining gap. If current retrieval fails or only advertising is available, say evidence was not verified and do not fill the gap with a confident recommendation.
