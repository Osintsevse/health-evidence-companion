# Methods, populations and follow-up

Selected study 2026-10-17. These original notes deepen existing GI, endometriosis and child-growth material. Read the complete relevant heading, including its limits. They do not supply an individual testing, prescribing, plotting or feeding protocol.

## GI-M1 — A negative result has a target and test conditions

Initial assessment of H. pylori infection and confirmation after eradication are different questions. Antibodies may persist after successful treatment, so a later positive antibody result does not by itself establish persistent infection or eradication failure. Breath, stool-antigen and biopsy-based methods have different requirements and uses. A visually normal endoscopy does not tell us which biopsy tests were performed. [S732; S733]

The studied IDkit label gives a concrete method example: PPIs, antimicrobials and bismuth can compromise a negative breath result; a positive result during PPI exposure can still be informative. It also identifies other urease-producing organisms and achlorhydria as possible false-positive contexts. These are possibilities, not proof of the cause of an individual's result. Do not equate every acid suppressant with a PPI. [S731]

Identify the actual medicine, assay/device, preparation, treatment history and report before deciding what a negative result excludes. ACG's dated 2024 professional summary and the company-submitted 2026 label do not have identical timing wording. This is not resolved by silently substituting one for the other: obtain the applicable instructions with the clinician/testing service. No medication interruption or universal preparation interval is supplied here. DailyMed's in-use text may differ from the latest FDA-approved label. [S731; S733; S303]

An infection result does not measure reflux exposure or establish a functional explanation for all symptoms. This is a bounded connection to existing G1/G2, not a new modern GERD diagnostic pathway. [S659; S660]

## REPRO-M1 — Keep extension, efficacy and safety populations separate

The newly inspected EMA sources describe EDELWEISS 3/6, rather than a new independent replication. There were 486 randomized and 484 treated participants in the parent trial. Extension entry, its efficacy set, its safety set and an observed endpoint are different populations. In the continued higher-dose add-back arm, the original treated group numbered 162, the extension safety group 122 and the extension efficacy set 121; the label lists 111 observed efficacy records at Month 12. Do not collapse these into one denominator or classify every difference as the same kind of dropout. [S375; S737]

Reported percentages also need their method. The regulatory report distinguishes a raw Month 3 dysmenorrhea response of 113/156 (72.4%) from a logistic-model estimate of 72.9%. An observed count beside an estimated percentage is not enough to recover its numerator. The exact Month 12 estimation/missingness procedure remains unestablished in this selected review; do not transfer the earlier model or sensitivity methods automatically. [S737]

For the studied Month 6–12 extension, active groups continued treatment and placebo entrants switched to active regimens. There was no continuing placebo comparison during that period. Entry excluded important bone-density deterioration, and some entrants discontinued after their DXA confirmed exclusion. A later high response therefore does not establish the result for all starters, superiority to first-line hormones or indefinite safety. [S375; S737]

The label's Month 12 lumbar BMD mean change of −1.10% (95% CI −1.79 to −0.41) concerns 86 participants with paired DXA readings. It is neither the result for all 162 original recipients nor an individual fracture-risk prediction. The report records post-extension DXA in 129 of 329 follow-up safety participants overall, with recovery patterns differing by skeletal site. Lumbar recovery signals do not establish complete recovery everywhere. [S375; S737]

Treatment-emergent events occurred in the selected extension groups. Zero serious events in one group does not establish zero rare risk, and an event occurring after treatment is not automatically caused by it. Compare population, exposure history, event definition and follow-up before comparing safety percentages. Keep endometriosis data separate from pooled fibroid data. [S737]

## GROWTH-M1 — The measurement and age convention belong to the result

The NHANES 2021 survey manual collects lying length through 47 months and standing height from age two when participants can stand unassisted. That overlap serves its survey protocol; it is not a reason to postpone the US clinical WHO-to-CDC chart transition at 24 months. Measurement method, indicator and reference population can change classification. Serial valid measurements and clinical context are needed to interpret a change. [S734; S580]

Alignment, equipment and recording checks matter. Recording to 0.1 cm does not establish that an individual measurement is accurate to 0.1 cm. Do not turn a survey correction or a population-average lying/standing difference into an automatic individual conversion. [S734; S580]

For preterm growth plotting, identify the actual chart, version and paper or digital implementation. RCPCH's 2009 paper instructions and current digital documentation differ: the digital document describes the former endpoints in corrected years, then states its own policy of correction throughout childhood, including term gestations. This is documentation of an implementation choice, not a universal clinical recommendation or evidence of improved outcomes. A developmental-age convention does not settle a growth-plotting convention. Chronological age still governs clinical routing. [S735; S736]

Record measurement dates, raw values/units, technique, indicator, chart/version, gestation and the age convention actually used. A band crossing alone neither proves poor growth nor excludes a real problem; it does not prescribe extra calories. [S580; bounded inference]
