# Foundations and learning index

Checked 2026-10-08. Educational scope only: these selected concepts do not represent a completed medical curriculum, practical training, diagnosis, or treatment advice. Clinical questions require current, applicable guidelines and local medicine labels.

## Selected concepts

### Homeostasis
Homeostasis is the active maintenance of an internal environment within workable limits, rather than an unchanging state. Negative feedback contributes by opposing a disturbance; hormone secretion provides examples of this regulation, alongside control by other hormones and nervous signals. [S154, S155]

### Anatomical organization and function
Cells contribute to tissues, several tissue types cooperate within an organ, and organs participate in systems. Understanding an organ therefore requires both its arrangement and the roles of its components: the stomach combines lining, supporting, muscular and nervous tissues. [S156]

### Enzymes and metabolism
Metabolism comprises the body's chemical reactions, including breakdown processes that release energy and building processes associated with growth. Enzymes make reactions proceed faster by reducing activation barriers; a reaction can be energetically favorable yet proceed very slowly without an effective catalytic route. [S154, S157]

### Pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics
Pharmacokinetics connects administered dose to exposure through absorption, distribution, metabolism and elimination; pharmacodynamics connects exposure at a target to biological effects. Dose alone therefore specifies neither concentration at the target nor the resulting therapeutic and toxic effects. [S158]

### Apparent volume of distribution
Apparent volume of distribution relates the amount of drug in the body to its measured plasma concentration. It is a model quantity influenced by binding and tissue distribution, so it should not be interpreted as a literal anatomical fluid volume. [S159]

### Penetrance and expressivity
Penetrance describes how often people carrying a particular variant show the associated disorder; incomplete penetrance means some carriers do not show it. Expressivity instead describes differences in manifestations among affected people, so the same genetic condition does not imply an identical clinical course. [S160]

### Variants of uncertain significance
A variant of uncertain significance indicates that available evidence does not establish whether the variant is related to a health condition. Additional population observations, functional studies or family information may help resolve classification; uncertainty itself establishes neither pathogenicity nor harmlessness. [S161]

### Pretest and posttest probability
A test result updates a probability estimated before testing; it does not have the same meaning in every population or clinical context. The likelihood ratio compares the probability of the observed result with disease against its probability without disease: posttest odds = pretest odds × likelihood ratio, with odds = p/(1-p) and p = odds/(1+odds). Sequential multiplication requires conditional independence of the tests. [S162, S163]

### Synthetic Bayesian example
With an assumed pretest probability of 10% and an assumed positive likelihood ratio of 5, prior odds are 1/9 and updated odds are 5/9, giving a posttest probability of approximately 36%. These invented inputs illustrate why a positive result need not establish a diagnosis; they are not performance estimates for a real test. Formula sources: [S162, S163]; arithmetic is an original synthetic exercise.

## Retrieval and rights procedure

For a new concept, identify one question, inspect its exact source section, then write an original card with prerequisites, explanation, exceptions and unresolved questions. Record source ID, edition, section locator, actual reading status, check date, jurisdiction, limitations and rights status. Keep mechanism explanations separate from demonstrated clinical benefit.

Store original prose and bibliographic metadata. Do not import full chapters, slides, images, recordings or question banks: public accessibility does not establish redistribution rights. MIT materials include third-party assets with separate restrictions; WebPath reserves redistribution rights; WONCA identifies noncommercial/share-alike terms. No OpenStax content is included in these concept cards or any source corpus.

A source failure remains a failure, not full-text appraisal. Recheck current sources for consequential clinical questions. Keep actual patient information and private archive links outside this public learning index.
