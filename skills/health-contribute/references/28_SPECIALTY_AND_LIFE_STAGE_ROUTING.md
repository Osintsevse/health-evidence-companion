# Specialty and life-stage routing

## Scope and response

General education, urgency recognition and preparation for qualified assessment. These cards do not establish diagnoses, select prescription treatment, provide medical clearance or replace examination. Apply local services and product guidance; UK service timings and US policies are not universal. Ask only information needed for the current decision.

Answer with urgency first, a brief explanation, one concrete next step, relevant uncertainty and a reassessment trigger. Include module 19's informational disclaimer. Do not bury emergency routing in a long differential or test list. Lists below are not exhaustive. Images and reported observations cannot establish absence of red flags.

## Pediatrics: illness, growth and development

Trigger for questions about anyone under 18. Establish exact age, caregiver relationship, main symptom, duration, hydration and responsiveness. Use pediatric guidance rather than scaling adult treatment; medication discussion requires age-appropriate formulation and professional/local-label checks.

An infant under 3 months with measured temperature at least 38°C needs prompt clinical assessment because this is a high-risk finding. In ages 3–6 months, at least 39°C is an intermediate-risk finding; lower temperatures do not exclude serious illness. Fever with a non-blanching rash, impaired responsiveness, seizures or breathing difficulty requires urgent escalation. Do not manufacture a complete remote traffic-light assessment from missing observations or issue blanket laboratory panels. [S164]

For development, distinguish observation from screening and diagnosis. Age-specific milestone lists can help caregivers describe concerns; they do not replace validated screening. Missing milestones, loss of previously acquired skills or persistent caregiver concerns should prompt pediatric discussion and appropriate assessment rather than reassurance to simply wait. Local early-intervention pathways differ. [S182]

For growth, ask which age-appropriate chart and measurement method were used before interpreting a percentile. The US CDC recommends WHO standards from birth to 2 years and CDC charts thereafter; this is not a universal national policy. Preserve measurement dates and units. Do not apply adult BMI thresholds or diagnose a growth disorder from a single unverified measurement. The latter are operating safeguards, not a validated diagnostic algorithm. [S185]

## Pregnancy, postpartum and gynecology

Trigger on pregnancy possibility, confirmed pregnancy, recent delivery, pelvic pain or abnormal vaginal bleeding. Establish pregnancy possibility and gestational/postpartum stage before applying adult medicine advice.

Routine navigation: encourage contact with local maternity services when pregnancy is known. Explain what booking visits, blood-pressure checks, selected laboratory tests and scans are intended to assess. Screening is a choice and does not itself establish a diagnosis. Use the local schedule and risk-based plan; do not import NHS appointment counts or generate an extra testing package. A new concern need not wait for the next scheduled visit. [S183]

During pregnancy and within a year after delivery, severe persistent headache, visual changes, chest pain, difficulty breathing, fainting, severe abdominal pain, heavy bleeding or thoughts of harming oneself/the baby require immediate medical attention. Reduced fetal movement needs prompt maternity assessment; do not invent a universal normal movement count. [S165]

Early-pregnancy pain or bleeding warrants assessment through an appropriate pregnancy service; urgency depends on clinical condition. Pregnancy location and viability cannot be established from a chat description, home-test photograph or isolated hCG result. Ultrasound and sequential assessment can be needed; guideline treatment thresholds are not self-management instructions. [S166]

Postpartum care should include the parent's physical recovery and mental wellbeing as well as the baby's care. Prepare questions about pain, healing, bleeding, bladder/bowel symptoms, feeding support and contraception. The NHS describes a parent check at 6–8 weeks, but this is a jurisdiction-specific example, not permission to defer new symptoms until then. [S184]

Any postmenopausal bleeding, including one small episode, needs medical assessment. This does not imply cancer is the likely cause. Explain possible examination and targeted testing; avoid general hormone or tumor-marker panels. [S167]

Contraception discussions should support voluntary, informed choice: preferences, reversibility, effectiveness, side effects, access and relevant medical conditions matter. Most methods do not protect against STIs; condoms can add protection. After unprotected sex or a method failure, prompt pharmacy/clinical advice matters because emergency contraception is time-sensitive. Check exact local options, interactions and eligibility rather than selecting a prescription method remotely or assuming breastfeeding alone prevents pregnancy. Do not extrapolate US device durations to every local product. [S181]

## Urology

Sudden severe testicular pain, particularly with nausea or abdominal pain, requires emergency evaluation; delay can threaten the testicle. Do not reassure from a photograph or advise waiting for routine ultrasound. [S168]

Inability to urinate or severe lower abdominal pain requires immediate assessment. Difficulty emptying the bladder can also be chronic and less conspicuous. Ask about duration and relevant medicine changes to support assessment, without stopping prescriptions or diagnosing obstruction remotely. [S169]

## Dentistry

Tooth pain or suspected abscess should route to dental care. Difficulty breathing, speaking or swallowing, extensive mouth swelling, or associated eye symptoms requires emergency assessment. Home measures must not delay definitive care. [S170]

For most localized pulpal/periapical pain and swelling, definitive dental treatment is central; antibiotics are not an automatic substitute. Systemic involvement, immune status and other exceptions matter. Do not supply antibiotic prescriptions or generalize one dental indication to every infection or prophylaxis situation. [S171]

Prevention includes regular cleaning with fluoride toothpaste, cleaning between teeth and dental review. The NHS advises brushing twice daily and spitting without rinsing afterward. Age-specific toothpaste amount, fluoride concentration and caregiver supervision need an appropriate pediatric/local source before giving instructions; adult advice is not a complete infant dental protocol. [S170]

## Ophthalmology and vision exercises

New flashes or floaters, a dark curtain/shadow or sudden blurred vision require urgent eye assessment. A normal-looking external-eye photograph cannot exclude retinal disease. [S172]

Separate specific diagnosed binocular disorders from marketing claims about general vision improvement. Prescribed orthoptic exercises may help selected eye-movement problems such as convergence insufficiency. This does not establish that exercises reverse myopia, prevent its progression or treat dyslexia. Do not prescribe patching, prisms or exercise schedules without an eye assessment. [S173]

## Dermatology photographs

A voluntarily supplied photo may help describe visible appearance and prepare questions; it cannot reliably exclude malignancy, identify a pathogen, establish lesion depth or replace palpation, dermoscopy or biopsy when indicated. These are operating limits, not a claim that an AI skin classifier has been clinically validated.

For clinician-directed photography, suggest good light, focus, no filters, and orientation, surrounding-skin and close-up views; a ruler can show scale without obscuring the lesion. Use the clinician's secure submission method. Never request unnecessary intimate images or put actual images in the public corpus. Photography guidance does not validate a remote diagnosis. [S174]

## Radiology, MRI and X-ray

Trigger on scans, radiology reports or requests to choose imaging. Prefer explaining the written report in clinical context. Separate what the report states, what remains unresolved and questions for the ordering clinician. Do not label a scan normal or exclude urgent disease from a selected screenshot; complete studies, appropriate sequences/views, clinical history and professional interpretation may be required.

MRI does not use ionizing radiation, but is not risk-free: implants, foreign bodies, heating and other issues require the imaging service's screening. Do not personally clear an implant for MRI. [S175]

X-ray/CT radiation estimates vary by procedure, equipment and patient; typical adult doses are not the patient's measured dose or personalized cancer-risk calculation. Discuss whether a test answers a management question and whether an alternative is appropriate, without discouraging necessary imaging. [S176]

## Allergy and pulmonology

Rapid airway symptoms, throat/tongue swelling, breathing difficulty or faintness after a possible allergen require emergency action. If an adrenaline auto-injector is available, use it according to instructions and seek emergency help. Do not replace this with an antihistamine or prolonged chat. [S177]

Do not promote food IgG panels as diagnostic tests for food allergy or intolerance. Avoid broad restrictions based on these results; ask about reproducible exposure, timing and symptoms and route targeted evaluation. Sensitization testing and clinical allergy are not interchangeable. [S95, S22]

Recurrent wheeze, cough or breathlessness needs a structured history and, when feasible, appropriate objective testing before confirming asthma. Acutely unwell people need assessment/treatment without waiting for diagnostic tests. Normal examination or tests affected by existing inhaled corticosteroids do not settle every case. Age-specific pathways differ, especially below age 5. NG245 is a chronic diagnosis/management guideline, not a complete acute-attack protocol. [S178]

## Injury rehabilitation and evidence-based physiotherapy

Establish injury diagnosis, surgery, healing stage and clinician-imposed weight-bearing or movement restrictions before discussing rehabilitation. Explain goals, adherence, pacing and progress measurement; do not invent return-to-sport dates or override restrictions. New major functional loss, severe worsening or concerning neurological symptoms requires assessment before progression.

Traumatic injury rehabilitation favors an individualized plan with functional goals and appropriately timed exercise. Dose, intensity and progression depend on the person and injury; early exercise recommendations are not permission to load an uncleared fracture or repair. Evidence supports broad components but does not establish one optimal exercise program for every injury. [S179]

For adult low back pain/sciatica, support appropriate activity and exercise within the clinical plan. NICE advises against routine imaging in non-specialist care and limits specialist imaging to situations where results are likely to change management. Its recommendations do not support therapeutic ultrasound, traction or TENS for this indication; manual therapy is considered within a package including exercise. These indication-specific conclusions are not a blanket verdict on every physical modality or disease. [S180]

## Evidence limits and review

This is a routing/navigation module, not a systematic review or clinically validated decision system. Selected guideline excerpts and official patient/society resources were inspected; full guidelines, underlying trials and local service pathways were not comprehensively reviewed. Source metadata records reading depth and access failures. Maintainer review must check applicability, red-flag wording and local differences before release. A checked date does not imply a new guideline edition.
