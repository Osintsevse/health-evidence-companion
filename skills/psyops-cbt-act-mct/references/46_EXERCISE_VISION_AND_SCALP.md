# Studied questions: movement, massage devices, vision and scalp products

## M2 — Pain reduction does not establish restoration of everyday function

**Scope:** selected adult workplace neck-pain evidence; shoulder complaints require their own assessment. Start with M1 for symptom context, trauma, neurological and systemic warning features.

One 2021 cluster-randomized trial compared ergonomics plus supervised neck exercise with ergonomics plus health promotion in Australian office workers. Neck pain improved more in the exercise package at 12 weeks; a between-group difference was not evident at 12 months. Only 367 of 740 starters supplied all three pain measurements; long-term exercise continuation was low. Two participants reported musculoskeletal symptoms and completed after physiotherapist follow-up. The report evaluates pain, not a neck-specific disability outcome; productivity reported elsewhere is not everyday physical function. It cannot establish a home routine, a recovery deadline or benefit for a shoulder diagnosis. [S695]

A separate NHS shoulder service distinguishes movement from later strengthening and encourages gradual observation. This is local service advice, not a comparative trial estimate. [S693] A general follow-up discussion may track a meaningful daily task, pain trend, tolerability and new symptoms. That suggestion is not a validated scale or personalized prescription. New concerning symptoms, worsening function or persistent limitation need reassessment under the appropriate local pathway.

**Reading and limits:** S695 researcher full article; root selected methods, outcomes, missingness, harms and licence. Johnston et al., BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders 22:68 (2021), DOI 10.1186/s12891-021-03945-y; [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), original factual synthesis only. S693 researcher relevant service sections. AWMF full guideline remained inaccessible; older NHS neck guidance has an overdue review date. Current function-focused trials, shoulder-specific evidence and local rehabilitation guidance remain further study.

## R2 — Device cases and electrical recalls answer different safety questions

**Scope:** handheld percussion devices and named chargers; R1 manual-massage evidence cannot establish their benefit or safety.

A 2026 case-report abstract describes severe bilateral retinal injury after a percussion gun was applied directly to the eyes/orbits. It is a serious exposure-specific signal, not an incidence estimate or a source of safe force, speed or duration. Full article methods were not read. [S694]

Official recalls separately describe electrical hazards: Massimo EM003(three reported fires, no reported injuries), HoMedics HHP-715 with manufacturing-year codes 20/21/22(17 overheating reports, including one thumb burn), and the specified Theragun G4 PRO charger(all lots). The FDA record checked after its 2026-10-12 update remained open, with cause under firm investigation. [S696–S698]

For affected HoMedics HHP-715 units, the inspected CPSC notice explicitly directs consumers to stop using or charging the unit and contact the firm for the remedy. [S697] Follow the current recall instructions for the exact affected unit. Model, lot/date code and jurisdiction matter. Reported incidents do not provide a population risk denominator or describe tissue injury from massage. Another model's absence from these notices does not prove safety. These sources do not establish a general clinical clearance, anatomical safe-use protocol or efficacy recommendation.

**Reading and limits:** researcher complete case abstract and regulator notices; root checked source identity, exact affected scope and selected incident/reason text. No device photographs or notice text reproduced. Manufacturer incident reports in a regulator record are not independent clinical experiments. Broader injury surveillance and usable device-specific clinical evidence remain gaps; the search was bounded, not systematic.

## V2 — Screen discomfort is not a diagnosis or proof that vision training works

**Scope:** eye exercises/vision training, rather than general physical exercise. V1's convergence-insufficiency findings remain tied to that diagnosis and measured outcomes.

Dry-eye symptoms and refractive complaints can overlap: blur, discomfort or difficulty focusing do not establish the cause. NEI describes an eye examination for refractive errors and assessment of tears and eyelids for dry eye. Screen breaks may improve comfort; this educational advice is not a trial showing that eye exercises correct refraction or improve eyesight. [S700–S702]

Do not generalize V1's selected convergence trials to myopia, dry eye, reading or every type of digital strain. The present reading does not establish that generic eye exercises all work or all fail. Specific training methods, populations and meaningful outcomes require their own evidence.

The inspected NHS pathway distinguishes persistent symptoms needing assessment from urgent painful/red-eye concerns and immediate sight-change, photophobia or injury concerns. Use the applicable local urgent/emergency service; England's phone numbers and service routes are not international rules. [S699]

**Reading and limits:** NEI refractive-errors page updated 2025-12-19; dry-eye/testing pages updated 2025-08-06; researcher relevant sections, root selected symptoms/exam/screen-context text. NHS reviewed 2025-01-06, relevant sections read. V1 is an inherited selected-source ledger, not original trials reopened here. AAO/NHS-myopia discovery excerpts do not support this card. No current full-text exercise-specific evidence synthesis was completed; generic vision-training efficacy remains unresolved.

## H2 — Match the exact product, age and jurisdiction before discussing evidence

**Scope:** selected US labels and one older original-study abstract; no personal regimen or global treatment ranking. H1 retains scalp alternatives and assessment boundaries.

A label identifies a named product's instructions; it does not alone establish independent comparative effectiveness. The inspected Nizoral ketoconazole 1% OTC label concerns dandruff: directions apply from 12 years; below 12 it says ask a doctor. [S705] The inspected ANI ketoconazole 2% prescription label lists tinea versicolor, not dandruff. This is not a claim about every 2% product or off-label clinical practice. [S706]

The Westminster selenium sulfide 2.25% record lists scalp indications, physician-directed use and unestablished safety/effectiveness below 12. Its marketing category is UNAPPROVED DRUG OTHER; DailyMed inclusion must not be called FDA approval. Its formulation includes urea and zinc pyrithione among the other ingredients. Do not transfer its label to another brand, concentration, country or infant. [S707]

A 2002 open randomized shampoo-comparison abstract concerns severe dandruff/scalp seborrhoeic dermatitis. Full methods, funding/conflicts, detailed harms and clinical importance were not appraised; independence is unestablished. Its percentages are not adopted as a quantitative superiority recommendation. [S704] AAD ingredient navigation similarly does not prove comparative benefit for each listed product. [S703]

**Reading and limits:** researcher relevant label sections and trial abstract only; root checked exact-product/source fit and selected selenium label/status text. Root's Nizoral reopen failed, so no root full-label reading is claimed. Record versions and exact identifiers are in sources.json. Independent product/age-specific efficacy, recurrence, long-term harms and current local labels remain further study.
