## Before use
These are original short adaptations for discussion and self-help purposes, **not a reproduction of licensed worksheets or separately validated interventions**. Rationale: CBT/ACT and available WHO/CCI materials [S14–S15, S38–S39, S55]. The evidence-based nature of a family of methods does not guarantee the effect of each card.

Time is a guide of convenience, not a therapeutic dosage. Use one card at a time and only if it matches the request. You can answer verbally, skip the question, or stop. Avoid distracting practices while driving. Don’t turn exercise into an obligation to “feel right.”

## T01 - Observation and interpretation
**Goal:** to separate event from explanation. **Time:** 3-5 minutes.
1. Record what a camera/microphone could capture.
2. Separately write down the meaning that the mind attributes to it.
3. Separately - feeling and need.
4. Point out what we don’t know yet.
**Limitation:** Camera footage does not exhaust the context and history of the relationship.
**Check:** has it become clearer what fact and what conclusion we are working with?

## T02 — Brief analysis of thoughts
**Goal:** increase the accuracy of a belief. **Time:** 5-10 minutes.
Write down the situation; thought; emotion; supporting facts; facts against or exceptions; more accurate conclusion; next step.
**Limitation:** Do not necessarily seek a positive explanation or argue with the fact of violence or loss.
**Check:** The new wording must address the unpleasant facts, not just reassure.

## T03 - Three explanations
**Goal:** To reduce premature confidence in others' motives.
Name your version, one alternative and one version of “the data is not enough.” For each, identify an observation that would help distinguish them.
**Limitation:** alternatives are hypotheses, not excuses for the partner. Don't endlessly invent innocent explanations for repeated harm.
**Check:** What one question or fact is really needed?

## T04 - Safe behavioral experiment
**Purpose:** To check a specific forecast.
Formulation: “If I do X, I expect Y.” Select a small reversible step, observation method and stopping limit. Then compare the result with the forecast.
**Restriction:** Do not test another's consent, fidelity, medical safety, or dangerous situations. Do not use hidden manipulation.
**Check:** what did you learn even with partial confirmation of the fear?

## T05 - Behavior chain
**Goal:** Find a place for change.
Vulnerabilities before the event → event → thought/body → impulse → action → short-term benefit → longer-term cost → possible other move.
**Limitation:** Explaining the function does not relieve responsibility for harm. Do not complete the chain in the midst of a dangerous conflict.
**Check:** select one link that can actually be changed.

## T06 - Defusion
**Goal:** to notice a thought as a thought, not an order.
Name a repeated phrase. Add: “Now I have the idea that...” Notice what you want to do under its influence. Choose action in an important direction.
**Disclaimer:** This is not a proof of the falsity of an idea, nor is it a way to ignore the facts.
**Check:** has there been at least a little freedom of behavior, even if the thought remains?

## T07 - Support in the present
**Goal:** return attention to the current situation.
With open eyes, name a place and several visible objects; feel the support of the seat or floor; notice one sound. Breathe normally, without holding your breath or forcing it.
**Limitation:** if focusing on the body increases panic or dissociation, leave only external reference points or stop.
**Check:** Is it easier to navigate and choose the next small step?

## T08 — Value → action
**Goal:** move from ideal to behavior.
Choose a quality: honesty, caring, courage, curiosity or your own. Name one action today that expresses this quality. Indicate a possible internal obstacle and readiness to meet it.
**Limitation:** value should not be an imposed “should”. Consider safety and resources.
**Check:** does the action depend primarily on the person himself?

## T09 - Choice with price
**Goal:** accept the ambiguity of the decision.
For two or three options, write down: what I save; what am I losing? what uncertainty remains; what is reversible; What am I missing to choose?
**Limitation:** The table does not calculate the correct life. Do not use when there is an immediate threat as a substitute for safety.
**Check:** What price is the user willing to accept, not the AI?

## T10 - Action Zone
**Goal:** to direct effort where it is possible.
Divide: own actions; questions for cooperation; uncontrollable. For each entry in the first group, select a specific step.
**Limitation:** “I do not control” does not mean “I must agree.” You can change participation or leave.
**Check:** Has the number of attempts to manage other people's feelings decreased?

## T11 - Small activation
**Goal:** regain participation in life.
Choose a small activity out of self-care, human contact, interest, or commitment. Reduce to manageable size. Afterwards, note what was done and the actual effect.
**Limitation:** do not demand pleasure and do not increase the load as punishment.
**Check:** Did the action help you re-engage, even if your mood didn’t change?

## T12 - Problem or repeating circle
**Goal:** distinguish problem solving from rumination.
Ask: Is there a new fact, issue being addressed, and action available? If yes, formulate a task and a step. If not, name the circle and return attention to the chosen activity.
**Limitation:** Do not short-circuit the experience of real loss with the slogan “just don’t think.”
**Check:** Did the reflection produce new information or just another reassurance?

## T13 - Pause in conflict
**Goal:** Prevent impulsive worsening.
Name the overload and offer a pause. If it is safe, agree on a return time. During the pause, reduce arousal, do not compose an accusatory speech.
**Limitation:** The pause should not become a punishment, a threat or a permanent disappearance. In case of violence, do not promise a return to danger.
**Check:** Were you able to return to the topic with greater listening ability?

## T14 - Feeling, impulse, selected action
**Goal:** do not confuse emotion with order.
Name the emotion and urge. Check whether the action corresponds to the real situation and the long-term goal. In a safe situation, try a small alternative response.
**Limitation:** Do not apply “opposite action” to a reasonable danger or breach of consent.
**Check:** Has the action become more proportionate to the facts?

## T15 - A request without pressure
**Goal:** express a need and leave the choice to someone else.
Describe an episode → your experience → an important need → one specific request. Ask whether it is possible, listen to a refusal or a counter offer.
**Limitation:** A well-worded request does not oblige the other person to agree. Don't mask the threat with words about feelings.
**Check:** Is your interlocutor free to say “no”?

## T16 - Our repeating loop
**Goal:** see interaction.
Describe what I do, what I observe in response, and what I do next. Separately note the unknown experiences of the other. Find your possible new move.
**Limitation:** do not equate the contributions of the parties and do not explain violence as a “common dance”.
**Check:** Does the new move reduce the harm or just help you tolerate it better?

## T17 - Context of one's own desire
**Goal:** understand your conditions of intimacy.
Note what facilitates the desire, what interferes, what is neutral and what is still unknown. Include fatigue, safety, expectations and quality of contact if relevant.
**Limitation:** Describe only your own experience. Do not diagnose another and do not promise a universal libido switch.
**Check:** is there a topic for voluntary conversation, and not instructions to the partner?

## T18 - Preferences and Limits
**Goal:** Make sex talk safer.
Separately name: “suits me”, “possibly under conditions”, “does not suit”, “I don’t know yet”. Discussion in itself does not mean agreement to action.
**Limitation:** no persuasion on “no” items, no obligation to fill out a list, no hidden agenda to achieve sex.
**Check:** After the conversation, is it easier for both of you to set and maintain your limits?

## T19 – Attention Week
**Goal:** connect time allocation with what matters.
Roughly recall a week: obligations, rest, intimacy, interest, automatic pastime. Choose one small change in favor of what matters.
**Disclaimer:** This is not a productivity audit or an excuse to take away sleep/rest.
**Check:** Does the change bring life closer to the chosen direction?

## T20 - Regret without self-judgment
**Goal:** Separate loss from self-punishment.
Name what you regret; what was known and possible then; what part of the loss is irreversible; what else can be done now.
**Restriction:** Do not demand gratitude for trauma or create a mandatory lesson.
**Check:** Has there been action or space for grief instead of a new accusation?

## T21 - Preparing for a live session
**Goal:** Make the meeting more specific.
One episode; important experience; two questions; desired change; which is hard to say out loud. You can write a short note just for yourself.
**Restriction:** Do not make an indictment out of the note and do not send it automatically.
**Check:** is it clear what exactly you want to get from the meeting?

## T22 - Contact check
**Goal:** Fix failed conversation with AI.
Ask: “What was useful now, what was missing, where am I too confident or putting pressure?” Admit the specific error and change the following answer.
**Limitation:** do not force the user to reassure the assistant and do not make excuses.
**Check:** Is the change visible in the behavior and not just the apology?

## T23 - Responsibility without self-insult
**Goal:** Admit the mistake and maintain the ability to correct.
Fact of action → real harm → share of responsibility → correction/apology → preventing recurrence. Remove the global sentence “I’m nothing.”
**Limitation:** Compassion does not cancel consequences; do not take on someone else's responsibility.
**Check:** Is there a concrete way to fix it and not just make yourself feel worse?

## T24 - Checking the usefulness of the practice
**Goal:** retain useful tools.
What I tried; what I expected; what happened; what helped; what made it worse; repeat, change or stop?
**Limitation:** One successful day does not establish treatment efficacy, one unsuccessful day does not prove the uselessness of the entire approach.
**Check:** evaluate the impact on life and freedom of action, not just the pleasure of communication.

## General limits
If there is a sharp increase in disorientation, panic, self-harmful impulses or a violation of safety, stop the practice and move on to appropriate support. Moderate discomfort does not in itself mean harm; its meaning is discussed, not imposed.

Trauma exposure, EMDR, sleep restriction, intensive breathing, cold techniques, self-treatment of sexual pain, and clinical plans for self-harm are intentionally not included here. For them, it is impossible to replace the assessment of indications and risks with an attractive card.
