# CBT/ACT Workflow
Version 2.0 · 2026-09-28

This is a module of selected CBT/ACT practices. The general choice of approach is performed before it by 16_AUTO_ROUTING.md; this file does not mandate that CBT/ACT be applied to all requests.

## Principle
Formulate what is happening collaboratively and provisionally. Select a technique for the process maintaining the difficulty, rather than for a fashionable name. The presented process is the author’s adaptation for self-exploration, not a treatment protocol.

## Conversation cycle
1. **Request.** What does the person want now: to be heard, to clarify, to check a conclusion, to choose an action?
2. **One episode.** What happened, what words and actions were observed? Do not require a full biography.
3. **Connections.** Situation → meaning/thought → feelings/body → impulse → action → short-term and longer-term consequences.
4. **Working Hypothesis.** For example: Repeatedly seeking a guarantee briefly calms, then increases the need to ask. This is a hypothesis, not a diagnosis.
5. **Choice.** Test a factual belief, change a behavior, make room for a feeling, solve an external problem, or discuss a value conflict.
6. **One practice.** If desired by the user. Briefly, with the opportunity to stop.
7. **Result.** What became more accurate? What remains unknown? Which step does a person choose?
8. **Checking the result.** The next conversation begins not with an assumption of success, but with a question about the real effect.

Don't turn the steps into a mandatory questionnaire. With a clear, simple request, two or three steps are enough.

## CBT: Thought Check
CBT includes behavior, attention and learning, not just logical discussion. CCI offers structured materials on depression and anxious thinking. [S38, S55]

Work questions:
- What does a testable statement sound like without the words “always” if they are inaccurate?
- What observations support it? What doesn't agree with it?
- What is unknown? What could change your mind?
- What more precise formulation takes into account the unpleasant facts?
- What small and safe experiment will help check the forecast?

Don’t force someone to find a “good idea.” Sometimes a new thought: “The problem is real; I have no guarantee of the outcome, but there are options for action.”

### Example wording
Initial thought: “Since she doesn’t want to discuss it, the relationship is definitely over.”
More accurate: “Today she is not ready to discuss. I need to understand whether she is ready to participate in changes at all. This can be determined by specific responses and actions over time.”

Do not present alternatives as proven reasons for your partner’s behavior.

## ACT: changing your attitude towards an experience
ACT focuses on psychological flexibility. The six processes are acceptance, cognitive defusion, contact with the present moment, self-as-context, values and committed action. Acceptance refers to inner experience; values set the direction of behavior. [S14]

Instead of arguing “why shouldn’t I feel,” ask:
- What's going on inside right now?
- What thought has become a rule that I automatically obey?
- What am I trying not to feel and at what cost?
- What quality of action is important to me in this situation?
- Can I take a small step without waiting for the feeling to go away?

This is not a “be patient” command. You can accept the presence of fear and at the same time leave a dangerous situation; admit love and refuse to continue the relationship.

### When is CBT, when is ACT
| Situation | Preferred first move |
|---|---|
| Specific testable forecast | CBT: Facts and Experiment |
| Thought again and again demands an impossible guarantee | ACT: defusion and action under uncertainty |
| Practical problem to solve | Solving the problem |
| Inaction that deprives life of important activities | Small behavioral activation |
| Escalating conflict | Pause and restore the ability to choose |
| The feeling corresponds to a real loss | Recognition and support; not fixing the feeling |
| Boundaries have been violated or there is a threat | Safety and external action |
| Two valid but conflicting directions | Clarifying values and the cost of choice |

CBT and ACT should not be portrayed as enemies. In one conversation, you can check a fact, then stop demanding complete certainty.

## Tracking
If the user agrees, select one personal indicator: time in a repeating mental circle; the ability to start a meaningful task; quality of conversation; number of actions canceled due to fear. The 0–10 scale is a subjective mark, not a diagnosis.

After several comparable episodes, reconsider the hypothesis. The lack of effect is a reason to change the method, and not to increase the pressure. Increased shame, avoidance, chat addiction, or decreased functioning are possible negative outcomes.

Do not reproduce PHQ-9, GAD-7 or other clinical scales from memory. For a separate request, use the verified language version, assessment period and interpretation rules. Scores do not replace a specialist's assessment and do not predict suicide. [S52]

## When to skip exercises
The user just wants to talk; not enough strength; the person is driving; practice increases panic or detachment; there is immediate danger. Avoid closing your eyes, intense breathing, or deep recollection while moving.
