## F1 — What does a confidence interval describe?

**Scope:** introductory frequentist inference, not an individual's probability of disease.

A point estimate summarizes the parameter estimated from a sample; an interval also represents sampling uncertainty under a stated method and assumptions. A 95% confidence procedure is designed so that, over repeated suitable samples, approximately 95% of its intervals include the fixed target parameter. This is not a statement that 95% of individual observations lie within one interval, nor that a future sample estimate must fall inside it. It is not a Bayesian posterior probability for the parameter. [psychology:S104]

An apparently narrow interval does not repair biased recruitment, systematic measurement error or a mismatch between the study population and the intended application. Identify what was estimated, units, comparator, outcome timing, interval method and relevant design before translating it into ordinary language. Do not use the simple normal approximation indiscriminately for small or extreme proportions, repeated observations or complex sampling. The selected reading introduces an elementary method; other methods require their own assumptions. [psychology:S104]

**Original synthetic illustration:** an invented between-group difference is +1 scale unit, with a 95% interval from -2 to +4. If higher scores are better, this interval is compatible with worse, unchanged and better group-average outcomes under that analysis. It does not predict an individual person's response or establish treatment equivalence. Whether a benefit matters depends on the outcome and a justified meaningful-effect threshold; it cannot be read from the confidence level.

## F2 — Does a small p-value prove an important effect?

**Scope:** statistical interpretation; no clinical treatment verdict follows from this card alone.

A p-value is computed under a specified null model. It describes how unusual the selected statistic, or results at least as extreme in the specified test direction, would be if that model applied. It is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true, that an intervention works, or that a reported effect occurred 'by chance'. State the hypothesis and assumptions rather than converting a p-value into a personal probability. [psychology:S104]

Failing to reject a null hypothesis does not establish that it is true. A study may leave important effects unresolved. Conversely, a very small difference can become statistically detectable in a large sample without becoming practically important. Examine the effect estimate, uncertainty, comparator, outcome scale and time horizon before discussing benefit or harm. Recommendation strength is another judgment and is not a synonym for statistical significance. [psychology:S104]

A Type I error rejects a true null hypothesis; a Type II error fails to reject a false one. A chosen significance threshold controls one error under assumptions; it does not remove every risk of a wrong conclusion. The consequences of mistakes and study design matter. Do not change thresholds after seeing a result to manufacture a favorable conclusion. That last instruction is this project's appraisal safeguard, not a reproduced textbook procedure.

**Original synthetic illustration:** two invented reports have the same small p-value but use different outcomes and report very different effect magnitudes. Their practical value need not be the same. A large p-value with a wide interval also cannot establish 'no meaningful difference'; this packet did not study equivalence/noninferiority methods. Do not claim that another test proves an exactly zero effect: meaningful bounds and exact equality are different claims.

**Reading:** OpenIntro 4e screen-reader selected pp. 211–213, 216–218 and 223–224: hypothesis framework, decision-error definitions, opening p-value definition and statistical versus practical significance. PDF pp. 218 and 224 visually checked. Full calculation exercises, multiplicity, equivalence and complete test-method appraisal were not completed. [psychology:S104]

## G1 — How should recurrent heartburn be framed?

**Scope:** adult symptom education. An acute chest or bleeding emergency takes priority.

Retrosternal burning or regurgitation can suggest reflux, but describing a symptom is not the same as establishing its cause. Review onset, trajectory, timing around meals/lying down, swallowing, vomiting, weight change, bleeding, relevant medicines and effects on sleep/function. NIDDK's adult reflux page describes medicine and pregnancy context; do not stop a prescribed medicine from a possible association alone. [medical:S645]

Upper-abdominal discomfort or early fullness may prompt a dyspepsia assessment. NIDDK describes reviewing symptoms, medicines, prior H. pylori infection and family history, then selecting examinations or tests for plausible causes. Functional dyspepsia is a clinical assessment when another explanation is not found; it must not become a default chat label for unexplained symptoms or a conclusion from anxiety alone. [medical:S660]

Difficulty/pain swallowing, persistent vomiting, appetite loss or unexplained weight loss need medical review. Blood or coffee-ground vomit and black tarry stool require prompt medical attention; fainting, marked dizziness, confusion or other shock features warrant emergency help. A normal home measurement does not clear bleeding. [medical:S645; medical:S661]

New concerning pressure-like chest discomfort, spreading pain, sweating or breathlessness follows the existing heart-attack/chest-emergency pathway. Indigestion-like symptoms do not safely distinguish cardiac from reflux causes; do not delay help for an antacid experiment. [medical:S276]

**Depth/limits:** this card extends NIDDK's July 2020 reflux education with March 2025 dyspepsia diagnosis and July 2024 GI-bleeding sections. It is not a complete current GERD guideline or a global age threshold for endoscopy. Pediatric, pregnancy and local treatment pathways require their own sources. Modern guideline appraisal and outcome-specific treatment comparisons remain pending.

**Next useful question:** what diagnostic decision would change after a particular result, and what alternative remains if it is normal? Avoid a universal test panel.

## G2 — What can reflux investigations establish?

**Scope:** purpose and limitations of selected tests, without ordering a test sequence.

NIDDK describes initial clinical assessment from symptoms/history and considering tests for suspected complications, another plausible cause or persistent symptoms despite treatment. This explains why every person with heartburn does not need the same investigation, while persistent or changing symptoms deserve reassessment. [medical:S659]

Upper-GI endoscopy inspects the lining of the esophagus, stomach and duodenum and can include biopsy. It can investigate visible abnormalities and tissue-level questions. Reflux monitoring measures exposure over time; the NIDDK overview distinguishes catheter assessment of acid/nonacid reflux from capsule measurement of acid exposure, and describes matching the recording to symptoms, diet and sleep. These methods answer different questions. A lining examination is not a recording of every reflux event, and symptom timing is not proof that every symptom has that cause. [medical:S659]

The dyspepsia source describes endoscopy/biopsy for selected causes and separate stool or urea-breath tests for H. pylori. An infection test and a reflux measurement are not interchangeable explanations of upper-GI symptoms. The indication, current preparation requirements, existing medicines and intended follow-up must be checked in applicable guidance; do not invent a medication washout or interpret a result without its method. [medical:S660]

Antacids, H2 blockers and PPIs have different roles in symptom relief/acid suppression, while procedures require separate selection and risk discussion. The stored NIDDK treatment overview is dated 2020. It does not select a product, duration or procedural candidate. Symptom improvement alone does not supply independent proof of causation; persistent symptoms require reconsidering the clinical question. [medical:S646; medical:S659]

## S1 — Why is chronic insomnia more than poor sleep habits?

**Scope:** adult education, with VA/DoD clinical guidance interpreted within its setting.

Adequate opportunity and a reasonably regular schedule support sleep; they do not establish that a disorder is absent. The VA/DoD 2025 guidance distinguishes persistent insomnia with daytime consequences despite suitable sleep opportunity from insufficient opportunity and other sleep disorders. Clinical history matters; a questionnaire does not independently diagnose insomnia. Consider relevant sleep, medical, mental-health and medicine context. Snoring/witnessed breathing interruptions or gasping can change the question toward sleep-disordered breathing rather than more hygiene tips. [medical:S657; medical:S648]

The guideline recommends multicomponent CBT-I for chronic insomnia. Its behavioral and cognitive elements address factors maintaining sleep difficulty; hygiene education can contribute but is not the whole treatment. Recommendation 5 is strong for CBT-I, with moderate confidence in critical sleep outcomes in the accompanying discussion; its update category is 'Not reviewed, Amended'. Recommendation 7 is weak against hygiene education alone, with low confidence and older underlying studies. Current publication date, recommendation strength, evidence confidence and new systematic appraisal are different attributes. [medical:S657]

Treatment can require adaptation. Restricting time in bed may temporarily increase sleepiness; excessive daytime sleepiness, bipolar illness, uncontrolled seizures, instability or acute mental-health concerns can affect suitability and timing. This card does not reproduce the guideline's treatment algorithm, questionnaire or restriction schedule. It cannot authorize self-directed restriction, sedatives or medicine withdrawal. Trained care and a suitable delivery format matter. [medical:S657]

**Reading:** VA/DoD version 3.0, January 2025, evidence through 31 March 2024; selected printed/physical pp. 7–8, 30–31, 34 and 44–50. Drug, full OSA-treatment and instrument appendices were not appraised. NHLBI habits provide dated 2022 background. No universal local protocol or patient clearance is inferred.

**Next:** review applicable local services, coexisting apnea/circadian problems and patient-relevant outcomes; assess exact delivery formats separately rather than calling any app CBT-I.

## C1 — Does better sleep establish general cognitive enhancement?

**Scope:** sleep deficiency, everyday function and outcome interpretation.

NHLBI describes sleep deficiency affecting learning, focus, memory, decisions, reaction time and errors. Unintended dozing and impaired alertness matter during work, driving and other hazardous tasks. Subjective confidence or a consumer score is not proof of safety or a substitute for assessment. This educational account concerns sleep/function; it is not a trial of a cognitive-enhancement product. [medical:S658]

The selected VA/DoD CBT-I discussion primarily reports insomnia severity, sleep efficiency, sleep-onset latency, wakefulness after sleep onset and sleep quality, with some other outcomes in particular studies. Improvement in one of these does not establish increased intelligence, durable everyday memory benefit, dementia prevention or an effect in already adequately rested adults. This is an outcome-applicability inference, not a guideline recommendation against every possible cognitive benefit. [medical:S657]

Separate three questions: correcting inadequate sleep opportunity, treating a diagnosed sleep disorder, and improving performance beyond a person's baseline needs. Each needs an appropriate population, comparator, outcome and time horizon. A change in a wearable-derived sleep stage or trained cognitive task is not automatically meaningful improvement at home, work or school. Use the measurement and evidence-method references before promising transfer.

Persistent impairment merits assessment of the cause, especially with sleepiness or possible breathing interruptions. Chat cannot determine fitness to drive or provide a performance-enhancement regimen. Do not simply add more sleep time or a supplement and infer that the cause has been treated.

**Reading/uncertainty:** NHLBI daytime-thinking/safety sections, updated June 2022, plus the selected VA/DoD insomnia pages above. The original cognitive trials and every sleep intervention were not independently reviewed. A comparative cognitive-outcomes review and meaningful transfer measures remain a study task; missing support here is not proof that an intervention never helps.
