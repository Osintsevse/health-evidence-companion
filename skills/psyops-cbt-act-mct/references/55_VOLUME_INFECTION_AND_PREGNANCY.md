## VOLUME-F1 — Concentration, fluid quantity and effective circulation can differ

A plasma concentration is a ratio. Tonicity concerns effective solutes and water movement across cell membranes. Serum sodium usually informs tonicity, with other-solute and measurement exceptions; it does not identify total body water or sodium inventory. Extracellular fluid quantity and adequate organ perfusion are also different questions. Cardiac output, vascular capacity and movement between capillaries and tissues help determine effective arterial blood volume, used by this review as a functional perfusion concept rather than an interchangeable anatomical volume. [S755]

In the review's progressive heart-failure example, inadequate effective circulation can stimulate renal salt and water retention despite expanding extracellular fluid. This is one mechanism, not a statement that every heart-failure presentation has low output or that every swollen tissue establishes its cause. A concentration result or visible swelling alone cannot settle the different quantities. [S755]

The kidney connects neural and hormonal responses: reduced renal perfusion pressure favors sodium retention; renal sympathetic signaling can release renin. The angiotensin pathway changes vascular tone and tubular handling and stimulates adrenal aldosterone, which increases distal sodium reabsorption. Vasopressin increases collecting-duct water reabsorption through aquaporin-2. Cardiac stretch can release natriuretic peptides that promote sodium/water excretion and oppose renin-aldosterone signaling. These interacting responses explain why concentration alone does not reveal the whole regulatory state. [S754]

## MICRO-D1 — An organism, toxin genes and host injury are different targets

In Eyre's selected adult medical/geratology inpatient study, stool culture recovered C. difficile; sequencing cultured isolates identified toxin genes. Recovery establishes an organism detectable by that method. Genomic toxin potential differs from current toxin activity and intestinal injury, which were not directly measured in these carriers. Disease-associated lineages were found without the study's suspected-CDI symptom pattern. This does not explain why an individual remained well. [S756]

The label asymptomatic needs its definition: participants below the local stool-frequency threshold could still report loose/frequent stool, and authors acknowledged possible mild disease. First sample also differed from admission: enrollment and bowel movements could be delayed, many eligible people returned no stool, and many sampled people provided only one. Do not turn a first positive sample into a clean admission-carriage or hospital-acquisition estimate. [S756]

Ask what specimen and assay target were used, when sampling occurred and what bowel/host context was observed. Neither a modern NAAT accuracy estimate, a universal toxin-negative exclusion rule, an antimicrobial decision nor a transmission-precaution policy follows from this cohort. Detailed toxin-cell effects and host protection require further evidence. [S756]

## MAT-F1 — A gestational hemoglobin concentration needs a stage and a purpose

Pregnancy expands both plasma and red-cell volume; greater plasma expansion can lower hemoglobin concentration while red-cell volume increases. A CBC helps assess anemia, but hemoglobin is not a direct inventory of body iron and a fall alone does not establish its cause. Establish gestational stage, units, laboratory interval, earlier results and the reason for testing before explaining a pattern. [S758, S757]

Tozzo's US retrospective EHR study distinguished gestational reference distributions from longitudinal change. Its reference subset contained 37,709 term pregnancies without specified complications; only 2,791 had all four timepoints for the principal longitudinal analysis. In that longitudinal subset, most pregnancies showed declining hemoglobin between 7–14 and 26–29 weeks. This is a population observation, not proof that an individual's low value is physiological or that iron deficiency is absent. [S757]

The main study intervals retained some anemic pregnancies; intervals excluding anemia were a separate sensitivity analysis. A reference interval therefore is not automatically a diagnostic or treatment boundary. Elevated red-cell indices and uncommon increases were associated with later complications, but neither these associations nor the absence of some low-value associations establishes what changing a result would achieve. Existing treatment may influence observational outcomes. [S757]
