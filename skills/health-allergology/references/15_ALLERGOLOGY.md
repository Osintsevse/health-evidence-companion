# Allergy information and diagnostic limits

General adult education; reviewed 2026-10-06. This module covers information workflows, not individual diagnoses or unsupervised challenges. It complements module 05. Scoped source-use/test-interpretation amendment2026-10-17 follows; other historical sections are not thereby refreshed.

## Recognize urgency before discussing tests

Rapid breathing difficulty, throat/tongue swelling, collapse or circulatory compromise after a possible trigger require emergency care. Anaphylaxis can occur without a rash. Explain immediate use of an already prescribed adrenaline/epinephrine auto-injector according to its action plan and urgent local assistance; do not delay this for antihistamines, photographs or a diagnostic interview. Intramuscular adrenaline is first-line treatment. Antihistamines may relieve skin symptoms but do not replace it; inhaled bronchodilators also do not replace it. Do not improvise an injection regimen or observation/discharge clearance. [S89]

## Terms that change interpretation

For food allergy, sensitization and a reproducible adverse reaction on exposure are distinct. Total IgE does not identify an allergen; allergen-specific IgE alone does not determine clinical causality or reaction severity. Interpret the test with symptoms, exposure history and purpose. False results are possible; do not infer a universal error rate. [S690, S691]

FDA's food-allergen framework is regulatory population evidence, not an individual diagnostic algorithm or guidance for drug/contact allergy. Its separate food-skin-test safety notice means a negative test cannot serve as permission for a home food challenge. Keep the particular test, food/route, timing and reproducibility visible. [S691, S692]

Food IgG/IgG4 panels remain a different question from IgE-mediated food allergy; the stored AAAAI source does not recommend them as a diagnostic basis for food-allergy/intolerance diets. This statement retains its earlier source scope and is not a fresh guideline review. [S95]

For targeted-test selection, named cutoffs, drug/contact allergy, supervised challenge eligibility and current treatment comparisons, retrieve usable applicable clinical guidance. The present packet does not supply these algorithms. Read AR1/AR2 in [the studied allergy and child-care packet](44_LABS_BP_ALLERGY_CHILDREN.md) for exact new reading scopes and gaps.

## A peanut-specific example of test interpretation

The 2020 AAAAI peanut parameter illustrates why a test result needs a clinical context. Published decision thresholds depend on the study population and baseline prevalence; a skin-prick or specific-IgE value alone does not establish peanut allergy. These tests also should not be used to predict reaction severity. A number can therefore change an assessment without settling either diagnosis or future severity. [S643]

Keep this example limited to IgE-mediated peanut allergy. The parameter has conditional recommendations with low or very low certainty, and the selected sections are not a current review of every food or newer test. No threshold, avoidance list or home exposure/challenge follows from this explanation. Mechanistic reading about IgE sensitization is background rather than independent diagnostic validation. [S641, S643]

## Rhinitis and medicine classes

Allergic-rhinitis symptoms can overlap with a cold or nonallergic triggers. Exposure timing and symptom course support assessment questions; they do not establish a diagnosis. The independently inspected NHS overview does not provide a comparative efficacy ranking. Earlier ARIA abstract-derived rankings are suspended pending verification of applicable source-use rights and adequate current evidence. [S21]

First-generation antihistamines can impair alertness; some second-generation agents can still cause drowsiness. Alcohol and other sedatives matter. Nasal decongestants are different drugs with duration limits and rebound-congestion concerns; verify the local label. Wheeze or ongoing breathing symptoms require an asthma assessment rather than a rhinitis-only explanation. Do not give driving clearance or recommend stepping up prescription treatment. [S15, S23-S24, S41, S73]

## Food allergy, hives and medicine allergy

Clinical decisions about avoidance, immunotherapy, biologics, medicine challenges and removing an allergy label require appropriate assessment and usable current evidence. This module provides no home desensitization, challenge or label-removal protocol. Historical EAACI/ARIA/NICE/urticaria locators are retained as bibliographic history rather than permission or completed clinical appraisal.

Hives have several possible explanations; a recurring rash is not automatically a food allergy. The NHS overview can support general signposting, with immediate airway/circulatory danger taking precedence. A full current urticaria treatment algorithm remains further study. [S96]

For a reported medicine reaction, preserve the source and uncertainty. ## Which allergy question would a test answer?

Reviewed 2026-11-24. For symptoms that recur around a particular exposure, ask what changed, how soon symptoms appeared, which organs were affected and whether the exposure has since been tolerated. This history helps an allergist decide whether testing would change management. A positive specific IgE result can indicate sensitization even when the person tolerates the exposure. A food IgG panel cannot establish which foods a person needs to avoid. [S690, S95, S814]

A rash under a watch strap that appears a day after contact raises a different question from sneezing within minutes of meeting a cat. A dermatologist can use patch tests with delayed readings to investigate suspected allergic contact dermatitis. An allergist can select immediate skin-prick or specific IgE testing when the history suggests an immediate allergy. A negative first patch series can leave an untested contact allergen unresolved. [S812, S814]

For troublesome pollen or dust-mite symptoms despite avoidance measures and medicines, a specialist can assess whether a relevant allergen has been identified and whether injection immunotherapy is worthwhile. Discuss the time commitment, possible lasting relief and risk of a systemic reaction. Injections require trained staff and facilities to treat a reaction. This discussion concerns environmental allergy and supplies no protocol for food desensitization. [S813]

## Galitifen syrup and Alvokast tablets in Serbia

These are two distinct products in the Serbian regulator's documents. When a name comes from speech or transliteration, confirm it from the package before applying any product-specific conclusion. A strength below identifies the presentation. It is not a suggested dose.

**Galitifen (ketotifen), 1 mg/5 mL oral syrup.** The Serbian label describes an antihistamine for selected allergic nasal or skin symptoms when a less-sedating oral antihistamine is unsuitable. For rhinitis it also specifies when topical antihistamines or nasal steroids are unsuitable. Its asthma indication is long-term prevention alongside anti-inflammatory treatment in patients with atopic symptoms. It cannot relieve an acute asthma attack. Alertness may be impaired, especially at the start, and alcohol or sedating medicines can increase impairment. The label lists epilepsy, use of oral antidiabetic medicines and breastfeeding among its contraindications. These findings apply to this syrup. An ophthalmic ketotifen product needs its own label. [S806]

**Alvokast (montelukast), 10 mg film-coated tablet.** This leukotriene receptor antagonist has an asthma indication from age 15 in the Serbian label. It is an add-on for specified inadequately controlled persistent asthma and also has an exercise-related asthma prevention indication. The seasonal rhinitis benefit is described in patients for whom montelukast is indicated for asthma. This tablet has no role as a rescue medicine during an attack. Other strengths and formulations require separate verification. [S807]

**Montelukast safety.** The Serbian label asks patients or caregivers to report neuropsychiatric changes to their doctor. The US FDA reserves use for allergic rhinitis for people with inadequate response or intolerance to alternatives. It advises stopping montelukast and contacting a health professional immediately for new mood or behaviour changes, including vivid dreams, agitation or suicidal thoughts. Serious events have also been reported after stopping. The FDA communication informs a safety discussion across borders. Serbian indications come from the Serbian label. [S807, S808]

**How much nasal symptom relief was observed?** A historical US prescribing document summarizes five randomized, blinded seasonal-rhinitis trials in people aged 15–82 over two to four weeks. Four reported a statistically significant benefit over placebo. In the illustrated trial, daytime nasal symptoms improved by an extra 0.13 points on a 0–3 scale with montelukast (95% CI 0.06–0.21 points). This average is small on the measured scale. The trial was not designed to establish superiority over loratadine, and short trials cannot quantify rare psychiatric harms. This is a label-reported trial result, with no independent appraisal of individual trial reports. [S811, S808]

## Other medicines for rhinitis, itchy eyes and hives

| Question | Options and limits |
| --- | --- |
| Sneezing, itching or hives | Oral H1 antihistamines such as cetirizine, loratadine or fexofenadine can relieve symptoms. They are generally less sedating than chlorphenamine or diphenhydramine. Drowsiness is still possible. Check for the same ingredient in a cold product, and consider driving, alcohol and other sedatives. [S809] |
| Intermittent versus persistent nasal symptoms | The Welsh pharmacy pathway for adults and young people aged 12 and over includes oral or nasal antihistamines such as nasal azelastine for intermittent symptoms. For persistent or more troublesome symptoms it includes a nasal corticosteroid such as mometasone or fluticasone, alone or with an antihistamine. Check nasal spray technique and regular use before assuming treatment has failed. A topical decongestant has a short label-defined duration because prolonged use can worsen blockage. [S810, S815, S21] |
| Itchy, watery eyes with allergic rhinitis | The same pathway includes sodium cromoglicate eye drops for allergic eye symptoms. The eye formulation and its label matter. A very painful red eye needs urgent assessment. A red eye with a new change in vision needs emergency assessment. [S810, S478] |
| Throat swelling, breathing difficulty or collapse after an exposure | Treat suspected anaphylaxis immediately, including the prescribed adrenaline auto-injector and emergency services. Antihistamines can relieve itching but cannot treat airway swelling or circulatory collapse. [S89] |

The Welsh options describe a local care pathway. Availability, licensed ages and specific products vary by country. The sources cover symptom relief and selected risks. They do not establish a universal drug ranking, an individualized regimen or an exhaustive interaction screen.
