# Digestion, food tolerance and changes in bowel habits

## When a meal seems to trigger bloating

For an adult with repeated bloating or loose stool, ask what changed, how soon symptoms followed, whether the same food in a different amount has the same effect, and what happens on days without it. Recent infection, travel, new medicines, antibiotic use, bleeding and unintended weight loss can change the next step. A short record of food, amount, symptoms and bowel habit can help a clinician choose a test. An association in a diary alone cannot identify lactose intolerance, celiac disease or irritable bowel syndrome (IBS). [medical:S241, medical:S229, medical:S452, medical:S856]

Lactose malabsorption means that some milk sugar reaches the colon undigested. Lactose intolerance additionally involves symptoms. During a clinician-ordered hydrogen breath test, the rise in breath hydrogen and symptoms during the test answer different questions. For someone with established lactose intolerance, small portions with meals, yogurt, hard cheese or lactose-free milk may be better tolerated. Calcium and vitamin D intake deserve attention if replacing most dairy. Tolerance varies. Lactose-free cow's milk still contains milk proteins and is unsuitable as a substitute for someone with cow's milk protein allergy. [medical:S241, medical:S855, medical:S859]

If wheat-containing meals repeatedly precede symptoms and celiac disease is a possibility, discuss testing **before** removing gluten. Restriction can affect the accuracy of blood testing and intestinal biopsy. Someone who already avoids gluten should tell the clinician how long they have avoided it, so they can plan the evaluation. A home challenge is inappropriate after a suspected immediate allergic reaction. Breathing difficulty or collapse after a food exposure needs emergency care. [medical:S229, medical:S859]

## When a restrictive diet has an end point

Monash University's programme for diagnosed IBS begins with a short, dietitian-guided reduction in fermentable carbohydrates. If symptoms improve, the next step is planned reintroduction to identify tolerable foods and portions. If the trial produces no meaningful improvement, review the diagnosis and other options with the care team. A long list of permanent exclusions after an unsuccessful trial adds burden. The programme describes implementation. It does not tell an undiagnosed individual the probability that their symptoms will improve. [medical:S858]

## Do over-the-counter digestive enzymes improve IBS symptoms?

Bertuccioli and colleagues (23 November 2026) randomized 300 Italian adults with established IBS to low-FODMAP advice with or without a specific multi-enzyme supplement. Among 292 who completed 14 days, mean bloating on a 0–10 scale decreased from 7.55 to 4.50 with the supplement and from 7.70 to 5.66 with diet advice alone. The unadjusted difference in mean change was about one scale point. Staff enrolling participants could see the allocation sequence. Participants knew whether they received a supplement, and two authors reported company roles. There was no placebo, no direct measurement of gas production and no evidence here of long-term benefit, benefit on an unrestricted diet or safety of gluten exposure in celiac disease. This is a short-term signal for further testing. [medical:S860]

## When diarrhea needs a different response

An otherwise well adult with brief watery diarrhea who can drink can replace fluid and electrolytes and return to their usual foods as appetite returns. Someone with kidney disease, diabetes or an existing fluid plan should ask what type and quantity of fluid is appropriate. New diarrhea during antibiotics deserves early clinical advice. Record when it began, frequency, fever, fluid intake, urination, blood and recent medicines. [medical:S856, medical:S857]

Arrange prompt assessment for visible blood, black tarry stools, high fever, severe pain, repeated vomiting or dehydration. New confusion, collapse or severe deterioration needs emergency help. Over-the-counter antidiarrheals are generally discouraged with fever or bloody stool pending assessment. Thresholds for infants and children differ from adult guidance. Recurrent or persistent symptoms need evaluation even when hydration is adequate. [medical:S856, medical:S857, medical:S462]
