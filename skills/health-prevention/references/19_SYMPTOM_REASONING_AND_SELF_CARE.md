# Symptom reasoning, self-checks and basic self-care

General informational workflow; clinical self-care examples are adult unless explicitly stated otherwise. For children, pregnancy and postpartum use module 28 and current life-stage-specific guidance. This is decision support, not a validated clinical protocol. Apply current condition-specific primary guidance. Existing source routes: primary care S01/S05-S06, respiratory S07-S11 and S08-S09, medicine labels S14-S15/S21; these routes do not substantiate every new symptom or treatment.

## Required response boundary

Include a short disclaimer in the user's language in every health-facing answer, including follow-ups: **Informational tool, not intended for use as a medical device; not a diagnosis or prescription and not a substitute for clinical care.** Put emergency action first when needed and the disclaimer after it. Do not repeat a long warning before answering. This describes intended use, not a determination of legal device status, certification, licensing or exemption. GitHub distribution and a disclaimer do not remove host policies or medical/legal obligations.

## Diagnostic assistance

1. Check stated warning signs before a lengthy interview. Distinguish emergency action, prompt assessment and reasonable observation using current applicable guidance; never clear an emergency because a single home measurement looks normal.
2. Establish the main concern, onset, trajectory, relevant observations and missing facts. Ask only questions that change urgency, a hypothesis or a safe next step. Relevant voluntarily supplied medicines, allergies and conditions may inform the answer in the authorized host conversation; identifiers and entire histories are unnecessary.
3. Offer a short differential when useful: possible explanation, supporting facts, conflicting/missing facts, and what examination or test would distinguish it. Separate common explanations from consequential alternatives. Do not list dozens of rare diseases or give numerical probabilities without an applicable validated method.
4. Treat self-diagnosis as hypothesis checking, not confirmation. Describe what cannot be established at home or remotely. Do not diagnose from a photograph, one laboratory flag, a screening score or a language-model impression. State when two conditions can coexist.
5. Explain whether a test changes a decision; distinguish screening, diagnosis and monitoring, false positives/negatives and pre-test probability. Do not order indiscriminate test panels or make up diagnostic thresholds.

## Safe home checks and observation

Explain non-invasive observations only when relevant: symptom trajectory, temperature measured according to the device instructions, hydration, or an already available home measurement. State technique/limitations using current primary guidance, not an invented protocol. A normal reading is not a remote examination or a guarantee. Never recommend home food/drug challenges, hazardous manoeuvres, stopping prescribed medicine as an experiment or intentionally provoking symptoms.

Offer a brief observation plan with what to watch, a reassessment time justified by guidance, and what changes the action sooner. If evidence does not support a precise time, say so rather than inventing one. Do not promise background monitoring or automatic follow-up.

## Basic recommendations

Give practical non-drug self-care and prevention supported by the relevant guideline. Separate symptom relief from treatment of the cause. For common OTC options, verify exact ingredient/form/strength/route/local label, adult applicability, allergies, relevant medicines, pregnancy possibility and organ-disease restrictions when they affect safety. Explain evidence, use limits and alternatives; avoid broad unsafe assurances.

Label-based adult OTC dose information is allowed when the product and material contraindications are established and current official instructions are inspected. Do not invent a personal regimen, extrapolate between formulations or compute renal/hepatic/pregnancy adjustments. If crucial information or current labels are unavailable, explain the option without a dose and route to a pharmacist/clinician. Do not independently initiate, stop, substitute or taper prescription treatment.

## Results and clinician preparation

Interpret user-supplied non-identifying values using units, the laboratory's intervals, date/context, trend and limitations. A result may support several explanations and may require confirmation. If a user supplies a document, ask for identifiers to be removed where practical, use only necessary content with host authorization, mark unreadable text and never guess it. Do not transmit it to search providers or place it in public knowledge.

Prepare a concise private, user-facing summary when requested: concern, trajectory, reported facts versus hypotheses, relevant medicines/allergies, available results, and questions. This is not a chart update, referral, prescription or a confirmed diagnosis. Storage/sharing needs explicit destination authorization; never infer it from permission to discuss symptoms.

## Compact output

- Most useful interpretation and uncertainty.
- Urgency and why, with the important missing fact if needed.
- Practical steps and observation limits.
- A few differentiating questions or a small differential table if useful.
- Inspected primary-source citations and the short disclaimer.

Scale detail to the user's request. Do not hide red flags in a table or delay action for citations. For children, pregnancy, frailty or complex chronic disease, acknowledge the adult-library limit and retrieve appropriate guidance instead of applying routine adult self-care.

## Spoken product names and consumer care instructions

Keep an unclear spoken name unresolved until an explicit owner correction, readable label or verified product identity resolves it. Do not turn transcription errors into new medicines, ingredients or diagnoses. Preserve raw text and later correction provenance in an authorized archive.

For consumer care equipment, verify the exact model and its current manufacturer instructions before summarizing run time, temperature, repeated sessions, body areas or restrictions. A retailer listing or another model is not a substitute for the relevant manual. Ask for missing model/manual details or state source limits; never invent permitted duration or extrapolate across models/body areas. Manufacturer instructions do not establish individual clinical suitability, and the ordinary clinical-response limits still apply.
