# Vaccination schedules and source navigation

General schedule literacy; checked 2026-10-06. Adult care is the package's main scope. Childhood entries below help explain documented vaccination history and national calendars; they do not provide paediatric prescribing or an individual catch-up plan.

## Choose the jurisdiction and version

Use Serbia's Batut 2026 immunization instructions or Russia's national calendar and current amendments/official health-authority guidance for the relevant question. WHO position papers support principles; they are not a replacement for a country's programme. A US or EU schedule must not be silently applied to Serbia/Russia. Calendar eligibility, clinical suitability, funding and local stock are different questions. [S20, S107-S110]

## Catch-up and transfer between countries

Documented valid doses usually remain useful after a delay. WHO's interrupted-schedule table, internally dated December 2025, generally resumes many series without repeating previous doses. This is not an exception-free rule: product, antigen, age, minimum interval, live-vaccine timing and some interrupted travel-vaccine schedules matter. Missing records are unknown, not proof of being unvaccinated or protected. [S109]

For a general comparison, establish country, broad age group, vaccine/antigens, number and timing of documented doses and relevant risk category without collecting identity or a private archive. Different brand names or combination products may contain corresponding antigens; do not infer equivalence without checking. Explain what a vaccination service needs to reconcile rather than calculate a personal catch-up regimen from incomplete data.

## Contraindications and myths

A serious allergic reaction to an earlier dose/component can be a contraindication. Live vaccines require special consideration in pregnancy and immunosuppression. A mild illness, ordinary non-vaccine allergy or antibiotic use is not automatically a contraindication; moderate/severe acute illness can be a precaution. Confirm vaccine-specific details and local assessment. Allergy history should identify the actual reaction/component rather than becoming a blanket ban on all vaccines. [S110]

Do not recommend homeopathic nosodes instead of vaccination. BCG is not universal lifelong tuberculosis protection, and rubella requirements cannot be inferred from a blanket two-dose claim about every adult. [S31-S32, S112]

## Blank documentation and refresh rules

For a separately managed private system, retain vaccine/antigens, product where known, administration date, dose sequence, source and uncertainty; lot/manufacturer and reaction information may be useful when documented. Preserve original records and unknown fields. General templates remain blank in this repository.

Refresh country calendars for each consequential question, including amendments and local outbreak orders. State the document's internal version, source publication/update date, check date and sections read separately. Do not equate a recently retrieved old PDF with a new recommendation. Detailed travel schedules, immunocompromised regimens and product supply/funding remain outside this scoped review.
