## Tasks
Sports psychology covers learning, attention, motivation, confidence, competitive pressure, recovery, coach/team relationships, injury, career retirement and mental health. Outcome and well-being are different outcomes. Training as a mental performance consultant does not in itself qualify as a clinical psychologist. [S94, S97]

## What the Research Supports
The applicability ratings below are editorial, not GRADE. Effect sizes do not translate into seconds per lap or probability of victory.

| Direction | What was found | Limitation and Application |
|---|---|---|
| Comprehensive skills training, mindfulness/acceptance, imagery | Reinebo review: 111 eligible papers, 25 in five quantitative syntheses. Positive ratings became statistically insignificant when non-randomized studies and subjective outcomes were excluded [S80] | Promising tools; sustained improvement in objective outcome cannot be promised |
| Pre-performance routines | Meta-analysis of 112 effect sizes supports benefit of pre-performance routines [S81] | Other sports tasks; not 112 independent studies or testing of our racing routine |
| Process goals | A review of 27 studies found a stronger effect of process goals than place/winning goals [S82] | Choose a controlled action; wanting to win is allowed |
| Self-talk, short tips | Meta-analysis of 32 studies supports targeted prompts based on task and learning [S83] | Not confidence spells and not an obligation to remove negative thoughts. |
| Imagery, mental rehearsal | 2025 review: 86 studies, 3,593 athletes; average benefits with task differences [S84] | Not a substitute for practice. Frequency/duration moderators are not a set dose for the rider |
| Working under pressure, choking | Review 2019 - 47 studies; meta-analysis 2026 - 21. Routines, attention, self-talk and other methods considered [S88–S89] | For open problems, estimates are less robust after adjusting for publication bias; the average result does not prove the benefit of the technique on the track |
| Competitive Anxiety | Ong/Chua meta-analysis examines anxiety reduction following psychological interventions [S90] | Subjective anxiety and performance quality are different outcomes; complete peace of mind is not a necessary goal |
| Sleep | Consensus takes into account travel, early starts and individual recovery needs [S91] | Do not prescribe a universal amount of sleep, drugs or sleep restriction before a race. |
| Mental health | IOC 2026 update considers environment and specialist assistance [S92] | Clinical difficulties are not cured by requiring persistence; AI effectiveness is not established by this document |

The reviews share some of the same research. Do not add up their samples as independent confirmations. Improvements in well-being, performance, safety, and time have varied outcomes.

## Working concepts
- **Confidence:** specific assessment of the mastered action and conditions; test for repeatability and feedback rather than require faith.
- **Arousal and anxiety:** finding out what changes in actions. There is no universal correct heart rate and no “be completely calm first” rule.
- **Choking:** noticeable deterioration of mastered action under pressure; normal error, lack of skill or malfunction does not establish choking.
- **Attention:** selection of landmarks, scanning, automation. External focus does not mean looking fixedly at one point.
- **ACT/MAC:** act when agitated; accepting the feeling does not allow you to ignore the danger.
- **CBT/REBT:** check predictions and excessive demands while stationary; Don’t argue with every thought during the race.
- **Deliberate practice:** small task, appropriate difficulty, feedback and repetition. “Try harder” does not describe the skill.
- **Flow:** a possible experience, not a mandatory mode and not an clearance criterion; one cannot promise to summon it by ritual.
- **Self-criticism:** maintaining responsibility for a mistake, removing the judgment of the entire person.
- **Motivation:** personal goals, pleasure, cost of participation and external pressure; rest and abandonment can be meaningful.

These are operational design differences, not a diagnostic system. The novelty of the method and popularity with the champion do not prove superiority.

## What exactly was researched in motorsport?
1. **Mullen et al., 2012:** 16 male students in a simulation. External focus turned out to be more useful than internal focus; with the pressure created, both groups improved their results. This is not a study of professional racers, nor is it a "anxiety always worsens driving" rule. [S85]
2. **Lappi, 2018:** analysis of motorsports educational literature describes cognitive skill, training and feedback. A review of practices is not a randomized test of their effectiveness. [S86]
3. **McKnight et al., 2026:** Self-reports from five F1 and five F2 drivers; data from the 2019 season. There are individual differences in attention/confidence, the overall indicator did not differ significantly. The small sample, multiple comparisons, and lack of intervention limit conclusions about causality and training. [S87]

It has not been established that the cards below speed up auto slalom, drift or amateur circuit racing. Separately indicate the transfer between disciplines, from the simulator to the track and from a human specialist to AI.

## Choosing the first step
| Observed Difficulty | Candidate | What to check first |
|---|---|---|
| Thoughts fly away before the start | Familiar short routine and one task | Sleep, illness, track, car readiness |
| It turns out in training, in the competition the action changes | Compare conditions; then pressure rehearsal | Surface, tires, pace, skills and number of attempts |
| Controls every body movement | Familiar action result prompt | The skill may not yet be mastered |
| After a mistake, he hurries to win back | Pre-agreed return to next task | Control, damage, need to stop |
| Gets stuck comparing with an opponent | A process goal | Actual gap in skill or car performance |
| Exhaustion, pleasure gone | Load, recovery, meaning of participation | Persistent conditions and medical causes |
| Fear after the incident | Safety check, return plan | Injuries, concussions, traumatic stress symptoms |

Use one line per episode, not the entire questionnaire. The psychological version can coexist with a technical or physical cause.

## Benefit check
Select one behavioral and one subjective indicator: whether the task was completed; penalties/disorientation; spread of comparable attempts; ready or stuck after an error. Marks 0–10 are personal observations, not tests.

Consider training, car, tires, weather, traffic and fatigue. Several successful attempts do not establish causation. If the practice adds stress, worsens attention, or becomes a mandatory ritual, simplify or stop. The pleasantness of a conversation with an AI is not the same as a sport performance outcome.
