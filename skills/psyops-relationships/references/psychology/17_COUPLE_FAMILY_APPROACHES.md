# Couple and family work: approaches and boundaries
Version 2.0 · 2026-09-28

Family psychotherapy is broader than working with a couple: it includes other family relationships, roles and generations. This module focuses in detail on voluntary conversation between adult partners. Working with children, guardianship, forensics and severe family violence requires separate professional training; this base does not replace it.

## What has been learned
| Approach/format | Focus | What can be concluded |
|---|---|---|
| Behavioral Couples Therapy/BCT | Concrete behavior, exchange of support, conversation and problem solving | Included in Interventions Researched for Relationship Distress [S24–S26] |
| Integrative Behavioral Couples Therapy/IBCT | Changing behavior while understanding and accepting differences | There are comparative and long-term observations; acceptance does not mean tolerating coercion [S63] |
| Emotionally Focused Couples Therapy / EFCT | Repeating cycle of interaction, emotions and needs for contact | There is RCT support; reviewed meta-analysis does not establish superiority over BCT [S24] |
| System perspective | Mutual influence, context, rules and roles | A useful way to ask questions; no reason to distribute blame equally |
| Discernment counseling | Different intentions of partners regarding the continuation of the relationship | A protocol and case series are described that are not RCT evidence of universal effectiveness [S65] |
| Marriage Checkup and preventive formats | Early detection of tension and regular feedback | There are improvements to particular outcomes in RCT; home talk and AI format are not tested separately [S64] |

Structural, intergenerational, narrative, solution-oriented schools have not received a separate comparative review here. The name “family” does not establish the same evidence of all methods. Don't promote a popular proprietary system as the best standard without specific research.

## What can you use in conversation?
- Make a map: situation → interpretation of each → action of each → response of the other → consequences.
- Highlight external circumstances: workload, health, money, everyday life, lack of sleep, caring for others.
- Separate the intention, the effect of the action and the responsibility for it.
- Find a specific request, an acceptable refusal, and an alternative.
- Explore sustainable differences and compatibility; Don’t reduce everything to poor communication.
- Prepare a question or short description of the episode for a specialist.

These are the author's ways of organizing the conversation and are not reproductions of complete licensed manuals. Do not conduct intense trauma-revelation techniques, provocative confrontations, or forced emotional intimacy through chat.

## Usefulness criteria
Both people can more accurately describe the other's position; notice specific changes in behavior; see opportunities and limitations more clearly; may refuse the offer without penalty. The number of marriages saved is not the only criterion for success. Ending a relationship can be an individual decision rather than a “therapy failure.”

Impartiality means vetting stories equally and respecting everyone's autonomy. It does not require neutrality towards threats, coercion, humiliation or violence. Do not consider the more eloquent participant more credible.
