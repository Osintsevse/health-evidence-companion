## PATH-F1 — Adaptation, injury and loss of viability are different questions

A cellular response can change size, number or phenotype while preserving viability. Hypertrophy concerns cell size; hyperplasia concerns cell number; metaplasia concerns replacement of a differentiated cell type. An enlarged tissue alone does not identify which process occurred. Adaptation can be useful or harmful in context. Injury can remain reversible or exceed recovery capacity; response depends on cell/tissue context and exposure extent or duration. [S713]

This is a mechanistic framework, not a clock predicting reversibility in a person. Do not infer a toxic cause, cellular mechanism or recovery prognosis from one symptom, laboratory marker or image. Selected toxicologic examples are not validated human diagnostic criteria.

## PATH-F2 — Morphology and molecular mechanism are separate evidence

A morphological pattern does not uniquely establish the molecular path producing it. The NCCD 2018 committee recommendations distinguish accidental from regulated cell death and describe regulated necrotic processes, including necroptosis. Thus the training slide's simple pairing of apoptosis with regulation and necrosis with accident is not an exhaustive mechanistic classification. Do not repeat its universal unregulated-necrosis statement. [S714; S713]

The committee's nomenclature is a dated conceptual source, not patient-outcome evidence. ## ONC-F1 — More cells, abnormal cells and invasive cancer are not interchangeable labels

NCI distinguishes increased cell numbers with preserved appearance/organization from dysplastic abnormality; neither term alone means invasive cancer or inevitable progression. Benign growth also need not be harmless: its location or size can matter. [S715]

Use the actual organ, specimen and report terminology rather than a universal progression ladder. These selected educational sections supply no individual probability, prognosis, screening interval or management plan. They do not settle every organ-specific meaning of carcinoma in situ or staging terminology. An illustrative sequence must not become a prediction about a person.

## REN-F1 — Filtration, tubular handling and regulation do different work

The glomerulus transfers water and smaller dissolved substances from blood into nephron fluid. Tubular reabsorption returns needed material to blood; removal of additional wastes or acid into forming urine acts in the other direction. Filtered fluid therefore differs from final urine. Renal roles also include water/mineral/acid balance and hormonal functions; a filtration rate does not directly measure each role. [S716]

Creatinine is a measured marker concentration; eGFR estimates filtration using markers and other variables. Muscle mass, diet, unstable renal function and assay effects can matter. Urine albumin contributes another kind of information. An isolated creatinine or eGFR is not a complete verdict on renal health or present fluid status. [S717; S437]

## FLUID-M1 — A concentration is not a body-fluid inventory

The inspected NLM page explains osmolality as a concentration of dissolved particles: changes in water or dissolved material can alter it. A concentration alone does not directly give total fluid volume. A low blood-sodium result can occur with sodium loss or dilution by retained fluid; it does not by itself establish a single cause or an instruction to add salt or change drinking. Interpretation uses clinical context and other appropriate measurements. [S718; S719]

This is plain-language measurement literacy.
