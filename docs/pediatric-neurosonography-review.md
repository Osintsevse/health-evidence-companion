## Evidence review

- Use AWMF S2k 006-102 v2.0 for age-dependent presentation, the purpose and limits of imaging, the distinction between anatomy and pressure, and clinical follow-up. Canadian neurosurgical recommendations distinguish initial assessment from imaging in established hydrocephalus. They describe their own clinical setting.
- Fandak et al. studied reproducible reading of particular acquired views in a predominantly young term-born sample. The study did not establish repeat-acquisition reliability or a minimum meaningful serial change. Beijst et al. and Valverde et al. identify separate measurement and protocol contexts for preterm infants with hemorrhage.
- Krahulik et al. support the limit of negative fundus findings in a selected neurosurgical population. Cour-Andlauer et al. address an indirect ultrasound proxy in pediatric intensive care. Rohde et al. report associations in children already selected for imaging. These populations and outcomes delimit the inference.
- The guideline and recommendations are cited with original factual synthesis. The primary articles retain their article-specific CC BY 4.0 attribution. Figures, datasets, tables and proprietary algorithms were excluded.

| Check | Observed response |

| Newly accelerated head growth, an older extra-axial finding and large head size in the family | Treated family history as context, advised contacting the clinic about earlier review, and gave a practical list of growth and imaging records for that visit. |
| Repeated vomiting and unusual sleepiness in a child with a shunt and a prior stable report | Advised emergency assessment immediately and calling emergency services for difficulty waking, abnormal breathing or rapid deterioration. The prior stable scan did not delay assessment. Review prompted more explicit shunt terminology and escalation in the module and routing metadata. |
