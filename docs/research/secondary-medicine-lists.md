# Using secondary medicine lists

Selected sections of the three supplied pages were inspected on 2026-10-06 UTC: Encyclopedia Pathologica, NCHPL and Russian Wikipedia (S122-S124). They provide names and references for discovery, not an authoritative efficacy classification. No article corpus or verbatim catalogue is redistributed.

Use the original indication-specific workflow in [module 18](../../knowledge/18_TREATMENT_EVIDENCE_AND_HOMEOPATHY.md): verify identity and claim, locate the current guideline/review, inspect meaningful outcomes, harms, certainty and applicability, then record a justified disposition. Keep a lead unassessed until this is done.

Corrections matter. A blanket harmlessness claim is unsafe; weakness of benefit evidence does not remove pharmacological/product risks. An old rimantadine flu example conflicts with CDC's inspected 2026 resistance guidance. EMA's restricted trimetazidine indication and the UK Nootropil cortical-myoclonus label show why a class/brand-wide ineffective verdict can lose meaningful distinctions. These checks do not establish every other use as effective. [S112, S125-S128]

Do not use publication counts, PubMed indexing, foreign registration or manufacturer funding as a substitute for appraisal. Preserve positive and negative findings, pending questions and source disagreements. Never recommend automatic withdrawal of a prescribed medicine merely because a popular list names it.
