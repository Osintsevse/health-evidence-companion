# Architecture packet review

## Scope and recommendation

This packet proposes a source/provenance and reference-discovery architecture for public medical knowledge. It intentionally does not add or recommend a clinical engine, executable score, treatment algorithm, or new route graph. The model is a thin overlay compatible with the existing navigation graph: topic destinations can point to typed claims, dated sources, populations, jurisdictions, review status, and refresh triggers while preserving established route identifiers. Clinical reasoning and interoperability standards are treated as implementation references, not as proof of clinical safety or a mandate to encode rules. S526 S528 S529

The packet proposes the following decisions for an architecture review:

- Preserve separate records for curriculum scope, foundation concepts, empirical evidence, guideline recommendations, regulator/product status, local availability, and patient-specific clinical care.
- Make each consequential claim traceable to an exact source version and locator, with population, setting, jurisdiction, reading depth, date, limitations, and rights status.
- Record evidence status as temporal states rather than a single rank: current guideline-supported practice, context-dependent option, new recommendation, authorized product, investigational intervention, negative/safety evidence, withdrawn/discontinued status, or unknown.
- Use curriculum and open-textbook discovery to identify prerequisites and learning gaps; do not treat curriculum coverage as treatment evidence or professional competence.
- Use source-change events and scheduled queues to trigger review; do not treat a successful fetch, new web timestamp, or due date as evidence that a recommendation was checked or changed.
- Keep urgent response and existing prescribed rescue plans separate from general retrieval. Do not allow a photo, model output, score, or isolated number to delay appropriate emergency care or drive treatment changes.
- Evaluate citation support, source applicability, rights, uncertainty, multi-turn changes, abstention, and escalation using wholly synthetic fixtures. These checks are not clinical product validation.

The route objects are reference-discovery behaviors and give maintainer-facing boundaries. They should be adapted to the repository's established route conventions during integration, without expanding the public user experience into an automated diagnostic or prescribing workflow. The module is 2,764 whitespace-delimited words, meeting the 2,500–4,000-word requirement. The register contains 22 unique source keys, 8 routes, and 10 visibly synthetic evaluations.
