# Laboratory tests, prevention and metabolism

Sources: S20, S28-S32. Principles and source routes; no individualized screening schedule is established here.

## Laboratory results

Record-design rules preserve analyte, value, unit, laboratory-specific reference range, timing, conditions and original source. Reference ranges depend on method. An out-of-range result does not always mean disease; an in-range result does not always exclude it. Symptoms and trends matter more than a red highlight alone. False positives and false negatives occur. [S28]

Do not propose broad panels "for everything" without a question. Explain what decision each test would change. Public examples must use wholly synthetic values, not real reports.

## Blood pressure

Hypertension is usually confirmed through repeated readings, home monitoring or ambulatory monitoring under the applicable guideline, rather than one post-exertion value. Technique, cuff and conditions matter. Recheck diagnostic thresholds and targets against the relevant current guideline. [S29]

## Metabolism

This area includes glucose, lipids, body mass, thyroid, kidneys and liver. First distinguish symptoms, an established condition and preventive risk assessment. Diabetes screening depends on age and risk factors; normal weight or one previous measurement cannot substitute for current assessment. [S30]

These are development areas, not a claim that all endocrine guidelines have been studied.

## Vaccination

For Serbia, verify current Batut instructions, exact vaccine, age, risk factors and travel route. Separate documented administration from assumptions about immunity. A booster date does not mean immunity disappears that day. Do not treat old notes as a current vaccination schedule. [S20]

BCG administration does not justify promising lifelong tuberculosis protection. Do not claim all adults universally require two rubella-containing doses; immunity criteria and indications vary. Check international MMR rules separately from the local schedule. [S31-S32]
