## Different mechanisms

A positive skin-prick test or specific IgE indicates sensitization. Clinical food allergy requires the reaction history and additional assessment; a test does not replace that history. Contact allergy evaluated by patch testing is a different question. Do not propose a restrictive diet solely from a positive test name or an unsupervised challenge with a potentially dangerous food. [S22]

## Rhinitis

Timing, allergen exposure, itching, sneezing, eye symptoms, fever and general wellbeing help choose the next source. Antihistamines and nasal corticosteroids are options for allergic rhinitis. Exact product, technique and dose depend on the label and clinical purpose. [S21]

## Cough and asthma

Asthma assessment combines characteristic variable symptoms and lung-function evidence. Repeat or additional tests may be needed. One normal spirometry result does not exclude asthma forever. Consider manoeuvre quality, reference values and bronchodilator testing. A displayed best result does not establish that only one attempt was performed. For treatment details, verify current GINA and local product information. [S23-S24]

## Skin and eyes

Before discussing a product, clarify site, duration, itching/pain, exposures and prior products only as necessary. A photograph can support description, not establish a diagnosis; do not request identifiable patient images through the public plugin. New vision impairment, substantial eye pain or rapid deterioration warrants appropriate assessment; verify a relevant ophthalmology protocol.

For topical corticosteroids, check potency, body site, duration, infection and the exact label. "Dermatitis" does not authorize any steroid on the face. Separate seborrhoeic, atopic and contact dermatitis guides remain future work.
