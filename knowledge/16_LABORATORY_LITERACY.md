# Understanding laboratory information

General adult education; reviewed 2026-10-06. Use public/synthetic examples or relevant voluntarily supplied non-identifying values within an authorized conversation. Do not solicit identifiers or publish actual results. A panel alone cannot establish a diagnosis; apply module 19's response and privacy limits.

## Read the result before interpreting the flag

Retain the analyte, value, unit, laboratory reference interval, specimen/method where relevant and sampling conditions. A reference interval describes a reference population; a diagnostic decision threshold and a treatment target answer different questions. An out-of-range result can occur in health, while an in-range result can occur with disease. Do not replace the report's interval with a range found on the internet. Comparisons across laboratories require method and unit checks. [S28]

Consider why the test was ordered, relevant symptoms, trend, timing, fasting requirements, recent illness, exercise, hydration, medicines and supplements. Request only context needed for the general explanation, without identifiers or an archive. Missing units, ranges or timing stay unknown. Do not guess a digit or unit from an unreadable image. [S28, S99, S101-S105]

## Common tests: what they can and cannot answer

| Test/group | Main information | Frequent interpretation error | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| CBC: haemoglobin, haematocrit, red cells, MCV | Blood-cell quantities and red-cell size; part of anaemia assessment | Treating one low haemoglobin or MCV as proof of its cause | S102 |
| White-cell differential | Types of white cells; counts must be considered together | Calling every percentage shift bacterial infection or allergy | S103 |
| Ferritin | Iron stores with inflammatory-context limitations | Assuming normal/high ferritin excludes deficiency, or high ferritin alone proves iron overload | S100 |
| CRP | A marker of inflammation, including infectious and non-infectious causes | Diagnosing the site/cause or choosing antibiotics from CRP alone | S104 |
| Creatinine/eGFR and urine albumin | Complementary kidney-function and damage information | Diagnosing chronic kidney disease from one result | S97-S98 |
| HbA1c/glucose | Different views of glycaemia over time or at sampling | Ignoring confirmation requirements or red-cell/assay interference | S99 |
| TSH with FT4/FT3 when indicated | Thyroid assessment depends on the pattern and clinical context | Applying TSH-alone logic to suspected pituitary disease, or ignoring biotin | S101 |
| ALT/AST, ALP/GGT, bilirubin, albumin and clotting tests | Different components of injury, cholestasis and liver function | Treating all enzymes as interchangeable or one elevation as a named disease | S105 |
| D-dimer | A component of a validated suspected-VTE diagnostic pathway | Population screening, or treating a positive result as proof of a clot | S106 |

These are interpretation prompts, not a recommended universal test panel. A finding needs an explanation that matches the question and follow-up that could change a decision.

## Four distinctions to preserve

**Iron and inflammation.** Ferritin can rise with inflammation and other conditions. WHO guidance uses inflammatory context in assessing deficiency and says ferritin alone should not identify iron overload. Explain accompanying evidence and causes; do not automatically prescribe iron or a chelation treatment. Do not import a population-survey cutoff as an individualized treatment threshold. [S100]

**Acute versus chronic kidney change.** KDIGO 2024 remains the current guideline foundation, with a focused treatment update in development at the reviewed portal. Establish chronicity rather than labeling a single low eGFR as chronic disease. Kidney abnormalities persisting for more than three months are part of the CKD definition; urine albumin and causes matter too. This does not mean waiting three months to assess a potentially acute deterioration. eGFR is an estimate, and the method and context affect its interpretation. The complete guideline was not studied here. [S97-S98]

**HbA1c versus glucose.** HbA1c reflects average glycaemia over roughly three months. In someone without clear symptoms, a diagnostic abnormality generally needs confirmation using the applicable criteria. Blood loss, transfusion, iron deficiency, altered red-cell survival or haemoglobin variants can distort results; discordance with glucose deserves explanation rather than choosing the convenient number. Do not invent a universal prediabetes range across all guidelines. [S99]

**Thyroid tests and assay interference.** NICE distinguishes primary from suspected secondary thyroid disease when selecting TSH and FT4/FT3. Biotin supplements can produce falsely high or low results. Explain that the laboratory/clinician needs to know about relevant products; do not invent one washout period for every assay or instruct automatic withdrawal of prescribed treatment. Acute illness and the reason for testing also matter. [S101]

## Probability, urgency and useful follow-up

A positive screening test is not necessarily a diagnosis. Predictive value depends on the starting likelihood as well as test performance; selective testing and confirmatory pathways matter. Broad panels can produce incidental findings and unnecessary follow-up. Do not fabricate diagnostic probabilities or apply a clinical score without its required assessment. NICE NG158 uses clinical probability and imaging alongside D-dimer in suspected VTE, rather than a stand-alone verdict. [S28, S106]

Distinguish an ordinary reference flag from a critical laboratory alert. Follow an actual laboratory's urgent contact instruction; severe symptoms or rapid deterioration warrant urgent care independently of a panel. Do not publish guessed universal emergency thresholds. A safe explanation states what is measured, what the pattern might suggest, what remains uncertain and which clinician/laboratory question resolves it. No reassurance based only on a normal result.
