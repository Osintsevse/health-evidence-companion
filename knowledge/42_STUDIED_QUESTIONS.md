## F1 — What does a confidence interval describe?

**Scope:** introductory frequentist inference, not an individual's probability of disease.

A point estimate summarizes the parameter estimated from a sample; an interval also represents sampling uncertainty under a stated method and assumptions. A 95% confidence procedure is designed so that, over repeated suitable samples, approximately 95% of its intervals include the fixed target parameter. This is not a statement that 95% of individual observations lie within one interval, nor that a future sample estimate must fall inside it. It is not a Bayesian posterior probability for the parameter. [psychology:S104]

An apparently narrow interval does not repair biased recruitment, systematic measurement error or a mismatch between the study population and the intended application. Identify what was estimated, units, comparator, outcome timing, interval method and relevant design before translating it into ordinary language. Do not use the simple normal approximation indiscriminately for small or extreme proportions, repeated observations or complex sampling. The selected reading introduces an elementary method; other methods require their own assumptions. [psychology:S104]

**Original synthetic illustration:** an invented between-group difference is +1 scale unit, with a 95% interval from -2 to +4. If higher scores are better, this interval is compatible with worse, unchanged and better group-average outcomes under that analysis. It does not predict an individual person's response or establish treatment equivalence. Whether a benefit matters depends on the outcome and a justified meaningful-effect threshold; it cannot be read from the confidence level.

**Reading:** OpenIntro Statistics 4e, screen-reader copy updated 2022-10-21: 5.2.1 and opening 5.2.2, p. 202; 5.2.5, pp. 207–208. Text read; PDF pp. 202 and 207 visually checked. ## F2 — Does a small p-value prove an important effect?

**Scope:** statistical interpretation; no clinical treatment verdict follows from this card alone.

A p-value is computed under a specified null model. It describes how unusual the selected statistic, or results at least as extreme in the specified test direction, would be if that model applied. It is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true, that an intervention works, or that a reported effect occurred 'by chance'. State the hypothesis and assumptions rather than converting a p-value into a personal probability. [psychology:S104]

Failing to reject a null hypothesis does not establish that it is true. A study may leave important effects unresolved. Conversely, a very small difference can become statistically detectable in a large sample without becoming practically important. Examine the effect estimate, uncertainty, comparator, outcome scale and time horizon before discussing benefit or harm. Recommendation strength is another judgment and is not a synonym for statistical significance. [psychology:S104]

A Type I error rejects a true null hypothesis; a Type II error fails to reject a false one. A chosen significance threshold controls one error under assumptions; it does not remove every risk of a wrong conclusion. The consequences of mistakes and study design matter. Do not change thresholds after seeing a result to manufacture a favorable conclusion. That last instruction is this project's appraisal safeguard, not a reproduced textbook procedure.

**Original synthetic illustration:** two invented reports have the same small p-value but use different outcomes and report very different effect magnitudes. Their practical value need not be the same. A large p-value with a wide interval also cannot establish 'no meaningful difference'; this packet did not study equivalence/noninferiority methods. Do not claim that another test proves an exactly zero effect: meaningful bounds and exact equality are different claims.

**Reading:** OpenIntro 4e screen-reader selected pp. 211–213, 216–218 and 223–224: hypothesis framework, decision-error definitions, opening p-value definition and statistical versus practical significance. PDF pp. 218 and 224 visually checked. Full calculation exercises, multiplicity, equivalence and complete test-method appraisal were not completed. [psychology:S104]

## G1 — How should recurrent heartburn be framed?

**Scope:** adult symptom education. An acute chest or bleeding emergency takes priority.

Retrosternal burning or regurgitation can suggest reflux, but describing a symptom is not the same as establishing its cause. Review onset, trajectory, timing around meals/lying down, swallowing, vomiting, weight change, bleeding, relevant medicines and effects on sleep/function. NIDDK's adult reflux page describes medicine and pregnancy context; do not stop a prescribed medicine from a possible association alone. [medical:S645]

Upper-abdominal discomfort or early fullness may prompt a dyspepsia assessment. NIDDK describes reviewing symptoms, medicines, prior H. pylori infection and family history, then selecting examinations or tests for plausible causes. Functional dyspepsia is a clinical assessment when another explanation is not found; it must not become a default chat label for unexplained symptoms or a conclusion from anxiety alone. [medical:S660]

Difficulty/pain swallowing, persistent vomiting, appetite loss or unexplained weight loss need medical review. Blood or coffee-ground vomit and black tarry stool require prompt medical attention; fainting, marked dizziness, confusion or other shock features warrant emergency help. A normal home measurement does not clear bleeding. [medical:S645; medical:S661]

New concerning pressure-like chest discomfort, spreading pain, sweating or breathlessness follows the existing heart-attack/chest-emergency pathway. Indigestion-like symptoms do not safely distinguish cardiac from reflux causes; do not delay help for an antacid experiment. [medical:S276]

**Depth/limits:** this card extends NIDDK's July 2020 reflux education with March 2025 dyspepsia diagnosis and July 2024 GI-bleeding sections. It is not a complete current GERD guideline or a global age threshold for endoscopy. Pediatric, pregnancy and local treatment pathways require their own sources. Modern guideline appraisal and outcome-specific treatment comparisons remain pending.

**Next useful question:** what diagnostic decision would change after a particular result, and what alternative remains if it is normal? Avoid a universal test panel.

## G2 — What can reflux investigations establish?

**Scope:** purpose and limitations of selected tests, without ordering a test sequence.

NIDDK describes initial clinical assessment from symptoms/history and considering tests for suspected complications, another plausible cause or persistent symptoms despite treatment. This explains why every person with heartburn does not need the same investigation, while persistent or changing symptoms deserve reassessment. [medical:S659]

Upper-GI endoscopy inspects the lining of the esophagus, stomach and duodenum and can include biopsy. It can investigate visible abnormalities and tissue-level questions. Reflux monitoring measures exposure over time; the NIDDK overview distinguishes catheter assessment of acid/nonacid reflux from capsule measurement of acid exposure, and describes matching the recording to symptoms, diet and sleep. These methods answer different questions. A lining examination is not a recording of every reflux event, and symptom timing is not proof that every symptom has that cause. [medical:S659]

The dyspepsia source describes endoscopy/biopsy for selected causes and separate stool or urea-breath tests for H. pylori. An infection test and a reflux measurement are not interchangeable explanations of upper-GI symptoms. The indication, current preparation requirements, existing medicines and intended follow-up must be checked in applicable guidance; do not invent a medication washout or interpret a result without its method. [medical:S660]

Antacids, H2 blockers and PPIs have different roles in symptom relief/acid suppression, while procedures require separate selection and risk discussion. The stored NIDDK treatment overview is dated 2020. It does not select a product, duration or procedural candidate. Symptom improvement alone does not supply independent proof of causation; persistent symptoms require reconsidering the clinical question. [medical:S646; medical:S659]

## S1 — Why is chronic insomnia more than poor sleep habits?

**Scope:** adult education, with VA/DoD clinical guidance interpreted within its setting.

Adequate opportunity and a reasonably regular schedule support sleep; they do not establish that a disorder is absent. The VA/DoD 2025 guidance distinguishes persistent insomnia with daytime consequences despite suitable sleep opportunity from insufficient opportunity and other sleep disorders. Clinical history matters; a questionnaire does not independently diagnose insomnia. Consider relevant sleep, medical, mental-health and medicine context. Snoring/witnessed breathing interruptions or gasping can change the question toward sleep-disordered breathing rather than more hygiene tips. [medical:S657; medical:S648]

The guideline recommends multicomponent CBT-I for chronic insomnia. Its behavioral and cognitive elements address factors maintaining sleep difficulty; hygiene education can contribute but is not the whole treatment. Recommendation 5 is strong for CBT-I, with moderate confidence in critical sleep outcomes in the accompanying discussion; its update category is 'Not reviewed, Amended'. Recommendation 7 is weak against hygiene education alone, with low confidence and older underlying studies. Current publication date, recommendation strength, evidence confidence and new systematic appraisal are different attributes. [medical:S657]

Treatment can require adaptation. Restricting time in bed may temporarily increase sleepiness; excessive daytime sleepiness, bipolar illness, uncontrolled seizures, instability or acute mental-health concerns can affect suitability and timing. This card does not reproduce the guideline's treatment algorithm, questionnaire or restriction schedule. It cannot authorize self-directed restriction, sedatives or medicine withdrawal. Trained care and a suitable delivery format matter. [medical:S657]

**Reading:** VA/DoD version 3.0, January 2025, evidence through 31 March 2024; selected printed/physical pp. 7–8, 30–31, 34 and 44–50. Drug, full OSA-treatment and instrument appendices were not appraised. NHLBI habits provide dated 2022 background. No universal local protocol or patient clearance is inferred.

**Next:** review applicable local services, coexisting apnea/circadian problems and patient-relevant outcomes; assess exact delivery formats separately rather than calling any app CBT-I.

## C1 — Does better sleep establish general cognitive enhancement?

**Scope:** sleep deficiency, everyday function and outcome interpretation.

NHLBI describes sleep deficiency affecting learning, focus, memory, decisions, reaction time and errors. Unintended dozing and impaired alertness matter during work, driving and other hazardous tasks. Subjective confidence or a consumer score is not proof of safety or a substitute for assessment. This educational account concerns sleep/function; it is not a trial of a cognitive-enhancement product. [medical:S658]

The selected VA/DoD CBT-I discussion primarily reports insomnia severity, sleep efficiency, sleep-onset latency, wakefulness after sleep onset and sleep quality, with some other outcomes in particular studies. Improvement in one of these does not establish increased intelligence, durable everyday memory benefit, dementia prevention or an effect in already adequately rested adults. This is an outcome-applicability inference, not a guideline recommendation against every possible cognitive benefit. [medical:S657]

Separate three questions: correcting inadequate sleep opportunity, treating a diagnosed sleep disorder, and improving performance beyond a person's baseline needs. Each needs an appropriate population, comparator, outcome and time horizon. A change in a wearable-derived sleep stage or trained cognitive task is not automatically meaningful improvement at home, work or school. Use the measurement and evidence-method references before promising transfer.

Persistent impairment merits assessment of the cause, especially with sleepiness or possible breathing interruptions. Chat cannot determine fitness to drive or provide a performance-enhancement regimen. Do not simply add more sleep time or a supplement and infer that the cause has been treated.

**Reading/uncertainty:** NHLBI daytime-thinking/safety sections, updated June 2022, plus the selected VA/DoD insomnia pages above. The original cognitive trials and every sleep intervention were not independently reviewed. A comparative cognitive-outcomes review and meaningful transfer measures remain a study task; missing support here is not proof that an intervention never helps.

## C2 — Learning a task is different from improving cognition generally

**Scope:** basic learning concepts from selected textbook sections; no cognitive-treatment or dementia-prevention estimate.

Retrieval practice means trying to recall learned material, rather than only reading it again. Goldstein's account describes a vocabulary experiment in which continued retrieval after an initial correct response helped delayed recall; initial success did not mean learning was finished. This is a textbook account of an older experiment, not this project's independent appraisal of the original trial. [psychology:S103]

Familiarity and easy rereading can feel like knowledge without establishing later recall. The selected sections also explain that performance depends on the relation between learning and test demands: a task emphasizing sound can favour sound-based learning over meaning-based learning. There is no universal rule that a subjectively deeper technique wins every test. [psychology:S103]

**Original application:** a learner could read a short explanation, close it, explain the idea in their own words, check mistakes, and later answer a different example. Checking the original matters: confidently repeating an error is not evidence of accuracy. Keep separate records of recognition, delayed recall, explanation and application. These are different outcomes; do not combine them into an unsupported score for “brain improvement.”

Practice on a particular task does not itself demonstrate transfer to everyday independence, general intelligence, disease prevention or a different untrained skill. Those claims need directly relevant comparison groups and outcomes. These selected pages do not establish the best schedule, individual response, long-term clinical benefit or an optimal note-taking medium. Do not turn the textbook's historical account of handwriting research into a universal preference for paper.

**Reading:** Goldstein 5e, Chapter 7, retrieval-practice and selected effective-study discussion, printed pp. 197–201 (PDF 231–235); transfer-appropriate processing, printed pp. 206–207 (PDF 240–241). Text read; PDF 232,234,241 visually checked. Printed p.197 opens in an earlier example; only the named later subsection supports this card. Source-study date 2026-10-17.

**Next:** original retrieval studies, contemporary replications and transfer outcomes; the full chapter and clinical enhancement literature remain unreviewed.


## L1 — Identify the laboratory result before interpreting it

**Scope:** preserving meaning and preparation context; no universal screening panel or numerical interpretation.

Record the exact test label, value and unit, specimen, collection date/time, laboratory, report status and the reference interval printed beside it. Preserve available assay/method details and corrections. These identity fields prevent an apparently similar label from silently becoming a different test. Missing units, specimen or decimal punctuation are unresolved data, not invitations to guess. This follows the project's laboratory-identity workflow; it does not establish assay equivalence. [S28]

For interpretation, use the report's own reference interval and relevant population. Laboratories can use different methods and intervals. An out-of-range value may occur without disease; an in-range value does not rule disease out. A flag alone is not a diagnosis. History, symptoms, examination and the purpose of testing affect what the result means. Do not automatically pool results from different methods or laboratories in one trend. [S28]

Preparation is test-specific. Food or drink, some medicines or supplements, strenuous exercise and missed preparation instructions can affect certain results. Follow the instructions for the particular test, report departures, and tell the clinician about medicines and supplements. Do not independently stop prescribed medicine or fast for every blood test. [S28, S662]

**Original fictional example:** a report says “potassium 5.3” without a visible unit or specimen. Ask for the complete report and relevant clinical context before comparing it with another clinic's result. If a laboratory or clinician reports a critical result, or the person has concerning current symptoms, follow urgent clinical advice rather than waiting for an archival comparison.

**Reading:** MedlinePlus Medical Tests, result/context, reference-range, units, false-result and preparation sections; preparation page's instructions and medicine/fasting discussion, checked 2026-10-17. Federal educational text; no linked third-party encyclopedia content was used.

**Next:** L2 requires a separate evidence review of reference intervals, clinical decision limits, treatment targets and diagnostic probability. The inspected CLSI public scope and a terminology review do not amount to reading the full standard; their distinctions are not adopted as settled evidence here.


## B1 — Blood pressure measurement has a setting and a method

**Scope:** general adult home-measurement literacy. Office, home and ambulatory records remain distinguishable; diagnostic categories and targets require applicable guidance.

Choose a validated upper-arm monitor and the correct cuff size; validation should cover the relevant population. Bring the device to a clinician to check fit and technique. A single value is a snapshot rather than confirmation of a persistent pattern. [S640]

For home measurement, the inspected AHA page advises avoiding smoking, caffeine and exercise for 30 minutes beforehand, emptying the bladder and resting quietly for at least five minutes. Measure over bare skin with the supported arm and cuff at heart level, without talking. Take two readings one minute apart and record both, with date and time. Agree the measurement frequency and monitoring period with the clinician; this card does not supply a universal averaging protocol. [S640]

Record context such as home versus clinic, device/cuff and unusual circumstances. Home monitoring can help a professional confirm a pattern outside the clinic, but it does not replace evaluation or justify changing medication independently. The existing measurement module distinguishes office, home and ambulatory monitoring; thresholds, sampling schedules and interpretation cannot be transferred casually between them. [S640; see 38_BLOOD_PRESSURE_AND_MEASUREMENT.md]

**Original fictional example:** several higher home readings and lower clinic readings should not be resolved by choosing the preferred number or mixing them into one average. Check technique and device, retain both records, and discuss appropriate confirmation. The example does not diagnose masked hypertension.

Current chest pain, breathlessness, sudden neurological or visual symptoms need the emergency pathway; a reassuring repeat reading must not postpone help. Very high current values need prompt clinical action using the applicable guidance. This measurement card does not replace that pathway or claim every high recorded historical value is a current emergency.

**Reading:** AHA Home Blood Pressure Monitoring, device, technique, repeated readings and urgent-action sections; reviewed 2025-08-14, checked 2026-10-17. Original factual paraphrase, no chart, tracker or algorithm copied. AHA's ordinary copyright terms are not a separately verified blanket AI licence or an automatic AI ban.

**Next:** B2 retains current jurisdiction-specific diagnostic confirmation, risk assessment and investigations; US guidance is not a universal Serbian or Russian threshold.


## M1 — Neck and shoulder pain: context changes the next step

**Scope:** adult symptom navigation, without a diagnosis, self-test or exercise prescription.

Ask about onset, trend, injury, location/radiation, sleep and ordinary function, fever/illness, sensation, strength and walking or hand coordination. Neck pain can spread toward an arm; a shoulder problem can limit arm movement. A symptom label such as “tight muscles” does not establish the cause. Mild improving discomfort can often be discussed alongside tolerable ordinary activity; persistent or worsening limitation needs assessment. NHS shoulder guidance advises review when not improving after two weeks. [S663, S664]

Use immediate local assessment for new weakness, poor balance or difficulty walking, new bladder/bowel loss of control, genital/saddle numbness, or pins-and-needles affecting both arms or both legs with neck symptoms. The bilateral qualifier concerns neurological symptoms; ordinary soreness in both shoulders alone is not automatically this emergency pattern. Major trauma or possible chest-pain/stroke symptoms follow their emergency pathways. [S665; see clinical/EMERGENCY.md]

Seek urgent advice for sudden severe shoulder pain, inability to move the arm, deformity or marked swelling, persistent altered sensation, an unusually hot/cold limb, injury-related pain, severe pain in both shoulders or fever/unwellness. Local services determine urgency and destination. NHS 111 is an England service, not a worldwide contact. [S664]

For an older adult, new pronounced shoulder/hip/neck stiffness after rest with systemic symptoms also raises inflammatory alternatives such as polymyalgia rheumatica. New headache, scalp tenderness, jaw pain when chewing or visual symptoms can accompany giant cell arteritis; visual disturbance warrants immediate clinical attention even if transient. These clues do not diagnose either disorder or justify a numerical probability from this card. [S666]

**Reading:** NHS neck and shoulder pages (2023 review dates, now overdue); University Hospitals Sussex 2025 leaflet's context/symptoms/urgent-help sections; NIAMS PMR/GCA overview and symptoms, February 2022, checked 2026-10-17. The leaflet addresses patients already seen in an emergency department; its reassuring recovery statements and exercise routine are not generalized to an unevaluated presentation.

**Next:** a current condition-specific rehabilitation review and M2 exercise/function evidence. No mandatory imaging rule, manipulation or universal recovery deadline is inferred.


## R1 — Massage: define the indication, comparator and follow-up

**Scope:** manual soft-tissue massage for adult neck/shoulder pain. Evidence cannot automatically transfer to forceful manipulation, a percussion device, a new injury or postoperative care.

The inspected NCCIH overview summarizes older reviews suggesting short-term neck/shoulder symptom benefit compared with inactive care, without superiority over other active treatments in one combined review. Its neck findings also refer to treatment frequency and duration, but this overview does not establish a personal regimen, optimal technique or durable benefit. The source was last updated May 2019; reading it today does not make its underlying evidence current. [S508]

Separate relief or relaxation from functional recovery, recurrence, long-term benefit and structural repair. An apparent advantage against no treatment is not proof of advantage against exercise, attention or another active treatment. Those are different comparisons. Do not promise disc repair, permanent posture correction or prevention of deterioration from the inspected evidence.

NCCIH describes few reported harmful effects but also rare serious reports, including nerve injury, fracture and blood clots, sometimes with vigorous techniques or vulnerable people. This overview does not provide a reliable incidence estimate or show that every massage style is equally safe. Do not delay assessment of injury, neurological change, fever or unexplained severe symptoms for massage; discuss suitability when health conditions or treatment may increase injury risk. [S508]

**Original fictional example:** someone with recent neck discomfort asks whether a more forceful massage gives better long-term results. Clarify the problem and desired outcome, check for reasons to seek assessment, and explain that these notes do not establish a force-versus-benefit rule. Consent and comfort do not establish clinical suitability.

**Reading:** NCCIH Massage Therapy: What You Need To Know, neck/shoulder evidence, harms and practical-advice sections, May 2019; rechecked 2026-10-17. Federal public-domain publication, original concise synthesis only.

**Open evidence gap:** a newer candidate was identified but its provider explicitly restricts AI use; it remains a bibliographic/permission pointer, not active evidence or a source to obtain through a mirror. AHRQ's 2020 independent review could not be opened beyond an access gate; search extracts are not section reading. R2 device safety and a usable newer independent appraisal remain open. These limits do not prove benefit or ineffectiveness.


## V1 — A convergence outcome is not a general eyesight outcome

**Scope:** diagnosed convergence insufficiency and selected child trials, not a home exercise programme.

Convergence insufficiency concerns coordination for near work. Near-task symptoms can prompt examination, but blur, headache or double vision do not establish that diagnosis. NEI describes convergence exercises as a treatment option after assessment; this does not show that generic exercises correct refractive errors. [S650]

A 2008 randomized trial in 221 children aged 9–17 compared defined office-based therapy, home approaches and office placebo over 12 weeks. The office group improved more on that trial's symptom and convergence measures. A 2019 CITT-ART report in children aged 9–14 found greater improvement in measured convergence with therapy after 16 weeks, while symptoms improved similarly in the therapy and placebo groups. Different outcomes can lead to different conclusions. [S669, S670]

The companion CITT-ART reading report found no added improvement on standardized reading tests versus office placebo at 16 weeks. The two 2019 reports concern the same trial, not two independent replications. A non-significant reading difference is not proof of exact equivalence for every reading outcome, age or treatment. The symptom-report abstract also has a trial-registration identifier inconsistent with its companion report; that metadata discrepancy remains unresolved. [S670, S671]

Do not promise improved myopia, school attainment, attention or everyday function from a convergence measurement. Conversely, limited transfer does not show every defined eye treatment is ineffective. Age, diagnosis, comparator, programme and follow-up constrain application.

**Original fictional example:** a child with near-work double vision should have an eye assessment; a claim that the same exercise will cure nearsightedness needs separate evidence. Sudden vision loss, severe eye pain or significant injury follows urgent eye-care guidance, not a training plan.

**Reading:** NEI selected overview; PubMed abstracts for 18852411,31651593,31651592, checked 2026-10-17. Full papers, instruments, allocation/blinding and detailed harms were not independently appraised. No questionnaire items or source figures are reproduced.

**Next:** verify the registration discrepancy, full trial methods and age-specific assessment; V2 strain, dry eye and refraction remain separate questions.


## H1 — Scalp flakes need age and pattern context

**Scope:** distinguishing a typical educational description from a diagnosis; no photographic clearance or product regimen.

Flakes and itch are symptoms. MedlinePlus describes dandruff, often beginning after puberty, as commonly related to seborrheic dermatitis. Scalp psoriasis and fungal infection are alternatives; itchy red/scaly patches with focal hair loss, or thick sore scale, deserve assessment rather than repeated assumption of simple dandruff. A clinician may need examination and condition-specific tests. [S651]

For mild typical dandruff, the federal summary says a dandruff shampoo can help and advises care if it does not. Choose an age-appropriate product using its local label or pharmacist's advice. The inspected summary does not establish a best ingredient, frequency, contact time, treatment duration or universal safety for children. H2 must address such product-specific questions separately. [S651]

Infant cradle cap is a different age context. NHS describes greasy scales that usually resolve and are normally not itchy or painful. Its gentle care advice includes an appropriate emollient, soft brushing and baby shampoo; it advises against picking, adult shampoos, fragranced products, olive oil and peanut oil. Ask about suitable infant products rather than transferring an adult dandruff regimen. If scales do not improve, are widespread, or the skin swells, bleeds or leaks fluid, seek clinical advice. [S668]

**Original fictional examples:** mild diffuse flakes without other features can start with a pharmacist discussion; a child with a focal scaly patch and broken hairs needs evaluation for alternatives. A baby whose scalp is painful or leaking fluid should not be reassured solely from the phrase “cradle cap.” General child illness and rapidly worsening symptoms require their own assessment.

**Reading:** federal MedlinePlus topic summary only, not linked A.D.A.M./AAD/other third-party pages; NHS Cradle cap, symptoms, care and seek-help sections, reviewed 2025-10-03. Checked 2026-10-17. NHS/OGL attribution applies to text, not its credited stock images.
