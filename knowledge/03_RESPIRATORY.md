# Colds, sore throat, nasal symptoms and acute cough

Adult scope. Sources: S07-S11, S14-S15, S18, S21, S40.

## Fundamentals

Common colds are usually viral. Inflammation, congestion, cough and disrupted sleep affect wellbeing. Influenza, COVID-19 and allergic rhinitis can look similar; the word "cold" does not distinguish them. Mucus or sputum colour alone does not establish bacterial infection. [S08-S09]

## Practical assessment

Establish onset, trajectory, measured temperature, breathing and risk factors. Rest, sleep, adequate fluids and saline nasal spray may support comfort. Discuss an appropriate pain/fever medicine only after checking restrictions. Consider whether influenza/COVID testing would change early treatment, especially for higher-risk adults.

Persistent high fever, dehydration, breathing difficulty, worsening after initial improvement or failure to improve change the next step. The CDC source advises assessment for fever lasting over four days or symptoms lasting over ten days without improvement. These thresholds never justify waiting through warning signs. Verify current wording for each new question. [S08]

## Cough

Antibiotics are usually unnecessary for uncomplicated acute cough. Cough may last three to four weeks. NICE does not recommend routine mucolytics, bronchodilators or corticosteroids for simple acute cough without a relevant underlying airway condition. Suspected pneumonia or increased complication risk requires a different assessment. [S07]

## Symptom medicines

Paracetamol and ibuprofen relieve pain/fever without treating the virus. A short course of a nasal decongestant may help congestion according to the exact label; prolonged use can perpetuate symptoms. Antihistamines and nasal corticosteroids have roles in allergic rhinitis, rather than forming a mandatory cold regimen. [S10-S11, S15, S21]

Benzydamine in Tantum Verde Forte is a topical pain/inflammation medicine. Its use does not establish eradication of infection or prevention of bronchitis. Verify formulation and label in module 04. [S14]

## Claims requiring care

The CDC cold source does not identify a specific antiviral treatment for the ordinary cold. Influenza and COVID-19 have separate options and treatment windows. A Russian 2025 guideline update describes a different medication practice: read the complete recommendation and the evidence for a specific medicine rather than transferring a list into Serbia. The full Russian guideline was not read for this edition. [S08, S18]

The Cochrane zinc review suggests possible shorter cold duration with uncertain evidence, heterogeneous regimens and potential adverse effects. Zinc is not a universal required supplement. [S40]
